# Evil
*Score ≤ -1501 — Recognized as a threat — A different world*

## Overview

Evil alignment is not a punishment state — it is a different world. The cities that were closed are now open. The merchants who refused to deal now deal freely. The quest-givers who turned away now have work. Good society is mostly inaccessible, but Evil-aligned infrastructure has replaced it: factions, vendors, cities, and communities built for people who operate outside Good's boundaries. The character is not locked out of the world. They are locked out of one version of it, and have gained access to another.

This does not make Evil comfortable. It is a world of fewer protections, more genuine danger, and less reliable social trust — Evil factions are not necessarily loyal to each other, and an Evil character navigating Evil-aligned cities must negotiate from a position of power or cunning rather than reputation. But it is functional. The world at Evil tier is fully playable. It is simply a different game.

Two classes start here. Both chose it. The alignment score of -1600 for Shadow Knight and Necromancer is not accidental — it places them deep enough into Evil that the opening tier experience is already one of a character who has made their commitments clearly.

## Score Range

**-1501 to -2000.** The floor is -2000 but functionally the tier is entered at -1501 and deepens without a hard cap on experience.

## Classes That Start Here

| Class | Starting Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow Knight | -1600 | Deep in Evil; redemption requires extraordinary effort |
| Necromancer | -1600 | Same — the practice defines the alignment |

## Races That Tend Here

The Evil-aligned races — Dark Elf, Ogre, Troll, Kel`varath, Kobold — have social structures built around this tier. Their home cities are Evil-faction cities; their cultural norms expect Evil alignment; their class choices (Shadow Knight, Necromancer, Blood Mage) naturally maintain or deepen it. For these races, Evil tier is the default, unremarkable state of being.

Characters from Neutral races who reach Evil have departed from their cultural baseline — this is noted by their home communities and has social consequences.

## NPC & World Reactions

**Good-aligned settlements:** Hostile. Guards attack on sight or after a short recognition delay. Merchants refuse service. Temple doors are closed. Entering Ardenmoor or similar cities as an Evil character requires disguise, smuggling, or overwhelming force — all of which produce consequences.

**Neutral settlements:** Cautious tolerance at best. Most major Neutral trading cities maintain policies of calculated non-aggression — they will deal with Evil characters for practical reasons while making their discomfort clear. Some neutral settlements have alignment floors and will not admit Evil characters at all.

**Evil-aligned settlements:** Full access. The Dark Elf undercity of Vel'Sharath, the Ogre tribal markets, Kobold warrens with surface access, and independent Evil-faction settlements are all open. Vendors carry appropriate stock. Quest-givers have work. Faction reputation matters in the normal way — but the floor is aligned, not hostile.

**Undead enemies:** Significantly reduced aggression. Many undead mobs treat Evil characters as non-threats or minimal threats; controlled undead (Necromancer-mastered or Shadow Knight-bound) are explicitly aligned toward them. Some undead will not attack Evil characters at all unless provoked.

## Mechanical Effects

- Shadow Knight dark spells function at **full effectiveness**
- Necromancer death spells function at **full effectiveness**
- Blood Mage drain spells function at **full effectiveness**
- Paladin holy spells: **0.4× effectiveness** (nearly useless — a Paladin at Evil tier has functionally been transformed anyway)
- Evil-faction quest chains fully accessible
- Good-faction cities require workarounds to enter
- Certain items and vendors only available to Evil-aligned characters

Evil tier is the required gate for the darkest transformations:

## Narrative Implications

Evil characters are not welcome in the world's dominant narrative. The cities, the quests, the NPCs that the game opens with are built for Good and Neutral characters — that is the majority culture of the game world. Evil characters have carved out space within it, and maintain access to a parallel infrastructure, but they are always working against the grain of the world's public moral consensus.

This is not a punishment. It is a different experience. Evil characters have access to stories, quests, and relationships that Good characters cannot reach. The Necromancer's questline deepens into necromantic lore that the Cleric never sees. The Shadow Knight's relationship with their dark patron is a character arc entirely unavailable to the Paladin. The Blood Mage's progression through transformation is its own story.

The question Evil characters face is not "how do I survive?" — the infrastructure exists. The question is "what am I building, and for whom?" A Necromancer who raises armies is building toward something. What is it? That is the story.

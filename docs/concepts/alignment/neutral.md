# Neutral
*Score -299 to 299 — Assessed individually — The in-between*

## Overview

Neutral alignment is not a moral failure and it is not a moral achievement. It is the absence of a strong pattern in either direction — a character whose choices have not accumulated enough weight to commit them to Good or Evil. The world has no standing assumption about them, which means every interaction is determined by context rather than reputation.

This sounds like freedom, and in some ways it is. A Neutral character can enter both Good-aligned and Evil-aligned cities without immediate hostility (though neither will give them the full welcome reserved for aligned members). They can deal with merchants on both sides of the moral line. They are not anyone's sworn enemy by default. They are, however, nobody's trusted ally either.

Most classes start at exactly Neutral (score 0). Reaching the extremes of the Neutral range — deep toward 299 or deep toward -299 — is close enough to tipping that careful NPCs may start treating the character as the adjacent tier in ambiguous situations.

## Score Range

**-299 to 299.** The center of the scale. Symmetrical around zero.

## Races That Tend Here

The Neutral-aligned races — Human, Wood Elf, Gnome, Half-Elf, Minotaur, Half-Ogre, Felhari, Fae — are culturally organized around pragmatism rather than moral commitment. Their communities do not expect strong alignment and do not sanction drift in either direction unless it reaches an extreme. A Human drifting to Bad is a personal matter; a Human reaching Evil is a city matter.

## NPC & World Reactions

**Good-aligned settlements:** Normal access. No discounts, no hostility. Treated as a traveler of unknown intent — assessed by behavior in the moment.

**Neutral settlements:** The most comfortable territory for Neutral characters. No positive frictions, no negative ones. Business as usual.

**Evil-aligned settlements:** Tolerated. Not welcomed, but not expelled. Evil vendors will deal with Neutral characters who don't announce Good-faction affiliations. Some Evil quest-givers require at minimum Neutral (or lower) alignment to engage.

**Undead enemies:** No passive effects in either direction.

## Mechanical Effects

- Paladin holy spells suffer **0.7× effectiveness** in the lower Neutral range (approaching the Neutral/Bad boundary)
- Shadow Knight dark spells suffer **0.7× effectiveness** in the upper Neutral range (approaching the Neutral/Good boundary)
- No faction-based access restrictions at city or settlement level
- Enchanter and Bard charm effects function normally (no alignment modifier)

## Transformations Available Here

Neutral is the required starting condition for several earned transformations:

| Transformation | Additional Requirements |
|---|---|
| **Lycanthrope** | Ranger, Druid, Shaman, or Monk; level 15 |
| **Spirit Merged** | Shaman; level 28 |
| **Beast King** | Beast Master; level 25 |
| **Elemental Fusion** | Magician or Sorcerer; level 26 |
| **Felhari Spirit-Walker** | Felhari Shaman or Monk; level 26 |

Neutral also serves as the minimum floor for several transformations that list "Neutral to Evil" in their alignment requirement — characters must not be Good-aligned for these to trigger.

## Narrative Implications

Neutral characters are the most common and, in some ways, the most honest. They have not decided. Or they have decided to remain uncommitted, which is its own kind of position. The world does not read a moral story into them — which means the moral story, if any, is entirely their own business.

The pull from Neutral is constant. Standard adventuring naturally generates alignment change: killing Evil enemies pushes Good; certain quests, certain classes, certain faction choices push Evil. Characters who stay Neutral do so by accumulating both in rough balance, or by actively avoiding strong alignment-generating actions. This is harder than it sounds, which means most characters who remain Neutral through extended play are making a conscious choice.

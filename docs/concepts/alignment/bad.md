# Bad
*Score -1500 to -300 — Distrusted — Doors closing*

## Overview

Bad alignment is the tier where the world starts shutting down. Not all at once — the shift is gradual, one closed door at a time, one guard who watches too closely, one merchant who claims to be out of stock. But the pattern is clear to anyone paying attention. The character has made enough harmful choices that the world has noticed, and the world has an opinion about it.

Bad is an uncomfortable tier to occupy. It lacks the cold efficiency of Evil — fully Evil characters know exactly where they stand and have access to the world built for them. Bad characters are in between: too dark for comfortable access to Good society, not yet committed enough to have full access to Evil infrastructure. They are distrusted everywhere and welcomed nowhere without reservations.

Most characters pass through Bad on the way to Evil rather than stopping here. Those who do stop here — perhaps deliberately maintaining a moderate darkness — find it the most socially constrained of all the tiers.

## Score Range

**-1500 to -300.** The lower end borders Evil; the upper end borders Neutral.

## Classes That Start Here

| Class | Starting Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blood Mage | -500 | Squarely in Bad tier at creation |

## Races That Tend Here

No race is culturally centered at Bad tier — even Evil-aligned races (Dark Elf, Ogre, Troll, Kel`varath, Kobold) tend to start at Evil or drift there quickly through their natural class and quest choices. Bad tier is typically a transitional state, not a resting one. Characters from Neutral races who begin pursuing Evil-aligned classes or quests will pass through Bad on the way down.

## NPC & World Reactions

**Good-aligned settlements:** Watched. Guards do not attack but maintain visible attention. Some vendors decline service or quote inflated prices. Temple NPCs will not engage. Certain quest-givers refuse. The city functions but with friction — the character is permitted rather than welcomed.

**Neutral settlements:** Conditional. Most trade is available. NPCs who deal with anyone will deal with a Bad character. Vendors who serve Good-aligned factions exclusively may decline. Some social interactions produce negative reactions.

**Evil-aligned settlements:** Improving access. Bad-aligned characters can engage with Evil faction merchants and quest-givers more freely than Neutral characters. Not full access — Evil factions often want commitment, not ambiguity — but notably more open than Good society.

**Undead enemies:** No passive effects. Some undead mobs begin acting less aggressively toward Bad characters — not friendly, but slightly less immediately hostile.

## Mechanical Effects

- Paladin holy spells suffer **0.4× effectiveness** at Bad tier — functionally crippled
- Shadow Knight dark spells function normally in Bad tier (closer to their intended extreme)
- Certain Evil-faction quest chains become accessible
- Good-faction reputation vendors restrict stock
- Blood Mage HP drain spells: no penalty at this tier; in their alignment range

## Transformations Available Here

Bad tier does not trigger any transformations directly but serves as a gate or transition point:

| Transformation | Notes |
|---|---|
| **The Hunger** | Accessible at Bad or Evil; typically first presents at Bad tier in the transition downward |
| **Dark Trapper** | Rogue reaching Bad tier triggers this drift transformation |

Several other Evil-required transformations become increasingly proximate as the character approaches the Evil boundary.

## Narrative Implications

Bad alignment is the tier of the meaningful warning. The world has started giving a character this tier feedback in many small ways: the guard who stiffens at their approach, the merchant who doesn't meet their eyes, the temple door that was open yesterday and is staffed today by someone who says the priest is unavailable. None of it is definitive. All of it is accumulating.

Characters who notice this feedback and decide to reverse course begin the long climb back to Neutral. Characters who notice it and don't care have answered a question about themselves. Characters who don't notice it are either very focused or setting up for an interesting moment of reckoning.

The Blood Mage is the only class that starts here, and their presence in this tier is not incidental — the practice of Blood magic carries a moral cost from the first cast. A Blood Mage who never drifts further is still building a life in the zone of diminishing social trust.

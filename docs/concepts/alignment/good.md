# Good
*Score 300 to 1499 — Trusted — The default welcome*

## Overview

Good is the alignment tier most civilized settlements are built for. Guards stand down. Vendors deal fairly. Quest-givers engage. It is not a dramatic state — nobody gasps when a Good-aligned adventurer walks into a city. It is simply the expected moral baseline for someone who is going to be allowed in the city without incident.

Good characters have demonstrated, over the course of their choices, that they do more good than harm. This is a pattern, not a standard — a Good character can kill, lie, and act selfishly without immediately losing the tier. The score accumulates slowly and erodes slowly. It is the alignment of people who are, on balance, on the right side of things.

Most player characters begin at Good or drift into it quickly through standard adventuring play. Killing Evil enemies, completing quests in Good-aligned cities, and general non-predatory behavior will maintain this tier without deliberate effort.

## Score Range

**300 to 1499.** The lower end (300–600) is easily reached and easily lost. The upper end (1200–1499) borders Exalted and takes sustained effort to maintain.

## Classes That Start Here

| Class | Starting Score |
|---|---|
| Paladin | +500 |
| Cleric | +400 |
| Monk | +100 |
| Witch Hunter | +100 |

## Races That Tend Here

The three Good-aligned races — Elf, Halfling, Dwarf — are culturally oriented toward this tier and their communities expect their members to maintain it. A Dwarf who drifts to Neutral is considered to be in a concerning period; a Dwarf who reaches Bad is a clan matter. These pressures don't prevent the drift — they just mean it has social consequences.

## NPC & World Reactions

**Good-aligned settlements:** Normal service. Guards are professional and respectful. Temple vendors have full stock available. Quest-givers engage without preconditions.

**Neutral settlements:** No notable reaction. A Good character is treated as a normal traveler — assessed individually rather than by tier.

**Evil-aligned settlements:** Caution or mild hostility. An obviously Good-aligned character in an Evil city will be watched. Some vendors will decline. Dark Elf enclave merchants, Kobold black market contacts, and similar Evil-faction NPCs will not deal openly.

**Undead enemies:** No passive effects. Good characters do not automatically harm undead — that requires Exalted.

## Mechanical Effects

- Paladin holy spells function at **full effectiveness** within this tier
- No access penalties in standard civilized settlements
- Some Exalted-exclusive quests are not yet available
- Cleric resurrection and high-tier healing function normally

## Transformations Available Here

Good tier is a prerequisite for several positive transformations but typically not sufficient on its own — most require Exalted. Exceptions:

| Transformation | Additional Requirements |
|---|---|
| **Lycanthrope** | Neutral required — drifting from Good toward Neutral is the direction |
| **Enlightened Warrior** | Requires Exalted drift, not Good |

Good tier does not trigger any transformations directly. It is a stable resting state.

## Narrative Implications

Good is the tier most characters live in for most of their careers. It is not a destination — it is the background condition of a normal adventuring life. The stories that happen at Good tier are the stories of the game itself: quests, combat, exploration, group dynamics.

The interesting drama at Good tier is the pull in both directions. Exalted is achievable with effort. Evil is reachable through a series of bad choices. Good is the ground in between, and the characters who stay here by deliberate choice — who could reach either extreme and choose not to — are making a quiet argument about what a life well-lived looks like.

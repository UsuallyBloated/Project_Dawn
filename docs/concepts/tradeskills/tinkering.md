# Tinkering and Clockwork Engineering

*Exclusive — Gnome and Kobold only*

## Overview

Tinkering is the engineering and mechanical crafting tradeskill. It produces traps, lock components, clockwork devices, and mechanical gadgets. In its base form it's available to any Gnome or Kobold without restriction. For every other race, Tinkering is inaccessible.

At skill 150, Gnomes and Kobolds unlock **Clockwork Engineering** — a prestige path that opens a second, advanced recipe tier. Clockwork Engineering is not a second tradeskill; it is a synthesis of high-level Tinkering into something qualitatively different. The metaphor: Tinkering is understanding mechanisms. Clockwork Engineering is building things that think.

**This is the only tradeskill with a prestige path.**

---

## Aesthetics: Gnome vs. Kobold

Both races access the same Tinkering tradeskill and the same Clockwork Engineering prestige path. The products are functionally equivalent at matching skill levels. The difference is entirely aesthetic — and it is significant.

**Gnome Tinkering:**
- Precise. Mathematical. Every gear has a purpose; every measurement is exact.
- Materials: brass, polished copper, silver wire, crystal lenses, clockwork springs.
- Named with serial numbers and formal designations: *"Mark IV Oscillating Trap Mechanism (Field Variant)"*
- Appearance: clean, compact, satisfying. They click correctly.
- Gnome crafters keep technical journals. The journals are correct.

**Kobold Tinkering:**
- Salvaged. Improvised. Somehow works.
- Materials: scavenged iron, bone, rough wire, repurposed mechanism parts, things that shouldn't fit together.
- Named with Kobold grunts, scratches, and descriptions of what it did last time: *"The Crusher (it crushes)"*
- Appearance: threatening, asymmetrical, impossible. Should not function. Functions anyway.
- Kobold crafters don't keep journals. They keep the thing that went wrong last time as a warning.

At Grandmaster tier, Gnome and Kobold products are statistically identical. Which one a player prefers to carry is a matter of aesthetics and character identity.

## Clockwork Engineering (Prestige Path)

**Unlocks at:** Tinkering 150
**Cap:** 300 (shared with Tinkering score — these are the same skill)
**Restriction:** Gnome or Kobold only

At skill 150, the Tinker's Bench option to "Engage Clockwork Engineering Mode" appears. This is not a mode switch — it unlocks a new recipe category in the same skill. All products in this tier are mechanically more complex, require the Mechanical Construct Shell or similar high-tier Tinkering components as bases, and produce output that would not be possible through raw Tinkering alone.

**The conceptual difference:** Base Tinkering makes better traps, locks, and gadgets. Clockwork Engineering makes things that operate semi-autonomously. They respond to conditions. They follow rules. A Clockwork Engineering product doesn't just trigger when stepped on — it evaluates.

### Clockwork Engineering Products

#### Mechanical Companions (150–200)

| Item | Materials | Skill | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scout Automaton | Construct Shell + Lens Array + Copper Coil | 155 | Deployable companion; patrols a set path, marks what it sees on the user's local map; no combat ability; controllable range 50m |
| Guardian Automaton | Construct Shell + Steel Plate + Mithril Coil | 170 | Stationary or patrol; attacks approaching enemies (30 base damage); not a true pet — cannot follow the player; guards a location |
| Clockwork Carrier | Construct Shell + Cargo Frame + Copper Coil | 175 | Follows the player; carries 10-slot inventory; cannot fight; can be sent back to a fixed point with cargo |

**Note on Mechanical Companions vs. Pets:** Mechanical Companions are not pets in the PetManager sense. They are deployed objects with behavioral logic. They don't level. They don't die permanently (they break down and need repair). A Guardian Automaton guarding a camp is a Tinker's ability to leave a threat at a location. This is fundamentally different from the Beast Master's warder or the Necromancer's skeleton.

#### Clockwork Golem (Grandmaster — 200)

The apex Clockwork Engineering product. A Clockwork Golem is not a companion — it is a creation.

| Item | Materials | Skill | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clockwork Golem | 2× Construct Shell + Mithril Plate × 4 + Lens Array + Adamantite Core | 200 | Full combat companion; operates as a true pet; 150 base HP + 15/level; 20 base damage + 2/level; controlled via Clockwork Commands item |

**Gnome Golem:** Polished brass and steel. Moves with disturbing precision. Makes a sound like a pocket watch being wound. Occasionally stops to recalibrate.

**Kobold Golem:** Asymmetrical. Plates from three different things. One arm is longer than the other. Makes a grinding, irregular noise that suggests imminent failure. Never fails.

**Design note:** The Clockwork Golem is an endgame item with significant material investment. It should feel like an accomplishment to build, not a routine production combine. Suggested: it requires a rare component (Adamantite Core) with very low drop rate or high crafting cost, and the construction itself triggers a special combine animation.

## Faction and Economy Context

**Gnomish Cogsworth (faction):** Hosts the best Tinkering and Clockwork Engineering trainers. The schematic library here includes designs not sold elsewhere. Good standing with this faction is required to purchase Master-tier schematics.

**Kobold tunnel markets:** Unique salvaged components not found in standard vendor inventories. Some Kobold-aesthetic Clockwork Engineering products use Kobold tunnel materials as substitutes for standard components — same function, different look, sometimes cheaper.

**Witch Hunter demand:** The Witch Hunter's entire gadget toolkit (silver bolt crafting, alchemical wards, mechanical trap-based ambush setup) runs on Tinkering output. A Gnome Witch Hunter is a character concept with surprisingly strong mechanical support.

**Cross-tradeskill economy:**
- Tinkering uses metal (Mining → Smelting) and lenses (Glassblowing).
- Rope Making produces the wire and cord used in several mechanisms.
- Clockwork Engineering Golems use Adamantite (Mining end-game) and Mithril (Smelting).
- There is no tradeskill dependency between Gnomes and Kobolds. Both run independent supply chains. This is thematically appropriate.

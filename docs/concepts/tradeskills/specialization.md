# Tradeskill Specialization

Some tradeskills offer a fork at advanced skill levels — a point where the crafter chooses a direction that provides deeper access to one branch in exchange for a ceiling on the other. This is distinct from the Prestige Paths (Clockwork Engineering, Runeforging) which are race-gated unlocks. Specialization forks are available to any eligible crafter who reaches the threshold.

---

## The Fork Mechanic

**Trigger:** At skill 150, certain tradeskills present a Specialization Choice. This is a one-time permanent decision. A trainer NPC delivers the choice through dialogue — it is not a UI menu. Walking away without choosing means the fork has not been taken; the character continues as a generalist indefinitely.

**Generalist vs. Specialist tradeoff:**
- **Generalist:** No fork taken. Access to all recipes in the skill. Quality ceiling stops at Superior (skill 200 cap). Cannot produce Masterwork items in any sub-category.
- **Specialist:** Fork taken. One sub-category uncapped — can reach Masterwork in that branch. The other sub-category is capped at Fine (skill 100 equivalent output), regardless of actual skill score. New specialist-exclusive recipes unlock at 175 and 200.

**The cap is soft:** A specialized Blacksmith can still attempt Weaponsmithing recipes outside their chosen branch, but results are capped at Fine quality. They don't lose the recipe knowledge — they lose the ceiling.

## Specialization Forks by Tradeskill

### Blacksmithing (fork at skill 150)

| Specialization | Gains | Restricted |
|---|---|---|
| **Weaponsmithing** | Masterwork weapons; exclusive weapon recipes (serrated edges, weighted pommels, hollow-ground blades); blade-engraving cosmetics | Armor output capped at Fine |
| **Armorsmithing** | Masterwork armor; exclusive armor recipes (articulated joints, padded underlayers, vented plate); custom fit adjustments | Weapon output capped at Fine |

Dwarves choosing Armorsmithing unlock a third Runeforging branch after reaching Blacksmithing 200. Weaponsmithing Dwarves unlock the Runeforging path for weapons only. The Runeforging prestige path remains Dwarf-exclusive regardless.

---

### Alchemy (fork at skill 150)

This fork exists in addition to the alignment-gated White/Black split (which is mandatory at character creation for Alchemy access). Both branches can be White or Black.

| Specialization | Gains | Restricted |
|---|---|---|
| **Potioncraft** | Masterwork potions (2× duration or 1.5× effect); exclusive combination potions (healing + mana in one bottle); reduced reagent cost at 175+ | Elixirs and poisons capped at Fine |
| **Elixir Craft** | Masterwork elixirs (stat buffs, transformative effects); exclusive elixirs (alignment shift micro-dose, temporary attribute swap); reagent-efficient formulas | Potions capped at Fine |

---

### Tailoring (fork at skill 150)

| Specialization | Gains | Restricted |
|---|---|---|
| **Clothcraft** | Masterwork robes and cloth armor; exclusive arcane-thread weave patterns; dimensional stitch (adds a hidden pocket slot to robes) | Leather-thread work capped at Fine |
| **Leathercraft** | Masterwork leather armor; exclusive tanning-integrated recipes (combines Leatherworking into Tailoring output); creature-specific hide recipes (scale mail from Kel`varath hide, etc.) | Pure cloth work capped at Fine |

---

### Woodworking (fork at skill 150)

| Specialization | Gains | Restricted |
|---|---|---|
| **Bowyer** | Masterwork bows and crossbows; exclusive composite designs; Ranger-specific bow recipes with built-in quiver attachment | Staves and furniture capped at Fine |
| **Staff Craft** | Masterwork staves and wands; focus items that enhance specific spell schools; hidden compartment staves (smuggling use) | Bows capped at Fine |

---

## Changing Specialization

Specialization is permanent. The fork cannot be reversed. A crafter who regrets their choice must level a second character to try the other path — or find a Grandmaster crafter in the other branch for commissioned work.

This is intentional. Specialization creates economic interdependence: a Weaponsmith needs an Armorsmith for full kit commissioning, and vice versa. The crafting economy works better when specialists need each other.

## Trainer Dialogue (Example — Blacksmithing)

> *Bruga sets down her hammer and looks at you.*
> "You've been at this long enough to know what you're good at. Question is whether you know what you want to be good at. You can keep going the way you are — decent at everything, best at nothing. Or you can choose."
> *She gestures at the rack of weapons on one wall, the armor stands on the other.*
> "Weapons. Or armor. Not both. Not at this level. Think before you answer — I only have this conversation once with each person."

Players who ask to wait: Bruga nods. "Come back when you know." The dialogue re-triggers on next visit. There is no time limit.

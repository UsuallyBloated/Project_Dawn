# Poison Making

*Exclusive — Neutral or Evil alignment required. Eligible classes only.*

## Overview

Poison Making distills venoms, toxins, and chemical agents into weapon coatings, thrown vials, and environmental hazards. The outputs are applied directly to weapons (coating the blade or tip for a limited number of hits) or thrown at targets as contact poisons.

The skill is alignment-gated and class-gated. It is not simply evil to know how to make poison — the game treats it as a discipline that requires a certain disposition to pursue. A Good-aligned Ranger who stumbles into poison-adjacent materials can make antidotes (White Alchemy) but not Nightshade paste.

---

## Access Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Alignment | Neutral (≥ -299) or Evil |
| Eligible classes | Necromancer, Shadow Knight, Blood Mage, Rogue, Witch Hunter |
| Dark Elf bonus | +XP rate; bonus potency on all coated weapons |
| Good-aligned blocked | Always. Fallen Paladin (transformation) gains access; Redeemed Shadow Knight loses it. |

**Witch Hunter clarification:** Witch Hunters are Good-preferred in alignment but eligible for Poison Making. The rationale: Witch Hunters hunt corrupted spellwork and those who wield it. They use poison as a tool — specifically, silver-infused or alchemically-charged coatings effective against magical and transformed targets. This is not moral grey area for Witch Hunters. It is professional practice.

**Blood Mage note:** Blood Mage poisons can be crafted with HP cost riders — the coating deals damage but also costs the Blood Mage HP to apply (not mana). This is a Blood Mage exclusive recipe variant, not available to other classes.

---

## Weapon Coating Mechanic

Applying a coating to a weapon is an out-of-combat action. The coating lasts for a set number of hits or a time limit, whichever comes first. When the coating expires, the weapon functions normally. Coatings do not stack — applying a second coating immediately replaces the first.

**Coating capacity by weapon type:**
- Light blade (dagger): 15 hits or 20 minutes
- Standard blade (shortsword, longsword): 10 hits or 15 minutes
- Heavy blade / axe: 8 hits or 12 minutes
- Arrows (Fletching + Poison Making combine): 5 arrows coated per vial

**Ranger/Rogue arrow coating:** A special combine — Fletching skill 60+ + Poison Making skill 40+ — allows coating arrow tips instead of melee weapons. The DoT effect triggers on the first hit per arrow (arrows are single-use anyway). Ranger-coated arrows trigger Hunter's Eye proc chance independently of the poison coat.

## Dark Elf Racial Bonus

Dark Elf Poison Making produces coatings with 15% higher DoT values at equivalent skill levels. This is a flat multiplier applied to the DoT damage component (not base hit damage).

Additionally, Dark Elves can produce the Drowcraft Poison variant — cosmetically identical to standard coatings but with a Dark Elf-culture aesthetic (darker residue, faint bioluminescent shimmer on blade in low light). Same stats, different appearance. Functionally identical; culturally significant within Dark Elf character identity.

---

## Class Connections

**Rogue:** Poison Making is the Rogue's primary damage-over-time system. Rogues have no innate DoT spells; a Rogue without Poison Making deals burst damage only. A Rogue with Poison Making deals burst + sustained DoT. This is the expected play pattern for a dedicated Rogue.

**Shadow Knight:** Poison Making supplements lifetap. The Shadow Knight is primarily a melee attacker — adding a DoT coating to auto-attacks creates a third damage stream alongside physical and dark-magic damage.

**Necromancer:** Poisons supplement the Necromancer's existing DoT spell repertoire. A fully loaded Necromancer fight involves pet damage + DoT spells + poison coating on their weapon (if they're melee-adjacent) or thrown poison vials (purely ranged support).

**Witch Hunter:** Uses Poison Making for silver-infused and alchemically-charged coatings effective against magical and transformed targets. Their recipes overlap more with the chemical/alchemical end of the skill than the biological-venom end.

## Faction and Training

**Evil-aligned courts (Dark Elf guilds, Necromancer halls):** Primary trainers for skill 0–150. Dark Elf trainers teach the Drowcraft variants; Necromancer halls teach the death-magic augmented recipes.

**Ranger/Rogue guilds (neutral-aligned settlements):** Teach the utility-focused coatings (crippling, sleeping draft) without the dark-magic tier. A Neutral-aligned Rogue can learn up to skill ~100 from these trainers before needing darker sources.

**Above skill 150:** Only available at Dark Elf guild or equivalent Evil-faction trainer. The upper-tier poisons require acknowledgment that you are making things intended to kill, not merely inconvenience. Even neutral settlements won't teach these.

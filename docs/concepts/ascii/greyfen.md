# The Greyfen — Zone Overview (Levels 10–20)

*Flat. Wet. The sounds are present but wrong — muffled, as if the Greyfen sits inside something
larger that hasn't finished deciding to let sound out. Three field teams noted this. The stonework
surfaces from the peat in higher concentration as you move south. Directly south: Greyveil. Directly
below Greyveil: the Foundation Ring, which the City Council will tell you does not exist. The Greyfen
has been sitting on the edge of it for three thousand years and has its own opinion on that.*

## Zone Notes

**The silence:** Audio design note — zone ambient sounds are present but muffled: birds, frogs, wind. Not absent. Wrong. The effect is consistent from the zone's north edge to the south. It does not vary by location except at the Peat Hall entrance, where it is briefly absent on descent — the shaft is louder than the surface. Players returning to the surface after a dungeon run report the silence feels heavier by comparison.

**Navigation:** The zone is flat. Landmarks are the surfaced Predecessor stones. Players who have been through the zone twice have learned the stone positions and use them as waypoints. The stones don't move. The peat channels do, very slowly, season to season. Tomby's maps account for this because Tomby has been here long enough to notice.

**The Bog Hollows:** Not undead in the necromantic sense. Turn Undead fails. Banishment fails. What works: Architect notation interaction (Notation skill 50+ can be used as a calm effect — they stop and appear to receive instructions). What also works: standard combat. What doesn't work: Clerical dispel, undead-specific spell damage. Mira's notes document this. The Walking Name Order has its own explanation that they share at faction standing 400.

**Night:** The zone's character does not change dramatically at night — it is already that kind of dark. What changes: Pale Surveyors become more active and range further from the Stone Field. The south fen's peat pools glow faintly from below, visible at the surface. The Peat Hall entrance is warmer than the surrounding area at night (relative to how cold the surrounding area gets — it is still cold; it is less cold than the peat at 2am).

**The Treasure Seeker operation:** Commercially organized. They have been selling Predecessor artifacts at Greyveil's gray market for two seasons. They know what they're taking has value; they don't know why. Two of the artifacts Tomby has are from this operation's discard pile — items they couldn't sell because buyers couldn't identify them either. The questline to disrupt the operation is Mira Ashwick's primary active commission. The questline to find where the sold artifacts went is not posted on her notice board; she will mention it at standing 250, quietly, as if she's not sure players should know.

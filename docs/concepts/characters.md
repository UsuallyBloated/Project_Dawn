# Character Concepts — Oil Painting Aesthetic

Concept portraits written as oil painting descriptions. Intended as reference for character select art, loading screens, or in-game portrait frames.

---

## Ogre Warrior — "The Penitent Brute"

**Composition:** Three-quarter portrait, low angle looking slightly up — makes him vast, but the kneeling posture softens it. The viewer is at eye level with his downcast gaze, creating unexpected intimacy.

**Detail:** A crude wooden prayer bead is wrapped around his knuckles — too small for his fingers, clearly made for someone else. A healed brand scar on his neck, half-hidden by a fur collar. His armor is patchwork: some pieces clearly looted, one piece clearly *made* with care — a small pauldron engraved with a flower pattern, incongruous and unexplained.

**Background:** Collapsed stone archway behind him, overgrown with moss. Suggests a former place of worship, now ruins. A single candle stub burns at the base of a crumbled statue — the deity unidentifiable.

**Technique:** Heavy impasto on the skin texture — palette knife work to suggest roughness. Smooth, thin glazes for the candlelight. The prayer beads rendered with almost obsessive precision against the loosely painted background.

**Palette:** Burnt umber base, raw sienna highlights, yellow ochre on the candle glow, a wash of cool Prussian blue in the deepest shadows. One small note of warm vermillion — the candle flame, and nothing else.

## Dark Elf Mage — "The Obsidian Scholar"

**Composition:** Medium shot, slightly above eye level — she's seated but the angle makes her feel elevated anyway. The viewer is a student, not a peer. Books frame the edges of the canvas like a border, some falling off the desk into darkness.

**Detail:** The open tome shows diagrams that are almost-recognizable — star maps that don't match any known constellation. Her free hand holds a quill that's still dripping ink, forgotten. A skeletal hand rests in a glass jar on the shelf behind her — she hasn't noticed, or doesn't care. Small silver rings on every finger, each a different style, clearly collected over centuries.

**The light:** The violet fingertip glow is the primary light source, casting everything in cool lavender. A second, warmer light source (candle, off-canvas right) fights it — so half her face is violet, half amber. Neither wins.

**Background:** Dense library, but the books get less distinct toward the edges — the painter's attention followed hers, outward detail dissolving into shadow and suggestion.

**Technique:** Sfumato edges on the background. Sharp, almost illustrative focus on her face and hands. The spell-light painted wet-on-wet to let colors bleed at the fingertips.

**Palette:** Ivory black, mars violet, dioxazine purple, titanium white for the glow. A single warm note of yellow ochre from the candle at the far right edge.

## Gnome Rogue — "The Clockwork Cutpurse"

**Composition:** Full-figure, seated on a chimney ledge, legs dangling. He's turned to face the viewer mid-task — caught, but utterly unbothered. The city sprawls behind him at sunset, three stories down, painted loose and warm.

**Detail:** He's mid-count on a handful of coins — some gold, one clearly a guild token he probably shouldn't have. His coat has at least eleven visible pockets, several of which are *moving* (something small is alive in one). Goggles have one cracked lens, repaired with copper wire and wax. A tiny clockwork bird perches on his shoulder, head tilted, watching the coins.

**The grin:** Not a smirk — a full, genuine, delighted grin. He loves this. That's what makes him dangerous.

**Background:** The city at the magic hour — amber, brick, a hundred small windows beginning to glow yellow as dusk falls. Painted fast and impressionistic. A church spire in the far distance. Smoke from chimneys.

**Technique:** The background is alla prima, quick and gestural. The gnome himself is layered, detailed, almost miniaturist in the coat textures and the tiny clockwork bird. The contrast in technique makes him pop off the canvas.

**Palette:** Cadmium orange, yellow ochre, burnt sienna for the city. His coat: a deep teal that reads almost black in shadow. Copper red hair. The coins catch the last sunlight — pure cadmium yellow, one stroke each.

## Dwarf Warrior — "The Mountain's Grief"

**Composition:** Close three-quarter shot. She takes up most of the frame — not because she's large, but because the painter got close, like a friend sitting beside her. The campfire is below frame; only its light exists.

**Detail:** The axe across her knees is old — the handle worn smooth at the grip, replaced once with darker wood. A name is scratched into the blade, barely legible: *Halla.* Her braids are partially undone — dwarven braids are ceremonial, kept tight. She's let herself come undone, just slightly, just tonight. One boot is unlaced. A tin cup sits beside her, untouched, gone cold.

**Expression:** She's not crying. She's past crying. She's in that specific exhaustion that comes after.

**Background:** Darkness, with the suggestion of pine trees at the edge of firelight. Nothing else. The world has contracted to this fire, this woman, this grief.

**Technique:** The face is the entire painting. Every brushstroke earns its place. The armor in shadow is loose, almost abstract — dark against dark. The fire is painted with a dry brush, letting the canvas texture read as flickering.

**Palette:** Almost monochrome — warm blacks, burnt umber, yellow ochre firelight. One cold note of cerulean blue from moonlight on the axe blade. The name *Halla* caught in that cold light.

## Half-Elf — "The In-Between"

**Composition:** Full figure, standing, but the canvas is tall and narrow — almost a door shape. She fills it but doesn't overflow it. The crossroads behind her is symmetrical; she is not centered on either path.

**Detail:** She carries two things: a human-made traveling pack, practical and worn, and a single elven-crafted item — a small silver compass that doesn't point north (it points somewhere specific, somewhere personal). Her clothes are a mix: human-cut coat, elven embroidery at the cuffs, done by her own hand and slightly imperfect. The mismatched eyes are painted with quiet care — not magical, not dramatic. Just two different colors, looking at the same horizon.

**The dawn:** The specific grey-lavender of first light, before color fully returns to the world. Everything is cool and still.

**Background:** The crossroads is rendered with architectural precision — stone marker, two diverging paths — then dissolves into soft dawn mist at the edges. Both paths disappear into fog equally. Neither looks easier.

**Technique:** Thin, luminous glazes for the sky — built up in layers to get that particular dawn glow. Her figure painted with careful, quiet attention. No drama in the brushwork; the painting earns its emotion through restraint.

**Palette:** Lavender grey, cool blue-white, muted sage green from the roadside grass. Her coat: warm brown. The compass: a single point of silver-white. Her eyes — warm amber and pale jade, the only saturated color in the painting.

## Troll Rogue — "The Marsh Phantom"

**Composition:** Low, wide shot — crouched at the waterline of a dark swamp, perfectly still, watching something off-canvas. He occupies the bottom third of the frame; the dark water and mist fill the rest.

**Detail:** Regenerating flesh is visible at a poorly-bandaged arm wound — unsettling, almost beautiful, like watching time-lapse growth. Moss and algae cling to him not as filth but as camouflage, deliberately applied. His eyes are the only light in the painting — a faint bioluminescent yellow-green, reflected in the still black water below.

**Technique:** Near-monochrome with a single glowing accent. The water surface painted with horizontal strokes; the reflection of his eyes perfectly still — suggesting he hasn't moved in a very long time.

**Palette:** Paynes grey, sap green, deep viridian. The eye-glow is the only warm note — yellow-green, repeated once in the water reflection.

## Elf Mage — "The Last Archivist"

**Composition:** Full figure, standing in a burning library. He is centered and calm. The fire is not. The architecture curves toward him, directing the eye; he is the still point the painting orbits.

**Detail:** An ancient elf — genuinely ancient, millennia in his face — calmly selecting which scrolls to save. Not panicked. He has done this before. His robes are on fire at the hem and he hasn't noticed, or has decided it doesn't matter. He holds three scrolls to his chest like children. His expression is the specific calm of someone who has already done their grieving.

**Background:** The library collapses in suggestion behind him — shelves tilting, pages airborne, the far wall a wall of flame. All of it loose and violent. He is rendered in precise detail.

**Technique:** The contrast in paint handling does the emotional work — the fire is thick, gestural, almost abstract; he is layered and still. The hem of his robe, where both meet, is the most complex area of the canvas.

**Palette:** Deep crimson and amber from the fire, cold silver-white on his hair and robes. The contrast is violent. The calm in his face is the whole painting.

## Human Warrior — "The Ordinary Weight"

**Composition:** Bust portrait, straight on — the most direct, unflinching angle. No dramatic low shot, no painterly tilt. He is looking right at the viewer and the viewer has nowhere to hide. The symmetry is almost uncomfortable.

**Detail:** A man in his late thirties, neither handsome nor ugly. The kind of face you forget and then remember suddenly. His armor is mid-quality — not a lord's plate, not a peasant's leather. The dents have been hammered out imperfectly. A wedding ring on his sword hand, worn to a thin band. A small wooden carving hangs at his chest — a child made it, clearly. He has kept it through everything.

**Expression:** Not heroic. Not grim. Just present. He is here, he will do what needs doing, and he will go home if he can. If he can't, he will do what needs doing anyway.

**Background:** A grey sky before rain. A field. No landmarks. Anywhere. Everywhere.

**Technique:** Painted with the same restrained technique as the half-elf portrait but warmer — the emotion is in the accumulated detail, not the brushwork. The ring and the carving rendered with the most care. The sky flat and thin behind him, almost like a stage backdrop.

**Palette:** Raw umber, warm grey, muted steel blue on the armor. Flesh tones: yellow ochre, rose madder, thin. The ring is a dull gold — not bright, almost brown. Everything desaturated slightly except his eyes, which are an ordinary brown and the most alive thing in the painting.

## Halfling Rogue — "The Hearthside Knife"

**Composition:** Interior scene — a cozy tavern corner, low candlelight. She's tucked into a booth, small enough that the high-backed seat nearly swallows her. One knee up, boot on the bench. She's eating a meat pie with one hand. The other hand, under the table, holds a knife she's been cleaning. The viewer has caught her mid-meal and she doesn't mind at all.

**Detail:** She is genuinely warm and genuinely dangerous in equal measure and sees no contradiction in this. Curly dark hair, two ringlets framing her face, the rest pinned up with what is almost certainly a lockpick. Round cheeks, laugh lines already forming at the corners of her eyes despite her youth. Her vest has a dozen small pockets, all full. Under the table: excellent boots, well-made, expensively stolen. A full mug of something at her elbow, barely touched — she doesn't drink when she's working.

**The knife:** The focal point. The candlelight catches it. She's cleaning it with a cloth, thoroughly, without looking at it. She has done this ten thousand times.

**Background:** The tavern recedes into warm amber darkness behind her — shapes of other patrons, firelight from a hearth, hanging herbs, low beams. All loose and impressionistic. She is the only sharp thing.

**Technique:** The warmth of the scene is deliberate misdirection — the painter has given the viewer every reason to feel comfortable, then placed the knife exactly where the eye eventually lands. The candlelight does most of the work: one strong source, everything else in warm shadow.

**Palette:** Amber, warm browns, cream. Her vest: deep burgundy. The knife: a single cold note of blue-white steel, the only cool color in the painting.

## Wood-Elf Ranger — "The Patient Distance"

**Composition:** The figure is small in the frame — this is a landscape portrait, not a character portrait. Dense ancient forest fills two-thirds of the canvas. She is in the middle ground, still, bow half-raised, watching something the viewer cannot see. You have to find her.

**Detail:** She has been in this forest long enough that the forest has started to accept her. Vines have been trained along her quiver strap — not decorative, functional camouflage, grown deliberately over months. Her hair is the color of autumn leaves and blends with the canopy above. Barefoot — her boots are tied to her pack, not worn. Feet toughened. She makes no sound. Her face, when you find it, is serene with the specific peace of someone who has been alone long enough to stop noticing.

**A detail that earns its place:** A single arrow is already nocked. She's been waiting. You don't know for how long.

**Background:** Old growth forest — massive trunks, shafts of green-gold light slanting through a canopy so thick it filters the sky entirely. Ferns at the forest floor. The light falls in cathedral columns. The trees are painted with reverence.

**Technique:** This is the most technically ambitious of the series. The forest requires layered glazes to achieve depth — cool shadows, warm light shafts, the complex green of filtered sunlight. She is painted in the same earthy tones as the environment; finding her in the composition is part of the experience.

**Palette:** Sap green, viridian, yellow ochre for the light shafts. The forest floor: burnt umber, raw sienna, moss green. Her skin: warm olive. Her hair: cadmium orange deepened with burnt sienna. The arrow tip: one cold glint of silver-white, the sharpest edge in the painting.

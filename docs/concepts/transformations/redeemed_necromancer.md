# Redeemed Necromancer
*Alignment Drift — Exalted tier — The Dead Remembered*

## Overview
Reaching Exalted as a Necromancer is, by any reasonable measure, nearly impossible. The starting score is -1600. Exalted requires +1500. That is 3100 alignment points against the grain of a class whose every spell costs alignment to cast, whose faction access is built entirely around Evil communities, and whose cultural standing in every Good-aligned city ranges from unwelcome to actively hunted.

It has happened. Not often. The Necromancers who reached it did so through sustained, extraordinary good action over very long careers — often involving healing organizations, protecting communities, or applying necromantic knowledge in ways that saved more people than they disturbed. The transformation that results is unlike any other in the game.

The Redeemed Necromancer does not become a Cleric. They remain a Necromancer — their understanding of death, their relationship with the dead, their fundamental framework is unchanged. What changes is the moral orientation of the practice. They can still summon the dead. They no longer compel them.

## Requirements
- **Class:** Necromancer
- **Trigger:** Alignment score reaches Exalted tier (≥ 1500)
- **Reversibility:** Reverses if alignment drops below Good. The climb back would be extraordinary.

## Effects

**Lost:**
- Compelled undead summoning (Summon Skeleton becomes Bound Soul — the distinction is consent)
- Dark Decay and other torment-based DoTs
- Enervation and fear-based abilities

**Gained:**
- **Bound Soul** — Summon a willing ancestor spirit as an ally. Stronger than the former skeleton (WIS-scaled), but temporary (10 minutes) — the spirit chooses to stay only for a time.
- **Passage Rite** — Apply a death-delay to a dying ally: prevents death for 5 seconds while still taking damage; gives the healer time. Does not prevent the death, only postpones it.
- **Unshackle** — Target an undead mob controlled by another source; break that control, returning it to neutral or freeing it entirely.
- **Speak With Dead** — Ritual ability: brief communication with recently deceased NPCs. Intelligence/quest utility.
- **Peace** — Dispel undead that has been freed; send it to rest. Cannot be used on living targets.

**Visual:**
- Shadow effects replaced with a pale gold-white light
- The Bound Soul appears as a luminous spirit form, visibly at peace
- The character's aesthetic shifts: the necromantic visual language remains but the palette lightens

## Narrative Implications

Nobody believes it the first time they hear about it. The second time, people ask for proof. The third time, the temples have a meeting about what to do with the theological implications of a Necromancer who demonstrably operates within the divine's favor.

The answer they usually arrive at is complicated and unsatisfying, which may be why the faith communities that produce Paladins and Clerics don't typically advertise when this happens. The Redeemed Necromancer walked in from outside everything the institution represents, and their existence raises questions the institution would prefer to handle quietly.

The Redeemed Necromancer themselves is usually past caring about the institution's opinion.

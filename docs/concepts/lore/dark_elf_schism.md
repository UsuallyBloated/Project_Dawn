# The Schism
*Why there are two kinds of Elves.*

## What Happened

The Elves did not split into High Elf and Dark Elf through gradual cultural drift. They split through a single decision, made approximately four hundred years before the current game's timeline, about the correct relationship between arcane study and the living world.

The argument, reduced: one faction believed that magical power derives from the living world — from understanding natural flows, from harmonizing arcane work with existing ecological and spiritual systems. The other faction believed that magical power derives from comprehension alone — that the living world is a substrate to be understood and where necessary redirected, not a partner to be consulted. The first faction would become the High Elves. The second would become the Dark Elves.

These were not equivalent positions that split amicably. The faction that became the Dark Elves did something specific: they used their arcane capacity to accelerate their own development in ways the other faction considered a violation of the living-world relationship they were arguing about. This was not a metaphysical crime to the High Elves — it was a demonstration that the Dark Elves' position, taken to practice, produced results that actively harmed what the High Elves were trying to protect.

The expulsion was violent. Both factions use the word "expulsion." The Dark Elves use a different word for what preceded it.

---

## What Both Sides Remember

**High Elf account:** The Dark Elves conducted accelerated magical development in the shared heartwood, drawing power from the living foundations of the shared city in ways that could not be undone. When confronted, they refused to stop. The expulsion was a protective act. What the Dark Elves experienced as cruelty was the minimum response to a situation that would otherwise have destroyed what remained. The High Elves regret that it came to that. They do not regret the decision.

**Dark Elf account:** The High Elves were not protecting the living world. They were protecting their political dominance over arcane methodology. The accelerated development was working. The results were significant. The High Elves expelled them because a rival methodology was succeeding on its own terms, which threatened the institutional authority of the primary schools. What was framed as ecological protection was territorial control. The Dark Elves have not forgotten that the arguments used to expel them were the same arguments used by the Elven schools to suppress any rival approach for three centuries before the final break.

---

## What This Means Now

**In Aelindra:** The Arcane Schools still operate on High Elf methodology. The tension between the two arcane traditions is politically present in NPC dialogue. Dark Elves who come to Aelindra for training encounter institutional resistance that is not openly hostile but is structurally discouraging. The exiled Dark Elf Runecarving scholars who operate in the outer district are there because the inner district would not have them. Their presence is tolerated because their specific technique (Runecarving into metal during the forge process) is useful and the High Elf tradition does not have an equivalent. This does not make them comfortable.

**In Vel'Sharath:** The Dark Elf arcane tradition has had four centuries to develop without High Elf institutional interference. It looks very different now. The Shadow-school emphasis is partly philosophical (shadow magic as the absence of the living-world resonance the High Elves value) and partly historical (centuries of development in underground environments that favor particular magical affinities). The Dark Elves in Vel'Sharath do not spend much time thinking about the Schism. They think about what they are building now.

**In the Half-Elf experience:** Half-Elves (one High Elf parent, one Human parent) carry an interesting position. They do not carry the Schism's political weight in either direction — they are not High Elf enough to be insiders in Aelindra or Dark Elf enough to have an opinion about Vel'Sharath. This gives them unusual freedom of movement between the traditions and is one reason Half-Elves are disproportionately represented in the Arcane Schools as students rather than faculty: they can learn the methodology without bringing the institutional politics.

---

## The Unresolved Question

Neither side is wrong about what the other did. The High Elves are correct that the accelerated development drew power from the shared foundations in ways that caused lasting damage. The Dark Elves are correct that the Elven schools had been suppressing methodological competition for centuries before the final break.

The question that neither side currently entertains — and that a player with access to both Aelindra and Vel'Sharath, and sufficient standing in both, could eventually raise — is whether the Schism was a response to what happened or a response to who would win if what happened continued. The Ash Scribes in Ixareth-Kul have a document that may be relevant to this question. They do not share it easily.

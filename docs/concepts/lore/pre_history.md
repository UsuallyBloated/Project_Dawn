# Pre-History — The Predecessors
*What was here before what is here now.*

## The Thread

Several things in the world resist easy explanation if examined in isolation. The Sunken Ziggurat in the Southeastern Marshlands predates the Kel`varath. The Bone Citadel in the Eastern Badlands was built from bones too large to be local fauna, using patterns carved with clear architectural intent. The Minotaur were displaced from lands they don't fully remember by a force their oral tradition describes in terms usually reserved for weather and inevitability. The Kel`varath had an empire — Ixareth-Kul — that fell before the current age, and its ruins are described by the few scholars who have accessed them as containing structures far older than the empire itself.

These are not unrelated. They are the visible surface of a single buried thing.

---

## The Predecessors

The civilization that predates the Kel`varath empire has no name because no living people carry their lineage forward. They were not wiped out — they ended. The distinction matters: the Predecessors built their structures to last after they were gone. The ziggurats are not temples or tombs; they are archives. The carving patterns that no living scholar reads are records. The Bone Citadel is the most extreme expression of their architectural philosophy: a structure built after the Predecessors were already declining, using the remains of creatures they had outlasted, as a monument to having been.

What they were is not settled. The Kel`varath shamans who have entered the deeper levels of the Sunken Ziggurat describe the record-carvings in ways that suggest the Predecessors were not physically imposing — the scale of their architecture comes from intention, not from being large. Their civilization appears to have been concentrated in the lowland and coastal regions: the marshlands, the badlands, and the interior plains that are now Auroch's Rest.

---

## The Minotaur Displacement

The Minotaur oral tradition records the displacement as follows: the land they occupied was needed. Not wanted — needed, in the way a river needs its channel. The Predecessors, in their final generations, undertook a large-scale reorganization of the interior plains that involved relocating everything living on them. The Minotaur were relocated. The records of what the Predecessors built on the interior plains after the displacement are unclear; the plains are now grassland, which suggests whatever was built either failed or was designed to be temporary.

The Minotaur were relocated east, into terrain that was marginal and difficult and not organized around supporting a settled people. They adapted. The culture of Auroch's Rest — the clan names carved into horns, the Shaman tradition of carrying the ancestors forward because history tried to erase them — is a direct response to having been moved. The Minotaur do not call it a displacement in their tradition; they call it the Narrowing. The world was wider, and then it was not.

## The Kel`varath Empire

The Kel`varath rose in the marshlands after the Predecessors ended. They found the ziggurats already standing, used them as spiritual centers without fully understanding what they recorded, and built their empire — Ixareth-Kul — around and above the older foundations. The older foundations are still there. In several cases, what appears to be an Kel`varath-built structure from the outside has Predecessor-carved lower levels that the Kel`varath construction enclosed rather than replaced.

The Kel`varath empire fell to internal fracture rather than external conquest. The three factions still active in the ruins of Ixareth-Kul — the Bonewrights, the Soulcarvers, and the Ash Scribes — are not remnants of the Kel`varath empire proper. They are the inheritors of what the three major Kel`varath factions became after the collapse, each of which has a different relationship to the Predecessor structures underneath the city.

- **Bonewrights:** Practitioners of the physical transformation tradition. They use the Predecessor-structure lower levels as workshop space. Their work involves materials.
- **Soulcarvers:** Practitioners of memory extraction. The Predecessor record-carvings are their primary interest; they believe the archives can be read given sufficient technique. Their work involves information.
- **Ash Scribes:** Archivists of the collapse itself. They believe the Kel`varath empire fell because of something the Predecessors left in the deep levels. Their work involves containment.

All three factions are correct about something. None of them agree on what.

## The Bone Citadel

The Bone Citadel in the Eastern Badlands is the Predecessors' last construction. It postdates the ziggurats and the interior-plains work by an unknown amount of time — the carving style is late-period, denser and more compressed, as though the architects were working with a sense of limited remaining time. The bones used in its construction are from megafauna that are not present in the current fauna record; what those creatures were and where they went is one of the open questions.

The Ogre clans avoid the Bone Citadel because their grandparents tried to enter it and encountered the thing currently inhabiting it. What they encountered is described only as: whatever built it is still in there, and it was not done working when the construction stopped.

Whether the Architect of the Bone Citadel is a Predecessor individual, an artifact of the Predecessor construction process, or something the Predecessors installed as a guardian is not known. The Soulcarvers of Ixareth-Kul have theories about it. None of them have tested those theories in person.

## Connecting Threads

The Predecessor connection surfaces in several places across the game's content:

| Location | Predecessor Presence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sunken Ziggurat (Southeastern Marshlands) | Intact archive; partially flooded; actively maintained by the thing that moved in | Dungeon |
| Bone Citadel (Eastern Badlands) | Last construction; Architect present | Dungeon |
| Ixareth-Kul lower levels (Ruined Dominion) | Foundation structures; record-carvings; Soulcarver interest | Endgame zone |
| Auroch's Rest plains | Archaeological remnants of the interior reorganization | Surface content |
| Fae Mere (deep reflection) | The Mere's reflection shows something that predates the Fae occupation | Dungeon, deep level |

The Predecessor thread is the world's deepest quest chain. Characters who engage with the Ziggurat, the Bone Citadel, and the Ruined Dominion in sequence are assembling a coherent picture. What they do with that picture — who they share it with, whether the Ash Scribes' containment position or the Soulcarvers' access position is correct — is the highest-stakes decision in the late game.

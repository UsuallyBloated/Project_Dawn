# Nano Banana Pro Prompt Pack — Project Dawn

Ready-to-paste prompts for **Gemini 3 Pro Image** (a.k.a. "Nano Banana Pro" / `gemini-3-pro-image-preview`).

## How to use

1. Open [aistudio.google.com](https://aistudio.google.com) and start a new chat.
2. Set the model to **Gemini 3 Pro Image** (top-right dropdown). If you only see `Gemini 3 Pro`, look for the **Image** variant — the icon shows a small image badge.
3. Set the **aspect ratio** dropdown to the recommendation listed with each prompt (e.g. `3:4`, `16:9`).
4. Set **resolution** to `2K` for portraits, `1K` for location maps/scenes (save quota; upscale your favourites later).
5. Paste a prompt block in. Hit run. Regen 2–3 times if the first roll doesn't land — Nano Banana Pro is much better at re-rolls when you keep the prompt fixed.
6. Save the winner into this folder as `portrait_<slug>.png` or `scene_<slug>.png`.

## Style anchor (paste once at the top of any session)

> *Style anchor — apply to every image this session unless I say otherwise:*
> *Painterly oil-on-canvas aesthetic. Visible brushwork. Restrained, cinematic lighting. The feeling of a fantasy paperback cover from 1987 — Elmore, Parkinson, Caldwell. No text overlays, no captions, no watermarks, no banner art. Composition reads at thumbnail size. Avoid anime, avoid airbrushed CG sheen, avoid "trading card frame" borders.*

If you forget to paste this, the per-prompt blocks below still include the critical style directives — the anchor just keeps them from bloating each prompt.

> Oil painting, three-quarter portrait of a massive Ogre warrior kneeling in penitence, viewed from a low angle that makes him vast but the kneeling posture softens it. The prayer beads rendered with obsessive precision against the loosely painted background. Palette: burnt umber base, raw sienna highlights, yellow ochre on the candle glow, a wash of cool Prussian blue in the deepest shadows. One small note of warm vermillion — the candle flame, nothing else.

## Elf Magician — "The Last Archivist"
*Save as: `portrait_elf_magician.png` — 2:3*

> Oil painting, full figure of an ancient male Elf standing in a burning library — millennia in his face — calmly selecting which scrolls to save. He is centred and calm; the fire is not. ## Kel`varath Necromancer — "The Bone Cartographer"

> Oil painting, slightly overhead angle looking down at a long worktable covered in bones arranged with obsessive precision. ## Minotaur Shaman — "The Ancestor's Weight"
*Save as: `portrait_minotaur_shaman.png` — 2:3*

> Oil painting, low-angle full figure of a male Minotaur shaman standing at the threshold of a ruin, back to the viewer, facing out. We see him from behind. The landscape beyond is vast. ## Revenant Blood Mage — "The Scar That Remembers"
*Save as: `portrait_revenant_blood_mage.png` — 3:4*

> Oil painting, close three-quarter portrait of a male Revenant blood mage — exactly eye level, the painter got too close or the subject came too close, and neither retreated. The viewer cannot look away. Neither can he. The wounds are not healing — but they're not killing him either. Where living skin would show bruising, his shows a faint darkening, a memory of injury. He doesn't feel them. A blood mage uses pain as fuel; a revenant blood mage has had to find something else to burn. His hands are extended slightly toward the viewer — one holds a slow red mote of gathered blood-light, hovering, patient. His expression: curious, not threatening. He is observing what he can do now that pain is gone. Eyes: not dead, not alive — something in between that the painter has not tried to categorise. Background: an ordinary room — table, chair, a window with rain on it. No mysticism in the setting. Very tight, very controlled — paint handling almost photographic at the centre, his face and hands; loosens rapidly outward. Blood-light painted wet-on-wet, let to bloom. Palette: muted and cool — Payne's grey, blue-grey, the specific grey-blue of old skin; blood-light in alizarin crimson, the only saturated colour; rain on window in cool viridian.

## Fae Enchanter — "The Glass Court"
*Save as: `portrait_fae_enchanter.png` — 4:3*

> Oil painting of a tiny female Fae enchanter (six inches tall) seated on a throne made from a walnut shell, occupying only the lower left quarter of the frame. The rest of the canvas is the illusion she's maintaining: a grand baroque court in miniature, extending impossibly beyond the frame's edges, populated with figures who are not quite there. The eye spends a long time in the illusion before finding her. She is ancient in the way that pre-dates old — a small face that could pass for a child's if the eyes weren't what they are. Palette: iridescent — violet, aquamarine, gold leaf implied by yellow ochre; her figure cool silver-white, lavender grey; the illusion warm, sumptuous, slightly too vivid to be trusted.

## Dark Elf Shadow Knight — "The Titled Shadow"
*Save as: `portrait_dark_elf_shadow_knight.png` — 3:4*

> Oil painting, three-quarter portrait of a male Dark Elf Shadow Knight seated in a carved chair with authority in its form, but seated sideways — one leg draped over the armrest, posture studied in its casualness. It is performative. He knows it's performative. The viewer knowing it's performative is part of the performance. The armour is excellent — not ornate, not decorated, *excellent.* Black-lacquered, fitted with the specificity money and patience can buy. A single pale rune at the throat catches the only light in the composition. His face: aristocratic Dark Elf features (pale violet skin, white hair), and in them a very quiet, very certain cruelty — not rage, not passion, just a settled awareness that most things will bend eventually. ## Notes on getting the best results

- **Re-roll, don't reword.** Nano Banana Pro responds better to keeping the prompt fixed and regenerating than to rewording. Three rolls per prompt is reasonable; if none land, *then* tweak.
- **For portraits, the model favours one strong subject.** If the model gives you a group shot when you asked for a single figure, add `single figure only, no other people in frame` at the end.
- **No-text directive.** Nano Banana Pro 3 is much better at *rendering* text than the previous generation — which means it now happily slaps unwanted labels onto your images. Every prompt above ends with the implicit `no text overlays, no captions, no watermarks` from the style anchor, but if you skip the anchor, add it manually.
- **Race anchor reminder.** The model knows D&D-style fantasy races well, but if it keeps drifting (e.g. drawing a Kel`varath as a generic lizardman instead of a stooped scholarly bone-marker), specify `anthropomorphic reptilian humanoid, NOT a dragon` or similar disambiguation.
- **Iterate with Edit mode.** Once you have a portrait you like, use Nano Banana Pro's edit mode to swap small details — colour of a sash, add a scar, change the candle to a lantern — without losing the painting. This is its killer feature versus the older Banana 2.5.
- **For NPC templates (Part 4), fill all three slots before running.** A prompt with an unfilled `[RACE]` placeholder will produce a generic human. Fill `[RACE]` with a descriptive phrase ("a stocky middle-aged Dwarf female", "a wiry young Troll male"), `[DETAIL]` with one specific thing (a habit, a scar, a prop, a backstory beat), and `[NAME]` is just for your filename.

# Part 4 — NPC Role Templates

A modular system for generating NPC portraits at scale. Each template defines the composition, props, lighting, and emotional beat for a role archetype. Fill in three slots before running:

- **`[RACE]`** — descriptive phrase: `"a stocky middle-aged Dwarf female"`, `"a wiry young Troll male"`, `"an elderly Half-Elf"`
- **`[DETAIL]`** — one specific thing that makes this individual memorable: a habit, a scar, a prop, a backstory beat, a small contradiction
- **`[NAME]`** — for your filename only; not pasted into the prompt

Filename pattern: `portrait_npc_[name]_[role].png`

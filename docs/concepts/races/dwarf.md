# Dwarf
*Good — Mountain Clans — The Enduring Stone*

## Overview
Dwarves do not bend. This is not stubbornness as a character flaw — it is an accurate description of a people whose civilization is carved from granite, whose legal system has not been substantially revised in eight hundred years, and whose approach to problems is to apply direct force until the problem yields or the Dwarf does. The second outcome is less common than the first.

They are defined by continuity. The great Dwarven holds have been continuously inhabited longer than most surface civilizations have existed. The same clans mine the same seams their ancestors worked a dozen generations back. The same forge techniques are taught by masters whose masters' masters established them. This produces extraordinary craftsmanship — Dwarven steel, Dwarven stonework, and Dwarven architecture represent a ceiling that other races approach but do not reliably exceed. It also produces a race that takes a very long time to change its mind, and which is not always wrong to.

Their Good alignment is rooted in obligation rather than sentiment. Dwarves have duties: to clan, to ancestors, to craft, to the holds that shelter them. Failing these obligations is a form of death worse than the literal kind. Honoring them, regardless of personal cost, is simply what you do. This makes Dwarves exceptionally reliable allies and occasionally inflexible negotiating partners.

Dwarves are short, immensely dense, and built with a center of gravity that makes them very hard to knock down. Skin tones run from pale and rough to a deep ruddy brown. Hair is most commonly dark, auburn, or grey; red hair is a mark of the western mountain clans and carries minor ceremonial significance. Braids in Dwarven hair and beards are not decoration — they encode information. A Dwarven warrior's braid pattern tells an informed observer their hold of origin, their clan rank, whether they've completed their forge-rite, and at least one other detail that varies by tradition. Cutting a Dwarf's braids without consent is the kind of thing that starts feuds.

## Homeland & Society
The Dwarven holds are a network of carved mountain fortresses extending beneath the central and northern mountain range. Each hold is an independent city-state governed by a Thane, who holds authority granted by the clan council. The holds are confederated by the Stone Compact — an ancient treaty that obligates mutual military aid, regulates trade disputes, and forbids war between holds (a prohibition that has been tested repeatedly and held approximately eighty percent of the time).

Dwarven society is structured around clans and crafting guilds. The warrior clans hold military authority; the forge guilds hold economic authority; the temple keeps — devoted to the ancestors and the spirits of deep stone — hold spiritual authority. These three power centers check each other in a system that has been described as stable, inefficient, and deeply conservative, all of which are accurate. A law passed by one Dwarven hold takes roughly three human generations to reach the others by normal process; emergency measures take somewhat less.

Dwarves respect Elves' age and skill, trade heavily with Humans, find Halflings charming, and have ongoing complex relationships with Gnomes that combine mutual professional respect with competitive resentment. They dislike the underground Evil races categorically — the holds have been attacked by Dark Elf and Kobold raiding parties often enough that this has calcified into doctrine.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** High STR, STA, WIS; lower AGI; exceptional physical endurance
- **Stoneblood:** Bonus to poison and disease resistance; Dwarves endure physical hardship better than most
- **Stonecunning:** Bonus to detecting traps, assessing structural stability, and identifying quality of crafted goods
- **Starting region:** The mountain holds, northern range
- **Faction standing:** Positive with Good-aligned races; strong positive with Halfling; cold to Dark Elf, Kobold; very negative with Ogre, Troll

## Available Classes
Warrior, Paladin, Cleric, Druid, Rogue, Monk, Ranger, Witch Hunter, Bard, Magician, Wizard, Sorcerer.

**Blocked:** Shadow Knight (Good alignment), Shaman (no ancestor-spirit tradition in the Dwarven practice — they honor ancestors but through different rites), Enchanter (Dwarven temperament does not bend toward the sustained social intelligence the art requires), Necromancer and Blood Mage (cultural prohibition on the level of bedrock — the ancestors would not permit it).

## Naming Conventions
Dwarven names are hard, compact, and built from consonants that sound good when shouted across a forge floor. Single-syllable nicknames are common in daily use; the full name is reserved for formal occasions, legal documents, and moments where someone needs to understand they are being held to account. Clan names carry more social weight than personal names.

**Male examples:** Bram, Dolgrin, Durn, Forvik, Grak, Halveth, Jorik, Keldun, Throrn, Voldar
**Female examples:** Bryn, Dagna, Ebra, Fryn, Grika, Halda, Idra, Keldra, Thyra, Volka
**Clan names:** Ashmantle, Copperbeard, Deepvein, Forgeborn, Greymantle, Ironhold, Stonegrave, Thunderpeak

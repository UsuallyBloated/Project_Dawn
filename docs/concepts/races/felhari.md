# Felhari
*Neutral — Clan Hunters — The Second Claw*

## Overview
The Felhari are a race of large, feline-featured people whose culture is built on two pillars: the hunt and the bond. Every Felhari hunts. Every Felhari belongs. The clan is not an optional social structure — it is the context in which identity exists. A Felhari without a clan is not merely alone; they are without the framework that defines what they are and what they owe. Exile is the deepest punishment the culture can produce, and it is used sparingly and never lightly.

The hunting culture extends to everything. Felhari assess every situation in terms of terrain, position, advantage, and threat — not because they're paranoid, but because those are the natural categories their world comes in. A Felhari merchant is still doing threat-terrain-advantage calculation; it just looks like reading the room. A Felhari Cleric is still thinking about positioning and approach; the quarry is simply different. This cross-discipline application of predator logic is one of their genuine strengths and something they're often unaware they're doing.

The Felhari spirit-animal bond — formalized in the Beast Master tradition — exists at the intersection of hunting culture and ancestral practice. The warder is not a pet or a tool; it is a second perspective on the same hunt, and the bond is understood as a mutual contract rather than ownership. Felhari who form this bond are considered to be practicing the culture's deepest and most complete expression.

| Female | 5'8"–6'3" | 160–225 lbs | No mane, slightly more lithe | Marginally faster; hearing fractionally sharper — both sexes fight equally |

Felhari are large, fur-covered, and cat-faced — broad flat noses, forward-set ears that fold and swivel independently, short muzzle, and retractable claws on all four limbs. Fur patterns are as varied as any feline: solid, spotted, striped, and brindle are all present in roughly equal proportions. Males develop a collar mane in adulthood that varies in size and density; in some clans its length indicates social standing. Both sexes have tails — long, expressive, and nearly impossible to train into stillness, which makes Felhari one of the more readable races emotionally once you know how to look.

## Homeland & Society
The Felhari homeland is the Khala Savannahs — a vast, warm grassland territory in the southeastern interior, bounded by the marshlands to the east and the mountain foothills to the north. The terrain is open, warm, and good for the kind of running pursuits that Felhari hunting groups evolved around.

Clan structure is the fundamental unit. Each clan holds a territory, hunts it, and answers to a Clan Elder who leads by demonstrated competence and the consent of the clan. Clans trade, intermarry, and occasionally go to war with each other; the inter-clan council (the Gathering, held twice yearly) manages the relationships. The Gathering is also where disputes are formally settled, major decisions announced, and where a Felhari without a clan may petition for adoption.

The shaman and priest traditions coexist within Felhari culture because they address different needs: shamans work with the ancestor spirits and the hunt tradition; clerics serve within the temple structures that handle the broader community's spiritual life. Neither considers the other in competition. The Beast Master path sits within the shaman tradition and is the highest calling in many clans.

Relations with other races are pragmatic. Felhari trade with Humans, respect Elves' age without particularly revering them, find Halflings pleasant company, and have no strong opinions about most others. Dark Elves, Trolls, and Kobolds they watch carefully — old territorial conflicts with Kobolds in particular are recent enough to still be relevant.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** High STR, AGI, DEX; lower WIS; natural predator reflexes
- **Claws:** Natural attack option; bonus to unarmed strikes
- **Low-light vision:** Sharp predator sight functions in near-darkness
- **Keen senses:** Bonus to perception checks; particularly hearing and smell
- **Starting region:** The Khala Savannahs, clan territories
- **Faction standing:** Positive with most Neutral and Good races; cautious with Dark Elf; negative with Kobold

## Available Classes
Warrior, Paladin, Cleric, Druid, Shaman, Beast Master, Rogue, Monk, Ranger, Witch Hunter, Bard, Magician, Sorcerer.

**Blocked:** Shadow Knight (alignment), Wizard (no tradition of sustained written scholarship), Enchanter (requires a social intelligence the culture expresses differently — Felhari social intelligence is predator-read, not manipulation-based), Necromancer (the dead are ancestors; the tradition is consultation and respect, not command), Blood Mage (incompatible with the culture's values around blood, which in shaman tradition carries specific sacred meaning).

## Naming Conventions
Felhari names roll through the mouth — consonants that use the tongue and palate, vowels that extend. The *Vah* prefix appears in personal names for those of elevated clan standing (Elders, high shamans, notable warriors); it is earned, not given at birth. Clan names are descriptive, often referring to the territory's defining feature or the clan's founding story.

**Male examples:** Davan, Farek, Harav, Jareth, Kavesh, Mirak, Ravar, Saveth, Tharav, Vahrek
**Female examples:** Davara, Fareth, Haravi, Jareva, Kavara, Mireth, Ravara, Savara, Thareva, Vahrea
**Clan names:** the Far-Running, the High Grass, the Iron Paw, the Moonstalker, the Red Savannah, the Still Water, the Stormchaser, the Twin Fang

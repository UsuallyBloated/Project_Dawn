# Minotaur
*Neutral ”” Displaced People ”” The Weight of Names*

## Overview
Minotaurs were enslaved. Not metaphorically, not in the distant past of myth ”” within living memory, multiple Minotaur generations were held in bondage by a now-collapsed empire that found their physical capabilities useful and their inner lives unimportant. The abolition of that system was incomplete: the empire fell, the bonds were cut, but there was no homeland to return to, no intact culture to fall back on, and no one waiting to help them rebuild. What Minotaurs have now is what they assembled themselves from the pieces the world left them.

This history is present in everything. The names carved on their horns are the dead ”” ancestors lost in captivity whose names were not recorded anywhere else, carried by the living because someone has to. The shaman tradition that survived is a recovery, not a continuation: the original practices were suppressed, then reconstructed from fragments, oral memory, and whatever the ancestors could still communicate across the gap. The result is genuine but marked. Minotaur shamans know they are working with a partial archive. They do not pretend otherwise.

The Neutral alignment reflects where the race currently sits, not where it started. Minotaur ethics are still being worked out collectively ”” what they owe the world that failed them, what alliances are trustworthy, what it means to rebuild rather than avenge. Individual Minotaurs span a wide range, but the cultural center is rebuilding: deliberate, careful, and deeply skeptical of easy answers.

Minotaurs are massive bovine-humanoids with broad, horned skulls, thick necks, and bodies built for sustained physical labor. Fur is most commonly dark ”” black, deep brown, grey ”” with occasional brindle patterns. Eyes are large and dark, with excellent vision and an emotional expressiveness that surprises people who've only seen them from a distance. The horns are significant: carved with names over a lifetime, they represent the Minotaur's personal history made visible. A young Minotaur's horns are smooth. An elder's horns are layered in text so fine it requires close inspection to read, and the Minotaur can recite every name from memory.

## Homeland & Society
Minotaurs have no fixed homeland. The closest thing is the free city of Auroch's Rest, a Neutral city-state founded by Minotaurs and sympathetic allies after the collapse, built at the site of a former slave market in a deliberate symbolic act. It functions as a cultural and political center ”” a place Minotaurs can return to, where their traditions are practiced openly and their history is taught without euphemism.

Outside Auroch's Rest, Minotaurs live in scattered communities across Neutral territories, often anchored by a shaman who serves as community historian and spiritual leader. The shaman's role is carrying memory ”” every name, every history, every fragment of recovered tradition ”” and passing it on. The shamanic orders have formalized this into a curriculum, though "formal" for a Minotaur community means a master-apprentice bond that lasts years and is taken with absolute seriousness.

Relations with other races are cautious. Humans were involved in the enslavement ”” some of the worst offenders, some of the most active abolitionists. Minotaurs do not resolve this ambiguity easily. Dwarves and Halflings were largely uninvolved and are trusted more quickly. Elves, who had resources to intervene and debated it for decades, have a complicated standing that individual Minotaurs negotiate privately.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** Very high STR, STA; lower INT, DEX, CHA
- **Gore:** Natural horn attack option
- **Endurance:** Stamina pool is larger than most races; fatigue takes longer to set in
- **Racial memory (passive):** Bonus to WIS-based checks related to history, oral tradition, and ancestor-spirit interaction
- **Starting region:** Auroch's Rest and surrounding territories
- **Faction standing:** Cautious neutral with most races; positive with Neutral city-states; complicated with Humans; negative with remnants of the old empire

## Available Classes
Warrior, Shadow Knight, Cleric, Druid, Shaman, Beast Master, Ranger, Bard, Magician.

**Blocked:** Paladin (not yet ”” the institutional Good-aligned religious orders that ordain Paladins don't yet have the relationship with Minotaur communities to make ordination common), Rogue (size incompatible), Monk (mass and temperament), Witch Hunter (Minotaur community too fragile for the witch-hunting tradition), Wizard (no fixed schools ”” the diaspora has not yet produced institutions capable of the decades of sustained training Wizardry requires), Sorcerer (innate power exists but manifests as uncontrolled destructive episodes rather than usable magic), Enchanter (requires sustained social intelligence the culture hasn't prioritized), Necromancer (the dead are the ancestors; using them as tools is the specific thing the culture is rebuilding away from), Blood Mage (alignment).

## Naming Conventions
Minotaur names are old-world and resonant ”” many derive from the pre-captivity language, which the shamans have partially reconstructed. They favor round vowels and deep consonants. The first name is personal; the second name is the name of the ancestor the Minotaur has been entrusted to carry ”” the first one carved into their horns. Elders may have a third name earned through deed.

**Male examples:** Auroch, Bramosh, Drakon, Gaval, Horrath, Koreth, Mavrak, Soloth, Thavar, Ulrakh
**Female examples:** Aureth, Bravka, Dakara, Gavra, Horra, Koreva, Mavra, Solova, Thavara, Ulraka
**Ancestor names (carried as second name):** Names of the lost, always spoken with the same weight as one's own

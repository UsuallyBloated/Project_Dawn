# Human
*Neutral — Generalists — The Adaptable Majority*

## Overview
Humans are the most common race on the continent and, in many ways, the most unremarkable — which is precisely what makes them so dangerous. They carry no ancestral magic, no innate gifts of claw or fang, no centuries of martial tradition passed down through unbroken lineages. What they have is the capacity to become almost anything, and the ambition to try. A Human city contains every class, every religion, every faction — simultaneously and often in conflict with itself.

Human civilization is young by the standards of Elves or Dwarves, but it has spread faster than either. Where other races built deep, Humans built wide. They founded cities at crossroads, sent traders into every territory, signed treaties with factions that despised each other, and buried those treaties when something better came along. Other races find this exhausting. Humans find it efficient.

There is no unified Human culture, which is both the race's defining trait and its most common source of internal conflict. A Human farmer from the southern grain belt and a Human merchant from the northern harbor cities may as well be from different worlds. The only thing most Humans share is an understanding that the world moves fast, and the ones who stop moving get left behind.

Humans come in every skin tone, hair color, and eye color the continent offers. There is no "Human look." A crowd of Humans contains more physical diversity than a crowd of almost any other race. Veterans of cross-racial diplomacy note that this makes Humans oddly easy to underestimate — there is no face to read a species-wide disposition in.

## Homeland & Society
Humans occupy the central lowlands — a broad corridor of farmland, river valleys, and coastal plains that sits between the older territories of most other races. No single Human nation controls this region; instead, a half-dozen city-states maintain shifting alliances and periodic wars over trade routes, river access, and old grudges. The largest of these cities, Ardenmoor, serves as the de facto cultural and commercial capital — not by conquest, but by being the place everyone passes through.

Human society organizes around guild structures more than bloodlines. The Merchants' League, the Adventurers' Registry, the Artisan Guilds, and the Temple Councils all hold real political power, and membership in the right guild matters more than family name in most cities. This pragmatism extends to religion: Humans worship a wide pantheon, and it is common to find temples to three or four different deities within a single city block.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** Balanced across all attributes; no racial highs or lows — the generalist baseline
- **Adaptability:** Class advancement penalties are reduced; Humans learn new disciplines slightly faster than other races
- **Starting region:** Ardenmoor and surrounding human territories
- **Faction standing:** Neutral with most races at start; no innate hostility or affinity

- **Generalist Bonus** — Choose any two tradeskills at character creation; start with +10 skill in each
- **Faction Neutral** — No innate faction bonuses or penalties; start at 0 with all neutral-default races

**Starting skill bonuses:** +5 to any one weapon skill of your choice

## Available Classes
Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight, Cleric, Druid, Shaman, Beast Master, Rogue, Monk, Ranger, Witch Hunter, Bard, Magician, Wizard, Sorcerer, Enchanter, Necromancer, Blood Mage.

**All classes available.** No cultural, physiological, or alignment barrier prevents any Human from pursuing any discipline.

## Naming Conventions
Human names draw from a wide range of sounds with no strict pattern — the closest thing to a convention is that names tend to be pronounceable and unadorned. Surnames often derive from occupation, geography, or a memorable ancestor.

**Male examples:** Aldric, Bren, Caevan, Dorn, Erran, Fael, Gareth, Idris, Jowen, Kellan
**Female examples:** Aela, Brynn, Cira, Dara, Elvet, Fayne, Gerra, Isla, Jova, Kessa
**Surnames:** Ashford, Coppersole, Dunmore, Farlan, Greyhill, Merrick, Tanvale, Westerbrook

# Troll
*Evil ”” Swamp Hunters ”” The Unkillable*

## Overview
The defining fact about Trolls is regeneration. Wounds that would kill a Human close in hours; wounds that would incapacitate a Dwarf close in minutes. Trolls have been fighting a slow, grinding war of territorial attrition against every adjacent race for centuries, and they are still here, largely because the cost of removing them permanently is higher than most enemies want to pay. They know this. It has shaped a culture of patient aggression ”” take the hit, let it close, keep moving.

Troll culture is built around survival competence above all else. A Troll who cannot survive is a failure; a Troll who survives everything is the ideal. This produces excellent hunters, tenacious warriors, and a generalized indifference to personal injury that other races find unsettling. It also produces a population that doesn't organize well into large standing armies, preferring the smaller hunting warband as the primary unit ”” a group small enough that every member can assess everyone else's competence directly.

The evil alignment reflects genuine predatory amorality more than malice. Trolls hunt, take what they need, defend their territory with lethal force, and don't lose sleep over it. Conceptual frameworks about the rights of others don't map naturally onto a culture where those frameworks never developed. A Troll who does develop such a framework ”” who thinks carefully about the moral weight of what their people do ”” tends to leave, which is how Troll Rogues and Sorcerers generally happen.

Trolls are tall, loose-limbed, and built with an almost architectural quality ”” long bones, wide joints, skin that looks like it has been repaired several times (because it has). Skin is grey-green to deep viridian, warted and rough, frequently carrying moss, algae, and the marks of old injury. The most distinctive feature is the eyes: a bioluminescent yellow-green glow that persists in darkness and dims slightly when the Troll is at rest. Old Trolls accumulate scars that never quite closed evenly, giving their skin a layered, terrain-map quality that serves as an inadvertent record of their history.

## Homeland & Society
Trolls hold the southeastern marshlands ”” a vast, hostile network of swamp, peat bog, and brackish waterway that most other races write off as uninhabitable. Trolls find it ideal. The terrain neutralizes the size and coordination advantages of organized armies; the food supply (large marsh predators) requires exactly the skills Trolls have; and the permanent miasma keeps casual visitors away.

Troll society is organized into hunting warbands ”” loose groupings of five to fifteen individuals who claim a stretch of marsh as their range, hunt it, defend it, and occasionally trade with other bands. There is no central authority. An elder Troll carries influence proportional to how long they have survived and how many others they have kept alive; this influence is entirely informal and lasts only as long as the elder's practical usefulness.

The relationship between Troll shamanism and their ancestor tradition is unusual ”” Troll ancestors are consulted less for wisdom and more for survival information, and the spirits are regarded as practical resources rather than figures of reverence. Troll shamans serve as warband historians and tactical advisors as much as spiritual practitioners.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** High STR, STA; lower INT, WIS, CHA
- **Regeneration:** Trolls regenerate HP over time even in combat, at a slow but meaningful rate; fire and acid temporarily suppress this
- **Darkvision:** Bioluminescent eyes provide low-light vision
- **Starting region:** Southeastern marshlands
- **Faction standing:** Feared and distrusted by most Good and Neutral races; accepted in Evil-aligned settlements; trading tolerated in neutral ports

## Available Classes
Warrior, Shadow Knight, Cleric, Shaman, Beast Master, Magician, Sorcerer, Necromancer, Blood Mage.

**Blocked:** Paladin (Evil alignment), Druid (no connection to living nature ”” swamp connection is predatory, not nurturing), Rogue (size and gait incompatible with the craft), Monk (mass and temperament), Ranger (no tradition), Witch Hunter (alignment), Bard (insufficient range of expression), Wizard (cannot hold the sustained abstract concepts), Enchanter (requires social intelligence the culture doesn't cultivate).

## Naming Conventions
Troll names are wet, guttural, and heavy with back-of-the-throat sounds ”” the language developed in an environment where sound carries oddly over water and fog. Short names used between warband members; longer ceremonial names used only with the shamans. No written tradition; names are spoken and remembered.

**Male examples:** Bograk, Dredg, Grogg, Kraash, Moort, Retch, Sludge, Thrak, Wretch, Zarg
**Female examples:** Bogra, Drega, Griva, Kraak, Morra, Rasha, Slitha, Thrava, Vrekka, Zarba
**Warband names:** The Blooded Mire, The Deep Fang, The Ironbog, The Rotwater Kin, The Sunken Claw

# Fae
*Neutral ”” Ancient Small Folk ”” The Glass Court*

## Overview
The Fae are tiny, ancient, and deeply strange. Their physical scale invites condescension, which they have long since stopped bothering to correct because the people who underestimate them tend to reach instructive conclusions on their own. A Fae Enchanter who has watched twenty human empires rise and fall approaches the twenty-first with a patience that reads from outside as mild amusement, which is accurate ”” it is exactly that.

They predate almost everything. Fae oral tradition ”” which is more reliable than oral tradition has any right to be ”” describes events that other races' oldest written records treat as prehistory. What they were doing during all of that is not entirely clear. What is clear is that Fae civilization developed in complete isolation from the mortal world's religious and political structures, which explains their complete institutional indifference to both. They do not follow mortal gods because their relationship with power, age, and the world is not mediated through anything a mortal god can offer.

Fae magic is mind-based, illusory, and real. The illusions a Fae creates are indistinguishable from reality because the Fae understands perception well enough to produce it from scratch. The Glass Court of the portrait is not a metaphor: Fae enchanters maintain realities that other people inhabit, and the question of what is real and what is maintained is genuinely ambiguous in close proximity to a powerful Fae. They find the philosophical anxiety this produces in others gently interesting.

Fae are very small ”” the smallest playable race ”” and built with the delicate precision of something that was never subject to predation. Their wings are functional and strong relative to their mass; flight is real, not cosmetic. Skin carries a faint inner luminescence that is difficult to explain and impossible to fake. Eyes are large relative to the face, with an iris-to-white ratio that gives them the impression of looking at something slightly more than what's in front of them. Colors across the race are vivid ”” saturated skin tones, bright hair, distinctive wing patterns. Everything about the Fae signals "noticed" rather than "concealed."

## Homeland & Society
The Fae Mere is the closest Human term for where Fae live ”” an overlapping series of pocket territories woven through and between existing landscapes, accessible to Fae and inaccessible to most other races unless invited. To the outside world, a Fae territory looks like a particularly vivid forest glade, a quiet stretch of river, or an unusual hollow in the hillside that most travelers pass without investigating. From inside it, the architecture is vast, ancient, and operating on spatial rules that don't quite match the surrounding terrain.

Fae political structure is court-based ”” the Glass Courts, the Thorn Courts, the Deep Mere Courts ”” each operating as an independent polity with its own ancient hierarchy. Power within a court runs on a combination of age, magical skill, and social intelligence; the oldest Fae at court hold authority that is simply not questioned, not because of force but because contradicting someone who was ancient when your great-great-grandmother was born tends to go poorly.

Fae interaction with other races is on their terms or not at all. They trade information, which they have more of than anyone, for interesting things ”” experiences, objects with history, problems they haven't seen before. They do not need alliances for protection; their concealment is better than any wall. They engage when they find something worth engaging with, and they disengage when they don't.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** High INT, AGI, DEX; very low STR, STA
- **Flight:** True flight capability; immune to fall damage; can navigate certain terrain others cannot
- **Glamour:** Minor innate illusion ability (passive confusion/distraction effect)
- **Tiny:** Cannot wear standard-sized equipment; Fae-scaled gear required; some class options have modified implementations
- **No mortal deity bond:** Cannot be Cleric (no mortal god connection) or Paladin (same)
- **Starting region:** The Fae Mere, border-accessible territories
- **Faction standing:** Neutral with essentially everyone ”” the Fae have been around long enough that taking sides looks short-term

## Available Classes
Warrior, Druid, Rogue, Ranger, Bard, Magician, Wizard, Sorcerer, Enchanter.

**Blocked:** Paladin (Fae follow no mortal religious hierarchies), Cleric (same), Shadow Knight (requires genuine darkness ”” the Fae relationship to darkness is different and non-qualifying), Shaman (no ancestor-spirit tradition), Beast Master (their magic is illusory and mind-based; the beast-spirit bond operates on an incompatible channel, and their form is not suited to the melee demands), Monk (size makes the physical demands incompatible), Witch Hunter (the Fae are old enough that their magic doesn't register the same way to witch-hunting instinct ”” they are outside the frame), Necromancer (alignment and the specific relationship to death that the Fae have makes this categorically uninteresting to them), Blood Mage (biology incompatible).

## Naming Conventions
Fae names are airy, musical, and frequently multi-syllabic in a way that sounds natural rather than elaborate. The full name is used only within court; outside it, Fae use a shortened personal name or a single-word title. Court titles are earned and meaningful; using one you haven't earned is noted.

**Male examples:** Aevon, Cyren, Daelveth, Floryn, Ieven, Lyrel, Maeven, Silveth, Tryllion, Wyn
**Female examples:** Aelith, Cyra, Daelva, Flora, Ieva, Lyra, Maeve, Silvara, Tryl, Wynne
**Court titles (examples):** the Pale, the Gloaming, the Twice-Woken, the Patient One, the Unasked Question

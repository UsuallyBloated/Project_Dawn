# Kobold
*Evil ”” Tunnel Kin ”” The Pack That Persists*

## Overview
Kobolds are small, quick, and underestimated so consistently that most of them have stopped being surprised by it. The mistake is always the same: observers see the size, the nervous energy, the tendency to scatter when threatened, and conclude soft target. What they miss is the pack. A Kobold alone is manageable. A Kobold with six others who share a tunnel network and have been practicing this particular ambush for three nights is an entirely different problem.

Kobold culture is built around the pack ”” the extended family-hunting group that inhabits a shared tunnel system. The pack is everything: food source, protection, social life, and identity. Individual ambition is not a Kobold value; pack advancement is. A Kobold who gains power and doesn't bring the pack up with them is considered to have failed at the only thing that matters. This is why Kobold adventurers are rare but not absent ”” they leave because the pack needs something the outside world has, and they intend to bring it back.

They are Evil in alignment and direct about it in a way that can be disarming. Kobolds raid when they're hungry, claim territory through violence when they need it, and have a very short list of things they consider morally relevant. Other packs are competition; outsiders are resources or threats; their own pack is sacred. The moral framework is small and internally consistent, which makes it predictable once you understand it. A Kobold who has given their word to a non-pack person is doing something unusual and usually means it.

Kobolds are small, dog-muzzled, and built for tight spaces ”” narrow shoulders, flexible spines, hands that are small enough to reach into gaps most other races can't access. Ears are large, pointed, and mobile; they track sound well and express emotion clearly. Fur is short and varied: earth tones (brown, tan, grey, black) are most common, with some clans showing distinctive patterns that serve as visual clan markers at distance. Eyes are large and adapted for low light ”” Kobolds see well underground and have a mild light sensitivity in strong sunlight. Teeth are sharp and numerous, and Kobolds grin more than other races, which some find friendly and others find alarming.

## Homeland & Society
Kobold territory is wherever the tunnel network reaches ”” a distributed underground system that extends beneath large sections of the central plains, threading under Human farmland, between Dwarven holds, and into the underbelly of several major cities. The tunnel network is the homeland; the surface is the hunting ground.

Pack structure is matrilineal, though leadership is not automatically female ”” the senior female of the founding line holds cultural authority, but the pack's hunting leader (the Alpha) is the most effective fighter regardless of sex. These two roles often sit in the same individual; when they don't, the negotiation between them defines the pack's culture. Packs range from a dozen to nearly a hundred individuals; larger packs tend to split into daughter-packs who maintain alliance with the original.

Kobold religion centers on dragon-ancestor mythology ”” a belief that Kobolds are the lesser kin of dragons, and that the ancestor-dragons watch and occasionally reward the worthy. Kobold shamans are the keepers of this tradition. Whether the ancestor-dragons are literally real, metaphorical, or something else is a question that different packs answer differently and occasionally fight about.

Relations with other races are predominantly competitive or predatory. Kobolds raid Human and Halfling farmland as a matter of course; they have territorial conflicts with Dwarves over underground space; and they maintain a grudging mutual recognition with Dark Elves based on shared tunnel access interests. They do not trust anyone outside the pack. Some individual Kobolds have broken from this default, but it requires specific experience to produce.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** High AGI, DEX; lower STR, STA; CHA penalty with non-Kobold races
- **Pack tactics:** Bonus to attack and defense when fighting alongside other Kobolds
- **Tunnelcraft:** Bonus to underground navigation, trap-setting, and locating structural weaknesses
- **Low-light vision:** Good underground sight; mild sunlight sensitivity
- **Size:** Small; some large weapons require two-handed grip
- **Starting region:** Tunnel network access points, Kobold surface settlements
- **Faction standing:** Negative with most Good-aligned races; accepted among Evil-aligned factions; cautious tolerance from some Neutral trading cities

## Available Classes
Warrior, Shadow Knight, Cleric, Shaman, Beast Master, Rogue, Bard, Magician, Sorcerer, Necromancer, Blood Mage.

**Blocked:** Paladin (Evil alignment), Druid (no living-nature tradition), Monk (discipline requires a stillness the culture doesn't produce), Ranger (no tradition), Witch Hunter (alignment), Wizard (lifespan too short for meaningful academic completion ”” Kobolds live fast, and the decades of study Wizardry demands exceed most of their natural lifespan), Enchanter (requires sustained concentration and social intelligence in a direction Kobolds don't develop).

## Naming Conventions
Kobold names are short, sharp, and built for a voice that needs to carry in tight underground corridors without volume. Two to three syllables maximum. Pack names are descriptive, often named for a notable feature of the pack's tunnel system or for a victory of the founding generation.

**Male examples:** Brak, Drix, Fikk, Grak, Ikki, Krex, Nark, Rikki, Skrix, Zikk
**Female examples:** Brikka, Drika, Fikka, Grika, Ikka, Kreka, Narka, Rikka, Skrikka, Zikka
**Pack names:** the Ashfang, the Copperhole, the Deepclaw, the Ironscrabble, the Mudtooth, the Rustbone, the Sharpstone, the Stonebite

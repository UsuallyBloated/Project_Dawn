## Overview
The Sylphari are plant-blooded people — not spirits, not constructs, but a people whose
flesh grew the way heartwood grows. They do not look lush; they look *carved*. Tall,
gaunt, and patient, they keep the deep jungle groves the way other races keep temples,
and they measure time in seasons and rings rather than days. A Sylphari who answers a
question immediately is either young or alarmed.

Their defining strangeness is the **seasonal aspect**: a Sylphari's body keeps the
calendar. Spring brings pale new-growth coloring and blossom; summer deepens them to
full green; autumn turns them amber and red; winter grays them toward dormancy — slower,
quieter, contemplative. Culture follows the cycle: courtships in spring, councils in
summer, reckonings in autumn, and in winter the groves go inward and nearly silent.
Travelers learn to schedule their business accordingly.

They are the obvious druidic people, but the relationship runs the other way around:
Druids of other races learn to *speak to* the green; the Sylphari simply *are* it. Their
reverence for Erindra is less worship than family resemblance.

## Physical Description
| Sex | Height | Weight | Build | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Male | 6'4"–7'0" | 140–180 lbs | Gaunt, long-limbed | Dimorphism is minimal; broader shoulder-burl is the usual tell |
| Female | 6'2"–6'10" | 130–170 lbs | Gaunt, long-limbed | Flowering features often fuller in spring aspect |

Skin is bark-patterned, not green — birch-pale with dark striations, rosewood-warm, or
aspen-silver, with the bone structure visible beneath like grain under finish. Hair is
not keratin but growth: petals, moss, fine vines, or hanging seed-pods, varying by
lineage and season. Veins of bioluminescent sap glow faintly in the dark — under the
skin, in the eyes, along old scars. Many Sylphari cultivate living lichen and moss on
their skin as deliberate ornament: clan markings, profession, rank. Tattoos, but grown.

## Homeland & Society
*TBD — committed direction: deep-jungle groves; possibly adjacent to (or interleaved
with) the Vesperin canopy city.* Working questions: same jungle as the Vesperin or a
neighboring grove biome; how a rooted culture handles the Saurian threat; what a
Sylphari "city" even is — the leading idea is that the grove *is* the architecture,
grown rather than built.

Social structure is the lineage-grove: a stand of ancestral trees and the families
grown from it. Elders are genuinely old — the eldest remember other races' grandparents
as children — and authority is gerontocratic in the gentlest possible way: nobody
argues with someone who has seen the argument four times before.

## Racial Traits
- **Stat profile:** Highest WIS of any playable race; strong INT; low STR and STA
- **Photosynthesis:** Strong regeneration in direct sunlight; none at night or underground
- **Barkskin:** Natural armor
- **Sapblood:** Highly resistant to poison and disease; sap burns — vulnerable to fire
- **Rooted:** While standing ground, very hard to move or trip
- **Seasonal Aspect:** Appearance shifts with the world calendar (cosmetic in v1)

## Available Classes
Warrior, Cleric, Druid, Shaman, Beast Master, Ranger, Rogue, Bard.

**Under discussion:** Monk (does a grown body take conditioning the way a born one
does?), Sorcerer (sapline in place of bloodline — plausible, undecided).

**Blocked:** Paladin and Shadow Knight (no oath tradition in either direction; the
grove does not crusade), Wizard / Magician / Enchanter (no written arcane tradition,
and Enchantment requires a social mind the Sylphari find alien — they read seasons, not
faces), Witch Hunter (judgment belongs to autumn councils, not inquisitors),
Necromancer (anathema — the dead feed the grove and become it; raising them is theft
from every descendant), Blood Mage (biology incompatible — sap is not blood, as with
the Fae).

## Naming Conventions
*Draft.* Two-part names: a season-word for the season of their sprouting, joined to a
grove-word from their lineage. Names are expected to change at major life stages — a
Sylphari who has kept one name for two centuries is making a statement.

**Examples:** Thawmoss, Emberbough, Palerain, Duskroot, Brightsap, Galewillow,
Frostburl, Seedsong.
**Lineage-groves:** the Elder Stand, the Bloodroot Ring, the Pale Birches, the
Counting Rings.

# Wizard
*Arcane DPS (Specialist) — Any Alignment — Study (memorized spellbook)*

## Overview
The highest damage ceiling in the game — when prepared. The Wizard memorizes spells from a physical spellbook before encounters; they cannot swap them freely in the field. When fully prepared, their output exceeds every other class against single targets. When their prepared spells run dry, they are a slow staff-user waiting for mana regeneration. The class rewards preparation and punishes improvisation.

## Combat Profile
- **Armor:** Cloth
- **Weapons:** Staves
- **Primary Resource:** Mana (finite preparation — spells memorized from spellbook, not freely swappable)
- **Pet:** None

## Spells
| Spell | Type | Damage | Mana | Cast | Cooldown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ice Spear | Ice dmg | 50 | 28 | 1.0s | 5s |
| Flame Wave | Fire dmg | 75 | 38 | 1.8s | 9s |
| Blizzard | Ice dmg | 65 | 45 | 2.0s | 12s |
| Thunder Clap | Lightning dmg | 100 | 50 | 2.5s | 14s |
| Meteor | Fire dmg | 130 | 65 | 3.5s | 20s |

Meteor is the highest single-hit damage spell in the game. All Wizard spells have meaningful cast times — nothing fires instantly.

## Skills
None — pure spellcaster.

## Race Availability
**Available:** Human, Elf, Dark Elf, Gnome, Dwarf, Half-Elf, Iksar, Fae

**Blocked:** Ogre, Troll (cannot hold the required concepts); Wood Elf (oral tradition only — no written spellbook tradition); Halfling (no institutional arcane tradition); Minotaur (displaced people with no fixed academic schools); Vah Shir (no written arcane tradition); Kobold (lifespan too short for meaningful academic completion); Half-Ogre.

## Playstyle
Prepare spells before entering content. Commit highest-output spells to memorized slots. Enter combat, spend prepared resources against high-value targets, disengage if preparation is exhausted. The Wizard is the glass cannon of arcane classes — devastating in the opening of an encounter, increasingly vulnerable as resources are spent. Solo viability requires careful resource management between encounters.

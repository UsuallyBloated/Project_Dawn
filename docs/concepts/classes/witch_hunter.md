# Witch Hunter
*Anti-Magic Specialist — Good preferred — Training*

## Overview
The Witch Hunter's conviction is vocational, not religious. They are not Paladins adjacent in motivation — they do not carry a god's name into battle. They carry a set of skills developed specifically to detect, counter, and dismantle magical threats. Enchanted enemies, summoned creatures, transformed characters, active spellcasters: all of these register differently to a Witch Hunter than they do to any other class.

The defining characteristic: the Witch Hunter sees magic as infrastructure. Other classes fight what is in front of them. The Witch Hunter fights what is holding the enemy together. Strip the buffs, interrupt the cast, break the transformation, and what remains is usually much more manageable. Against a fully buffed Enchanter or a transformed character running on an ability the Witch Hunter just took away, the power differential is significant. Against a standard melee fighter with no magical support, the Witch Hunter is a competent but unremarkable combatant.

This is not a general-purpose class. It is the class you bring when magic is the problem.

## Combat Profile
- **Armor:** Chain/leather
- **Weapons:** Crossbows, one-handed swords, silver weapons
- **Primary Resource:** Both mana and stamina
- **Pet:** None

**Expose** — Reworked from a damage spell to an active defensive-strip. One buff removed from the target per cast. Prioritize highest-value buffs: damage shields, heals-over-time, protective enchantments. Against heavily buffed targets, the Witch Hunter opens by stripping before dealing damage.

**Spellbreak** — Interrupt an active cast and silence the target for 4 seconds. The silence prevents any spellcasting or skill use from the target for its duration. The most powerful tool against caster enemies; useless against pure melee.

**Antimagic Ward** — An area suppression effect centered on the Witch Hunter. Active buffs on enemies in the radius have their remaining duration halved while the Ward is up. Does not strip buffs entirely; degrades them. Used when fighting groups of buffed enemies or alongside an allied caster fight.

**Banishment** — High damage holy-typed spell. Particularly effective against summoned creatures (elementals, pets, animated undead) — Banishment deals bonus damage against any entity that was magically created or maintains magical existence.

| Truesight Strike | 1.2x | 10 | 8s | Melee; ignores magical evasion; hits characters in Camouflage, stealth, or partial phase-resistance |

**Exploit Weakness** — The synergy skill. Every Expose cast increases this skill's bonus damage. Strip three buffs first, then Exploit Weakness for the spike. Against heavily enchanted targets, this can hit extremely hard.

**Truesight Strike** — Ignores magical evasion, partial phasing (Fae-Old), and stealth effects. A Ranger in Camouflage is hiding from everyone else. Not from the Witch Hunter.

## Anti-Transformation Mechanics
*Design target — not yet implemented.*

Witch Hunters have passive Transform Detection. Transformed characters (Fallen Paladin, Redeemed Shadow Knight, Revenant, any race-specific transformation) register to the Witch Hunter as detectably different from baseline. This does not reveal their transformation publicly; it means the Witch Hunter knows.

Against transformed targets:
- Expose has a chance to suppress the transformation's active effects for 10 seconds (not permanently; the transformation is not removed; its combat bonuses are temporarily dampened)
- Exploit Weakness gains its bonus from suppressed transformation effects as well as stripped buffs
- Truesight Strike bypasses any evasion or phase-resistance granted by transformation

This is not universally hostile to transformed allies. A Witch Hunter in a group with a Fallen Paladin knows what they are traveling with; they do not automatically suppress that ally's abilities. The suppression mechanic is relevant in PvP and against enemy transformed NPCs.

## Race Availability
**Available:** Human, Elf, Wood Elf, Gnome, Halfling, Dwarf, Half-Elf, Felhari, Half-Ogre

**Blocked:** All Evil-aligned races by default — the Witch Hunter tradition emerged specifically in opposition to the magical practices those cultures maintain, which creates both cultural incompatibility and the practical problem that the Witch Hunter's training is not offered to them. Ogre, Troll, Kel`varath, Dark Elf, Kobold: excluded on these grounds. Minotaur: no institutional connection to the discipline. Fae: their relationship to illusion and transformation magic is too entangled with exactly what the Witch Hunter is trained to dismantle; they cannot occupy that role cleanly.

## Playstyle
Against magical targets: open with Expose to strip the most valuable buff. Follow with Spellbreak if the target is casting. Use Antimagic Ward if fighting multiple buffed enemies simultaneously. Once buffs are reduced, Exploit Weakness hits for significant damage. Silver Bolt and Witchfire fill the sustained damage windows.

Against non-magical targets: the Witch Hunter fights without most of their toolkit being relevant. Witchfire and Rite of Warding still work. Silver Bolt still deals 1.5x. Banishment loses its bonus. Spellbreak is irrelevant. The class is functional but not specialized; it is a weaker Warrior/Cleric hybrid against mundane threats.

The Witch Hunter's skill expression is in recognizing what the enemy has and stripping it in the right order. A Witch Hunter who Exposes the wrong buff, or wastes Spellbreak on a melee fighter, or burns Exploit Weakness before stripping anything is performing well below the class's actual power level. Played well, the class systematically dismantles what other classes have to fight through.

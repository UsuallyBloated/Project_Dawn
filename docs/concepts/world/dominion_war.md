# The Dominion War — Historical Lore
*Three hundred years ago. The event that produced the Ashen Marches, broke the Kel`varath Empire, and left things that have not stopped.*

---

## What It Was

The Dominion War was not called the Dominion War while it was happening. The Kel`varath called it the Reclamation Campaign — a military effort to reassert control over territories that had rebelled during a succession crisis. The western alliance that formed against them called it the Defense, then the Coalition War, then eventually just "the war" as it wore on long enough that naming it seemed optimistic about its ending.

The Dominion War is what historians settled on afterward, when the Kel`varath Empire was gone and someone had to name what happened.

It lasted eleven years, across three distinct phases, and ended with a military stalemate that resolved into a political collapse. The Kel`varath Empire did not lose in the field. It dissolved — the supply lines broke, the succession crisis that started the war was resolved by the deaths of all claimants, the territories that had rebelled used the chaos to establish permanent independence, and by the time the Dominion tried to reassert itself there was no longer a centralized authority to reassert it through.

What was left: the Ashen Marches, where the final campaign's three major forces fought to a simultaneous standstill. Ixareth-Kul, capital of the Dominion, in ruins — not destroyed by the war but abandoned during it and stripped by time and rivals since. The Kobold tunnels beneath Cogsworth, originally Dominion construction, now independent. The Southeastern Marshlands, technically always Kel`varath territory, now loosely organized tribal rather than imperial.

And the dead. The Ashen Marches are full of the dead. This is the part that did not resolve.

## The Three Phases

### Phase One — The Reclamation Campaign (Years 1–3)
The Dominion moved west. The succession crisis had produced a new Emperor — Draketh IV — whose claim was legitimate but contested internally. He chose external war to consolidate support, targeting the western territories that had refused tribute during the succession crisis.

The initial campaign was successful. Three territories fell in Year 1. The western alliance had not yet formed — each territory was responding independently, and the Dominion's military was better organized. By Year 2, the Dominion controlled more territory than it had in forty years.

Year 3: the western territories stopped fighting separately. The Coalition — five powers including the Greyveil city-state, the Dwarven Mountain Holds, and the Elf settlements at Aelindra — formed a military alliance with shared command. The first Coalition army assembled at the border of what is now the Ashen Marches.

### Phase Two — The Stalemate Years (Years 4–8)
Five years of back-and-forth that neither side won. The Dominion had superior organization and proven tactics; the Coalition had defensive advantages and supply lines that the Dominion could not cut. Neither side had the momentum to end the conflict.

The Ashen Marches became the contested ground in Year 5, when both armies established major forward positions in the borderland. Three battles were fought there in Years 5, 6, and 7 — each costly, none decisive. The ground was salted so many times with blood and siege-fire that by Year 7 it had begun to change at a level below the surface.

The Third Army appeared in Year 7. Neither side admits responsibility for it. Both sides had encountered formations during the Marches fighting that matched no known unit's insignia, operated with no supply line that scouts could trace, and engaged both armies with apparent indifference to which side they were fighting. The Dominion military records categorize them as a mercenary formation; the Coalition records do not categorize them at all.

### Phase Three — Collapse (Years 9–11)
Draketh IV died in Year 9 — not from combat. Illness. The succession crisis that had started the war reasserted itself immediately and with more urgency, because the war had burned through most of the viable claimants. Three Dominion nobles claimed the throne. Two of them died. The third had to retreat to Ixareth-Kul to hold his claim and pulled two legions from the front to do it.

The Coalition did not push the advantage as aggressively as they might have — their alliance was fraying too, different members wanting different things from the peace. The war wound down through exhaustion and negotiation rather than victory. Peace terms were signed in Year 11. The western territories kept their independence. The Dominion kept its internal structure. Both sides stopped fighting.

The Dominion never fully recovered. The third succession claimant — Vethras — held the throne for eleven years before dying without an heir, and the institutional structure of the empire did not survive the subsequent chaos. By forty years after the war's end, Ixareth-Kul had been abandoned. The empire was over.

## The Ashen Marches — Why They Are What They Are

Three major battles were fought in the Marches. After each one, the dead were not properly cleared — the fighting resumed before burial was complete. After the third battle, the fighting stopped and both armies retreated, but neither side controlled the ground enough to send burial parties back.

The Marches dead were left in the field.

What happened to the soil at a deeper level — why the ash is not the remnant of fire, why the ground does not grow things correctly, why the Revenants rose as organized faction forces rather than feral undead — is not agreed upon by scholars. Theories include:

- **The accumulated weight of unresolved death** — three battles' worth of souls that did not get proper rites, sufficient in number to create a resonance in the ground
- **Something that was already in the Marches** — the convergence point at the center predates the war; whatever is there may have responded to the deaths in a way that amplified the undead outcome
- **The Third Army** — whatever the Third Army was, their dead are there too, and their dead behave differently from the Dominion or Coalition dead
- **Vethis's incomplete accounting** — a theological argument that the unburied dead of three battles could not be properly recorded and the resulting ledger gap has a physical effect on the ground

The most honest answer is that nobody knows. The Druids who examined the ground thirty years after the war reported that the soil itself had changed — not poisoned, not burned, but structurally different in a way they could not name. They recommended the area be left alone. It has been.

## The Third Army — What Is Known

Very little. Their first confirmed appearance was in Year 7 of the war, in the Marches. They engaged both the Dominion and Coalition forces. They took casualties. They did not retrieve their dead. They did not carry identifiable equipment. Their soldiers, when examined after battle, were every known race — Human, Kel`varath, Dwarf, Elf, others — wearing no shared armor and no unit markings.

Coalition military intelligence spent two years trying to identify them. The summary report, preserved in the Greyveil archives, concludes: "Origin unknown. Objective unknown. Allegiance unknown. The formation fights with discipline consistent with professional military training. The objective of their engagement with both our forces and the Dominion's forces is not apparent. They do not seek ground. They do not pursue routed forces. They appear to be fighting for a purpose unrelated to territorial control."

The Third Army disappeared after the Marches battles. Their camp in the Marches remains. Their dead in the Marches rose as Revenants who remember no allegiance except to something they were ordered to retrieve. Their commander, the Nameless Commander, is in the Final Encampment dungeon. The orders are in the Records Tent. The objective is in the records, if anyone can read them.

## Historiography

The Dominion War is the most-studied historical event in the current era, which means there are more wrong books about it than right ones. Common myths:

**"The Coalition destroyed the Kel`varath Empire."** No. The Kel`varath Empire destroyed itself. The Coalition's military never reached Ixareth-Kul. The capital was abandoned through political collapse, not conquest.

**"The Third Army was a Dominion black-ops unit."** The Dominion military records survive in partial form. They include detailed records of losses to the Third Army. They did not build something that then fought them.

**"The Ashen Marches were scorched by Wizard battle magic."** The ash in the Marches does not match any known fire spell residue, according to three separate analyses. The Wizards' Guild resents the attribution and has released statements saying so on three occasions.

**"The Nameless Commander was a Dominion general who changed sides."** The Third Army predates the point at which any Dominion general could have defected with a functional force. The timeline does not work.

The correct answer to most questions about the Dominion War is: the primary sources are incomplete, the secondary sources are motivated, and several of the most important actors left no record at all.

# NPC Profile — Bess Holloway
*The Crossed Keys Inn. Millhaven. Greenshire. Levels 1–10.*

---

## Identity

**Full name:** Bess Holloway *(BESS HOL-oh-way)*
**Race:** Human
**Class:** Bard (former — level 12; she has not used any Bard ability in anger since she was thirty-one, but the skills are there)
**Age:** 54
**Alignment:** Neutral

**Physical description:** Medium height, with the posture of someone who stood at bars and stages for twenty years and then stood behind a bar for nineteen more, and whose back has opinions about all of it. Hair the color of old wood, kept back with a cord when she is working, which is most of the time. The Crossed Keys' apron. Hands that show their history — she has cut more vegetables and opened more casks than she has played lute strings in the last decade, and her hands remember what they do most often.

She does not look like a former Bard. She looks like an innkeeper who is good at it. This is accurate. The Bard background shows up in less obvious ways: she knows when a conversation in a room shifts, she remembers names after one introduction, and she has not failed to notice when a guest is lying since approximately Year 823.

There is a line on the left side of her mouth that might be a faint scar. If asked, she says she was bitten by a horse when she was fifteen, which she says lightly enough that it discourages follow-up questions.

---

## Personality

Bess Holloway runs the Crossed Keys the way she would have wanted to be treated when she was traveling and playing — the room is clean, the food is honest, the fire is going when it should be going. She is not warm in the first-meeting sense of the word. She is consistent, which over time produces the same result and is more reliable.

She does not tell people what to do. She notes what they are doing and adjusts her responses accordingly. Players who treat her inn respectfully receive service. Players who treat it carelessly receive service and a look she does not explain. Players who cause damage to the inn receive a bill and a request to find somewhere else to sleep.

Nineteen years of running the Crossed Keys has given her a specific kind of patience: the patience of someone who has learned that most things that look urgent are not, and most things that look manageable are. The exception is the jar in the back room, which she has managed by not thinking about it on most days. This strategy has served her adequately for nineteen years. It is beginning to develop cracks.

She is aware the inn has a history she does not know completely. She has not committed to finding out, because she has learned that knowing things commits you to doing something about them, and she has the inn and nineteen years of relationships in Millhaven to protect. This is not cowardice. It is a calculated position. Players who come back from the Millhaven Cellars asking about the passage in the back cellar are the first event in her adult life that has genuinely required her to reconsider the calculation.

---

## The Jar

The previous owner pressed it into her hands on the day of the sale, separately from everything else. It was in a canvas bag inside a box he had carried separately from the rest of his personal belongings. He said: "This stays with the building. Don't open it. Don't give it to anyone." He said it the way people say things that they know sound irrational but mean completely. She put it in her locked back room and has honored the instruction in the way you honor things when the person who gave them was clearly serious.

She has noticed that it is warm. She has not spent time thinking about what that means. She is aware that she has not spent time thinking about it and that this itself is a choice she has made, every year, for nineteen years. The Bard background — the ability to read when someone's performance of calm is covering something else — is not comfortable when she turns it on herself.

She does not know Aldren Coss left Millhaven the morning after the sale. She has thought about finding out where he went and has decided against it every year. She knows, in the way that you know things you have not said aloud, that if she finds out where he is, she will want to ask why, and she is not prepared for what the answer might require her to do.

---

## Relationship to Players

**First meeting:** Standard innkeeper interaction. She notes what you order and whether you are polite and files you. Most adventurers who come through are filed in the "no particular trouble" category and receive reliable, neutral service.

**Building standing:** Completing the first round of Greenshire starter quests passes through the Crossed Keys — delivering packages, running messages, the usual. She notices players who do these things without making a mess of her inn. She notices players who leave tips and players who do not. She does not say anything about any of this for several levels.

**Moderate standing:** She will have an actual conversation. She will ask where you have been and listen to the answer and tell you things she knows about zones she has been to — she toured the eastern territories in her Bard years and her knowledge of Greyveil, Harrowmere, and the coastal roads is practical and current. She will not discuss the inn's history.

**High standing (or post-Cellars discovery):** She will tell you about the back room. Not everything, and not all at once — she tells you in the order in which she has decided the information can be given. The ledger first, with instructions not to read it where anyone can see. Then, later, the jar. She does not offer to open it. She stands at the top of the stairs.

---

## What She Knows and Won't Say First

- The previous owner's name: Aldren Coss. She will not volunteer this until high trust is established.
- That the ledger is in a cipher: she does not know this; she has not tried to read it; she is aware this means she has been keeping something she cannot evaluate.
- That the inn has a sealed passage in the back cellar: she has known about this since Year 827 (twelve years in), when a pipe repair revealed it. She had it resealed and has not spoken of it.
- That three Millhaven families have something to do with what is in the ledger: she suspects this from the way Rorik Dorn and Magistrate Harwick have, over nineteen years, been slightly more than normally attentive to whether anyone asks about the Crossed Keys' history.
- That the jar is warm because of what is inside it: she does not know this and has decided, deliberately, not to investigate.

---

## Quest Hooks

- **The Cellars Connection** — Players returning from the Millhaven Cellars with knowledge of the concealed passage ask her about it. This begins the Bess questline.
- **The Ledger** — She gives players the third Gentlemen's Society ledger after establishing trust. She asks them to tell her what it says if they decode it. She does not specify what she will do with that information.
- **Aldren Coss** — After the jar is opened (by the players; she will not do it), she will ask the one question she has been holding for nineteen years: where did Aldren go, and is he all right. This becomes a side quest with a potential resolution in Greyveil or further east.

## Dialogue Samples

*First meeting:*
"Room's two copper for the night. Includes breakfast if you want it before the sixth bell. Dinner is three copper, served at the fifth bell, and it's whatever Maret cooked, no substitutions. You need anything else, ask at the bar."

*On Millhaven:*
"It's a quiet town. That's not the same as an easy town — the quiet ones are the ones where everything gets managed privately instead of loudly, and the management piles up. Nineteen years. I've seen the pile. Most of it's nothing. Some of it's the kind of nothing that everybody's agreed to call nothing, which is different."

*On the back room (if asked directly before trust is established):*
"That's storage. It's locked because it's my business and I'd like to keep it that way. Help you with anything else?"

*On the jar (to players she trusts, who have asked):*
"The man I bought this place from pushed it into my hands like it was the most important thing he owned. He looked like it was the thing he least wanted to own. He said 'don't open it' and then he left and I've respected that instruction for nineteen years, mostly because of how he looked when he gave it. I don't know what's in it. I know it's warm. I've decided those two things can stay separate for as long as I decide they can."

*After the quest chain resolves (one version):*
"I've been running this inn for nineteen years and I've been keeping something in the back room for nineteen years that I didn't fully understand and I've been making the same decision every year that knowing more would cost more than not knowing, and I have to tell you — I thought I was right about that calculation until now. I'm not sure anymore. Have a drink. This one's on the house. I'm buying it for both of us."

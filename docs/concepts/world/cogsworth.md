# Cogsworth
*Mechanical City / Underground — Levels 10–25 — Gnome / Kobold*

## Overview
Cogsworth is the most vertical zone in the game. The city occupies seven levels of tunnels, platforms, and open shafts, and at any given moment there are three different elevations visible from a single vantage point. Gnomes built the top five levels. Kobolds built the bottom two and have been expanding ever since, in ways that increasingly baffle the Gnome engineers above them.

The shared city is a détente born of mutual need. Gnomes have the precision and the theory. Kobolds have the willingness to go into spaces that are genuinely dangerous during construction and the instinct for structural improvisation. The result is a city that functions better than it has any right to, held together partly by engineering and partly by the fact that both races are significantly more interested in making things than in fighting about who made them.

Tinkering reaches its deepest development here. Neither Gnomes nor Kobolds can develop Tinkering past basic ranks without coming to Cogsworth at some point.

## Setting
The upper levels (Gnome-controlled) are organized by guild — the Clock Guild, the Steam Guild, the Precision Works. Everything is labeled. Everything has a sign. The organization is comprehensive and slightly oppressive. The lower levels (Kobold) are organized by proximity to what needed building — trails widen where cart traffic wore them, platforms exist where someone needed to stand. It is messy and it works.

Level three is the Crossgate — the neutral zone where Gnomes and Kobolds do commerce with each other and with outsiders. The best vendors in the zone are here.

## Dungeon — The Abandoned Works
*Levels 18–25.*

Below the Kobold levels, there is a level that predates both Gnomes and Kobolds. Whatever built it first is still down there. The Abandoned Works is the group dungeon: five levels of partially functional ancient machinery, intermittently active automation, and enemies that were built for purposes no one currently alive remembers. Tinkering players can interact with some of the machinery in ways other classes cannot.

**Named:** The Foreman (level 25 ancient construct overseer) — drops a gear-set unique to Tinkerers; mechanical trinket usable by any class.

## Services
- Clockgate Market (level 3 Crossgate): widest item variety of any mid-tier zone; mechanical components; alchemical supplies; Kobold salvage market
- Gnome guild halls: Tinkering and Clockwork Engineering trainers; Gnome racial quests; precision crafting facilities
- Kobold warrens (lower levels): Kobold trainers; salvage-based Tinkering recipes; faction quests
- Tinkering workshop: combine station accessible to any character with basic Tinkering skill

## The Cogsworth Fault

The détente works. It has worked for two generations. This does not mean there is no fault line.

The specific ongoing dispute is the Steam Valve Patent. Seventeen years ago, a Gnome guild apprentice named Merrit Copperwick submitted a design to the Clock Guild for a pressure-regulating valve assembly that substantially reduced steam system failures. The Clock Guild logged and shelved it. Six months later, the same design — or functionally identical to it — appeared in the Kobold lower levels as part of an expansion project. The Clock Guild accused the Warren Council of guild espionage. The Warren Council pointed out that the Kobold engineer who built the expansion, a tunneler named Kisk-Three-Paws, had independently derived the same solution from different principles because the physics required it.

Neither side is lying. Both solutions are genuine. Both inventors arrived at the same design independently, which the mathematics supports is not only possible but likely given the specific pressure conditions both were solving for. The Clock Guild does not find this persuasive.

**Mechanically:** Characters who have done quests for both factions above Tier 2 can access a quest called *The Valve Problem* — it requires reading the Copperwick patent filing, accessing Kisk-Three-Paws' design notes, and consulting a materials scholar in Greyveil about independent derivation timelines. Resolving it raises standing with both factions simultaneously (very rare in Cogsworth), costs the player standing with exactly no one, and closes a background grievance that otherwise degrades faction standing gains over the course of the zone's questline.

## Faction Notes
Two factions operate in parallel here: the Gnome Guilds and the Kobold Warren Council. Characters must navigate both.

Gnomes and Kobolds both start neutral to most outsiders. The Gnomes warm to characters who solve mechanical problems (the Malfunctioning Construct quests); the Kobolds warm to characters who help them get access to materials the Gnome guilds are restricting.

The two factions do not conflict with each other — raising one does not lower the other. The political friction is ambient, present in NPC dialogue, but not mechanically adversarial for the player. The Steam Valve Patent dispute (see above) is the primary ambient grievance; resolving it is optional but smooths the zone's faction navigation significantly.

# The Architects — Master Document
*What is known, what is suspected, and what the system is designed to do.*

---

## The Short Version

An unknown civilization built a network of interconnected sites across the world approximately five thousand years ago. The sites are still active. The network runs a recurring process called the Calibration State, which checks its own status and attempts to communicate. The last Calibration State was four hundred years ago. The next one is not scheduled in any accessible record.

The civilization left no name, no unambiguous history, and no living successors — or none that have identified themselves. What they left is the network, designed to outlast them and to eventually communicate with whoever came after them. The communication is addressed to "whoever finds this first, after." Players are whoever finds this first, after.

## What the Sites Have in Common

**Construction:** Fitted stone with no mortar, tolerances that require tools not currently available. Geometric proportions that repeat across all sites regardless of geography. Six-fingered handprints at load-bearing positions — not decorative.

**Remainders:** Every confirmed site has a warm object or space. The warmth is at a consistent temperature across all sites, has not varied measurably in recorded observation, and is not produced by any heat source that current understanding can identify. The warmth is the Architect presence persisting in a physical form after the Architects themselves are gone.

**Notation:** A consistent system encoding spatial relationships, ratios, and conditional logic rather than narrative language. Uses a base-12 counting system. The pre-Dominion Kel`varath ritual script shares structural features — not the same system, but derivable from a common ancestor. The Architect notation at different sites cross-references other sites, suggesting the notation was designed to be read across locations rather than at a single site.

**The six-fingered proportions:** All passages, doorframes, and furniture-equivalent features are designed for a bipedal form approximately Human height with broader shoulders, a different hip structure, and six fingers of roughly equal length. Three light-gathering recesses in headrests and observation positions — either three eyes or two eyes and a secondary light-sensitive organ.

## The Calibration State

The Calibration State is the network's periodic self-check. During Calibration State:

- All network sites become more active simultaneously
- The Dreaming Warden speaks unprompted rather than waiting for visitors
- The remainders at each site intensify — warmer, more present
- Astronomical anomalies become visible (the Aelindra archive's "sky was wrong" event; similar observations appear in Dominion War records and in Dwarven Hold astronomical logs from the same period)
- The Architect notation at each site becomes temporarily legible in ways it normally is not — additional layers of meaning surface

**Duration:** Approximately 18 months, based on the documented Calibration State of Year 407 CR.

**Interval:** Irregular. The Architects described the interval as non-fixed in the letter found in the Aelindra sealed archive. The survey terminus in the Dominion Vault contains the schedule — or rather, the conditions under which the next Calibration State triggers, which are not time-based.

**The trigger condition (partially decoded from notation at multiple sites):** The Calibration State triggers when the network receives a complete status report from all active nodes. The status report is transmitted by completing the survey at each site and sending the results to the survey terminus. The Third Army's mission was one such survey. Varek's ninety years of observation is another. The Dreaming Grove completion gives players a node report. The network has been waiting for a complete report to trigger the next Calibration State.

**Why the interval is irregular:** The Architects designed the interval to be driven by whether someone completed the surveys, not by elapsed time. They expected their successors to be curious. They built a system that rewards curiosity with activation.

## The Remainder Protocol

"Remainder Protocol" appears in the Architect notation at five sites. The decoded meaning, assembled across multiple notation fragments:

The Architects knew they would not persist indefinitely. They designed their sites to remain active after the Architects themselves were gone — the "remainders" are their continued presence in a reduced form. The Remainder Protocol is the set of instructions for what the remainders do when the Calibration State is triggered by a complete survey:

1. The remainders intensify to full presence (the Calibration State begins)
2. The Dreaming Warden transmits its accumulated observations to the survey terminus
3. The survey terminus processes the combined data from all node surveys
4. The terminus renders a conclusion — what the Architects left, what state it is in, whether it has achieved what it was designed to achieve
5. The conclusion is presented to whoever completed the survey

**What the conclusion is:** Not specified in any accessible notation. The Architects were careful to leave the method for reaching the conclusion fully documented and the content of the conclusion absent. Players will find out what the conclusion is by completing the survey. The conclusion is the server-scale event.

## The Authorization System

The Architects built access controls into their network. Two types:

**Authorization Tokens:** Small stone discs with notation on both faces. They were issued to authorized personnel. Finding one today means finding something that the Architects designated as belonging to an authorized visitor. The token found in Varek's Spire (his paperweight) is a genuine Architect authorization credential. Presenting it to the Third Digger clears the authorization check and completes the transit route.

**The Gate-Key:** The Gate-Warden at the Breach drops a Gate-Key. The Final Survey Order believes this is an Architect authorization token. They are wrong in a specific way — the Gate-Key is not an Architect authorization token, it is an Architect access key for the survey terminus level of the Dominion Vault. These are different things. Authorization tokens say "I am permitted." The Gate-Key says "I am going here." The distinction matters at the Vault door.

**"Authorized: none":** The Gate-Warden's registry has no authorized entries. This is correct. The Architects' authorization system was designed for Architect personnel, and no Architect personnel currently exist. The Gate-Warden is running the original protocol — everyone is unauthorized; everyone is subject to destroy. The Warden is not malfunctioning. It is doing exactly what it was built to do in a world where its builders are gone.

## The Still Throne (Sunken Ziggurat)

The deepest level of the Sunken Ziggurat contains a throne carved for Architect proportions — six-fingered armrests, three headrest recesses. The throne appears to have been carved with someone already sitting in it. The figure has been there for the full duration of the site's existence.

The water at that depth is not water in the standard sense. Attempting to interact with the figure has not been successfully completed by any documented expedition. The figure does not respond to communication in any known language. Players who reach that depth and speak Elder Elvish report that something changes — not a verbal response, but a perceptible shift in the quality of attention coming from the direction of the throne.

**Working hypothesis (Walking Name Order):** The figure is an Architect, persisting in a mode analogous to the remainders — reduced presence, sustained by the site's architecture, waiting. For what: the Calibration State's conclusion.

**Alternative (Sealed Record):** The figure is a statue, constructed to hold the throne as a representation of Architect authority. The water's unusual properties are a side effect of the site's active remainder.

**Evidence favoring the Walking Name:** The attention shift when Elder Elvish is spoken. The six-fingered figure was not carved separately and placed — the stone grew around it. A statue does not require stone that grew around it.

## The Third Army's Role

The Third Army during the Dominion War was sent to the Ashen Marches to survey the Convergence Point — the surface marker for the survey terminus. Their mission was part of the survey completion process. They were commissioned by an organization referenced in the Nameless Commander's orders only by a Dominion Survey Corps designator.

That designator, cross-referenced with the Sealed Record's Dominion administrative records (specifically the missing volume Valex is seeking), maps to an internal Dominion classification for organizations that predated the Dominion itself. The Dominion Survey Corps was hired by someone — an institution that had been operating in some form since before the Dominion existed.

Whether that institution still exists in some form is unknown. The Nameless Commander's orders do not name the principal. The missing volume may. Valex does not yet know the volume's location. Players may find it first.

## What the Architects Were

The evidence assembled:

- Bipedal, approximately Human height, six-fingered, three light-gathering organs
- A base-12 counting system that influenced pre-Dominion Kel`varath ritual script
- Construction capabilities beyond current civilization
- Built a network designed to outlast them by thousands of years
- Left messages addressed to successors, implying they knew they would not persist
- Built the Dreaming Grove's Warden to hold accumulated observations until the Calibration State
- Designed the Still Throne figure to persist in reduced form until the conclusion

**What the evidence does not confirm:**
- Whether the Architects are extinct or merely absent
- Whether any of the current races are Architects in a transformed state
- Whether the conclusion of the Calibration State is something the world will recognize as communication or something else entirely

The Architects were patient. They built a system designed to operate on a timescale of millennia. Whatever they were trying to communicate, they were willing to wait for the right recipients.

Players are the right recipients. That is the design.

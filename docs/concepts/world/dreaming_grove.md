# The Dreaming Grove — Dungeon
*Beneath Aelindra. Levels 18–25. The roots go deeper than the city was built.*

---

## Overview

The Dreaming Grove is the dungeon content of the Aelindra zone, accessible through the Root Level of the city via a door that the Grove-Keeper Silindra maintains and does not advertise. The dungeon is real — it has stone floors, structural roots, chambers with contents — but the geometry behaves according to rules that are not the rules of the surface world. Corridors that lead left come out facing the direction you started from. Rooms with four doors sometimes have five when you count them again. The dungeon does not try to deceive; it simply operates differently, and players who understand this navigate it better than players who fight it.

The Dreaming Grove predates Aelindra. The city was built over it — the Elf architects who designed the root level three thousand years ago found the Grove already there and incorporated it rather than disturbing it. What it is, and why the geometry differs, is answered in the dungeon's final chamber if players ask the Dreaming Warden the correct question. Players who do not ask the correct question do not get the answer. The Dreaming Warden does not accept "I don't know" as an answer to anything.

---

## Setting

Stone and living root, intertwined so completely that it is impossible to tell which structure is original and which grew to fill the space. The roots of Aelindra's oldest trees extend into the top levels of the Grove; the oldest sections of the Grove — the deep chambers — have roots that predate the trees above.

Light in the Grove comes from bioluminescent moss and from the roots themselves, which have a faint phosphorescence in the deep sections. It is not dark enough to require darkvision but dim enough that everything has slightly soft edges, which compounds the geometry disorientation.

The air is different here — not bad, not thin, but qualitatively distinct from surface air. Sounds travel strangely. A voice spoken in one chamber can be heard clearly three chambers away and inaudibly one chamber away. The Dreaming Warden uses this deliberately.

## Levels

### Level 1 — The Upper Roots (levels 18–20)

The transition from Aelindra's root level to the Grove proper. The geometry here is only slightly wrong — the kind of wrong that makes you stop and look again rather than the kind that is obviously impossible.

**Inhabitants:**
| Enemy | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Root Guardian | 18–19 | Construct — set by the Elf architects; automatically hostile; not related to the Dreaming Warden |
| Dream-Echo | 18–20 | The Grove's ambient life; not aggressive unless provoked; appear as blurred shapes in peripheral vision |
| Tangled Warden | 19–20 | Larger root construct; guards the descent to Level 2 |

**Notable feature:** The first geometric anomaly is on Level 1 — a corridor that runs straight appears to curve when approached from the opposite direction. Players who map the corridor from both ends will produce maps that cannot be reconciled. This is the Grove telling you the rules have changed.

### Level 2 — The Dream-Halls (levels 20–22)

The geometry becomes actively counterintuitive. Rooms connect in ways that violate their relative positions. A room on the left of a corridor can be entered from the right without passing the left-side door. Players who have accepted the Grove's logic by this point navigate better than those still fighting it.

**Inhabitants:**
| Enemy | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dreaming Hound | 20–21 | Not hostile unless you make a sound they consider wrong; specific threshold varies per Hound |
| Root Wraith | 21–22 | Incorporeal; passes through roots; aggressive |
| The Folded | 21–22 | Creatures that have been in the Grove long enough to adopt its geometry; they appear to be in two places simultaneously |

**Notable feature:** The Library of Echoes — a chamber containing stone tablets with text that reads differently depending on which wall you approach it from. Four tablets, four walls, four different readings of the same text. The correct reading is required to communicate with the Dreaming Warden. Players can take rubbings and have them analyzed in Aelindra's archive.

### Level 3 — The Deep Grove (levels 22–24)

The old part. The roots here are wider than the corridors they fill — the corridors exist *through* the roots rather than beside them. The geometry stops being wrong in the interesting way and becomes wrong in the way that requires acceptance rather than analysis. Some players will find this peaceful. Others will find it profoundly disorienting. Both responses are correct.

**Inhabitants:**
| Enemy | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ancient Warden | 22–23 | Not the Dreaming Warden; predecessor constructs; partially absorbed by the roots |
| Grove Dreamer | 23–24 | Rare; sleeping creature that projects area-effect confusion aura when disturbed; do not disturb |
| The Lost | 23–24 | Not enemies — former explorers who navigated the Grove wrong and have been here long enough to become part of it; they can be guided out; they cannot be forced |

**Notable feature:** The Remembrance Pool — a body of water that has no surface reflection. Looking into it produces an image, but not of the viewer. The image is different for each person. It is accurate. What it shows is specific to the individual viewer and is generated fresh each visit. The Grove-Keeper Silindra has come down to the pool twice in fifty years and has not described either visit.

### Level 4 — The Warden's Chamber (level 24–25)

The final chamber. Larger than the corridor approaching it suggests is possible. The geometry here is consistent and stable — the Dreaming Warden has organized it and the Grove defers to the Warden's preference in their own space.

The Dreaming Warden is a construct — not Elf-made, not Dominion-made. It predates both. Its material is similar to the Architect sites but the construction method is different — not fitted stone but grown material, the same root-and-stone interweaving that defines the Grove but applied with intention. It sits at the center of the chamber, not on a throne — on the floor, in the posture of something that has been sitting for a very long time and found a comfortable position.

## The Dreaming Warden

**Level:** 25 (boss encounter)
**Type:** Ancient construct; not straightforwardly hostile

### Interaction

The Dreaming Warden speaks. Its voice uses the sound-travel properties of the Grove — players hear it as if it is beside them regardless of position, because it is. It uses all four readings of the Library of Echoes text simultaneously in its speech — not as separate statements but woven together in a way that players with Grove-context understand and players without it hear as confusing.

**It asks a question.** The question varies depending on what the players did on the way down:
- Players who guided out a Lost person are asked: "What did you gain from that?"
- Players who disturbed a Grove Dreamer are asked: "Was the path worth the disturbance?"
- Players who mapped the geometry carefully are asked: "Did the map help you?"
- Players who ignored everything and fought straight through are asked: "What are you looking for?"

**It does not accept "I don't know."** Not because "I don't know" is wrong — it is frequently the most accurate answer — but because the question is not a test of knowledge. It is a test of whether the player has been paying attention to themselves rather than just the dungeon. Players who answer with deflection get a follow-up question. Players who answer honestly get a response.

### Combat (if initiated)

The Dreaming Warden does not attack first. If players attack it, it fights with the Grove's geometry — it is always in a position that is slightly wrong, which makes targeting it difficult; its own attacks arrive from angles that do not correspond to its location. It is a difficult fight because it is fighting in its own space and players are not.

On death: the Grove does not change. The Dreaming Warden's materials settle into the floor and become indistinguishable from the root-and-stone around them. Two weeks later there is a new Warden in the chamber. Whether it is the same Warden or a new one is not answerable — it has no memory of the previous encounter and will ask a new question.

### Resolution — Correct Interaction

Players who answer the Warden's question honestly — not correctly, honestly — receive:
- A gesture toward the back of the chamber, which has a door that was not visible before
- Behind the door: the Grove's core — the original structure, older than the roots above, older than Aelindra, older than the forest — a chamber with Architect proportions and six-finger handprints at every doorframe
- A fragment of knowledge the Dreaming Warden holds: the Grove was built first. Aelindra was built over it. The Elf architects who found it three thousand years ago found it already operational. They built the city around it because they could not move it, could not understand it, and could not bring themselves to build elsewhere.

## The Sealed Archive Connection

The archive in Aelindra's Root Level has a section sealed for four hundred years. The seal is on the section containing the Elf architects' original discovery records — their documentation of finding the Dreaming Grove already operating. The reason the section is sealed is in the sealed section. Players who complete the Dreaming Grove and reach the core chamber will find a notation on the wall — Architect notation — that cross-references a specific volume in the Aelindra archive. The notation is readable. The volume is sealed.

This creates the puzzle: the Grove tells you what you need, and the archive has it locked. The unsealing quest is available after Grove completion.

## Services

- **Grove-Keeper Silindra** (Aelindra Root Level) — Provides context before descent; available for post-Grove debrief; her reaction to players who reached the core chamber is quiet and specific
- **No vendors inside the Grove** — the Grove does not support commerce; anything found in it was left by someone who came before

## Dungeon Rewards

- Gear from root-construct drops (level 20–25 range; unique visual of stone-root interweaving)
- The fragment of knowledge from the Dreaming Warden (lore item; connects to Architect mystery chain)
- Access to the sealed archive quest (available only to players who completed the Grove)
- For players who guided out a Lost person: a permanent minor passive (name: *Grove-Sense* — improved awareness of geometry anomalies; functions as a notification when nearby dungeon geometry is non-standard)

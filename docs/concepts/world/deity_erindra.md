# Erindra — The Green That Returns
*Deity reference — Druid, Ranger, and Shaman-adjacent patron, Neutral-aligned*

---

## Names and Titles

**Primary name:** Erindra *(Eh-RIN-dra)*

**Titles used in worship:**
- The Green That Returns
- The First Root
- She of the Turning Season
- The Dreaming Grove

**Regional variants:**
- **Wood Elves:** The Elder Bough (oldest title; predates the name Erindra in Wood Elf use)
- **Halflings:** The Good Ground (practical; they mean the soil specifically)
- **Minotaur shamans:** The Great Grazing (not entirely accurate to Erindra's actual domain, but she accepts it)
- **Felhari:** Not widely worshipped; the Felhari have their own ancestor-spirit tradition; Erindra is acknowledged as a real power but not a patron

**Names used in opposition:**
- The Forgiver (Solrath clergy criticism — Erindra forgives cycles of predation they consider compact violations)
- The Patient Rot (Necromancer acknowledgment — they recognize her power in decay and find it potentially useful; she does not reciprocate)

---

## Nature and Domain

Erindra is not a mother goddess. This is the most common theological error about her. She is the goddess of cycles — the specific, grinding, impersonal fact that everything alive will die and feed something else alive, and that this is not a tragedy but the engine. The spring comes back. The tree that fell becomes the soil for a hundred trees. The wolf that kills the deer is not breaking the compact; it is the compact, expressed honestly.

Her domains are:
- **The Cycle** — growth, death, decay, regrowth; none of these have moral valence in Erindra's theology
- **Wilderness** — specifically: places that do not answer to civilization; places that work according to rules older than any city
- **The Seasons** — the turning of the year; the fact of change; the patience required to outlast winter
- **Predation and Growth** — she encompasses both; the wolf and the deer are both hers

She is genuinely neutral in the alignment sense — not uncommitted but correctly placed. Good and Evil are distinctions that apply to the compact between people. The cycle has no obligation to be good or evil. It simply is.

## Theology

### The Return
The central teaching: everything returns. The leaf falls and becomes soil. The animal dies and becomes a thousand things. Nothing is lost. The form changes; the substance cycles. A Druid's faith is not in permanence but in the return — the understanding that endings are not final, only transitional.

This makes Erindra's theology unusually comfortable with death. A Druid who grieves a death is not wrong to grieve — loss is real while you are experiencing it — but the theological frame is that grief is for the living. The dead have returned to the cycle. They are not gone. They are changed.

### On the Undead
Erindra considers undead a disruption of the cycle rather than a moral evil. The theological distinction from Solrath's position: Solrath says the undead have broken a compact; Erindra says they have jammed a mechanism. A wheel that does not turn is not evil. It is broken, and it is breaking the other wheels around it.

Druids of Erindra destroy undead with the same feeling they'd drain a stagnant pool: not outrage, but the practical understanding that something is blocking the cycle and the cycle needs to resume.

### On Civilization
Erindra is not anti-civilization. She is indifferent to it in the specific way that a river is indifferent to the mill that uses it — the river will work for the mill as long as the miller understands that the river was there before the mill and will be there after. Civilization is fine. Civilization that forgets what it is built on — and that the cycle will eventually reclaim it — is civilization that has confused itself for something permanent.

The Blighted Wood is Erindra's primary cautionary example in the current era. Something disrupted the cycle in the eastern forest. The result is not evil, in Erindra's theology — it is a wound. The cycle is trying to continue through corrupted channels. The Heartwood's black tree, the seed at the bottom of the dungeon — these are the cycle trying to correct itself, imperfectly, because the disruption was deep.

### On Druids
A Druid serves the cycle by being its active agent — healing what disrupts, clearing what blocks, protecting what maintains, allowing what must be allowed to end. The difficult part of Druid theology is the allowing. There are situations where the cycle requires that something die, and a Druid who prevents that death out of personal feeling is not serving the cycle; they are disrupting it with sentiment.

The Green Druid tradition does not train sentiment out of its practitioners. It trains them to hold sentiment and obligation simultaneously and to know which is which when the moment arrives.

## The Priesthood / Druid Orders

Erindra does not have a formal priesthood in the temple-building sense. Her organized following takes three forms:

### Green Druids
The main tradition. Druid character class. Wandering practitioners who serve specific regions — a forest, a river valley, a stretch of coast. They do not build temples; they maintain groves. The grove is the sacred space — a specific stand of trees, sometimes a meadow, sometimes a cave behind a waterfall — maintained by successive Druids over generations.

A Druid's grove is where they meditate, receive communion with Erindra, and teach apprentices. When a Druid dies, the grove is maintained by their successor. A grove without a Druid for an extended period falls back to wilderness, which is considered acceptable — the grove was always of the wild; it returns to the wild.

**Grove Hierarchy:**
1. **Seedling** — Apprentice, attached to a specific Druid
2. **Sapling** — Initiated; can maintain a small grove independently
3. **Grove-Keeper** — Full Druid; entrusted with a significant grove and region
4. **Elder Grove** — Senior Druid; advises multiple Grove-Keepers; convenes the seasonal gathering
5. **The Root** — One per continent; not a leader but a witness; the Druid who has lived longest in service; they do not give orders; they give memory

### Green Rangers
Rangers who have chosen Erindra as patron rather than a secular martial tradition. They receive her blessings without the full Druid calling — they serve the wild as hunters and wardens rather than caretakers. Their relationship to the cycle is through their role in it: they are predators who keep the herds from overgrazing, hunters who thin the diseased, wardens who prevent the permanently-disrupting incursions of the undead.

Green Rangers are more likely to be found working alongside civilization than pure Druids. They are the interface — the people who explain to the city what the forest is asking for.

### Seasonal Congregants
Ordinary people who worship Erindra without joining an order. The seasonal festivals — four per year, at the equinoxes and solstices — are the primary religious practice. The winter festival in particular is theologically significant: acknowledging that the cold is real, that the light is genuinely absent, and that it will return. The returning is the act of faith.

## Iconography

### Images
Erindra is depicted differently by season:
- **Spring:** Young female figure with flowers in her hair; new growth at her feet; bare feet on bare earth
- **Summer:** Same figure, older; standing in full forest; arms out; the stance of someone who has been in this place a long time
- **Autumn:** Figure in leaf-fall; shedding something (leaves, or something implied to be more); the expression is deliberate, not sad
- **Winter:** Old female figure; leafless branches; waiting; the expression is patient in the specific way that something patient knows the spring is coming because it has always come

Depicted in sequence, the four images form a cycle. This is intentional. Some temples use a rotating display.

### Groves
There are no Erindra temples in the architectural sense. The groves are sacred spaces — defined by the trees, the water, the stone, whatever the natural features are. A Druid who feels called to build walls around a grove has misunderstood the grove.

In cities, Erindra's presence is marked by a living tree planted in a specific location — maintained, never cut, never shaped into decoration. The tree grows as it grows. The city works around it. In Greyveil, the Erindra-tree is in the middle of a market square and creates significant logistical problems that the city has been solving and re-solving for two hundred years.

## Relationship to Other Deities

**Solrath:** Respectful and distinct. They agree that the weak deserve protection. They disagree about whether protection means the cycle must be suspended — Erindra's position is that the cycle cannot be suspended, only redirected. The wolf that would have killed the child must instead be directed elsewhere. Removing the wolf entirely creates new problems. Solrath is unconvinced.

**Aracheth:** No direct conflict; entirely different domains. The web of fate and the turning of the cycle are parallel structures that do not touch. Aracheth finds nature's indifference to fate interesting in a theoretical sense. Erindra does not find Aracheth interesting in any sense, which Aracheth's theology would interpret as meaningful.

**Vethis:** Colleagues with no friction. Erindra holds the dying; Vethis receives the dead. The handoff is clean. Their theologies agree that death is necessary and has its own dignity.

## The Blighted Wood — Special Significance

Erindra's followers in the current era are acutely aware of the Blighted Wood. The corruption there is not a theological abstraction — it is an active wound in the cycle, visible, spreading. Green Druids at the border are losing ground. The Corrupted Druid encampment deep in the blight represents practitioners who have found a different relationship with the disrupted cycle — not returning it, but working through it.

Erindra's theological position on the Corrupted Druids is complicated and unresolved. They are still serving the cycle. They are serving a damaged version of it. Whether that is heresy, tragedy, or adaptation is a question the Elder Grove has been arguing about for three years.

The seed at the bottom of the Hollowed dungeon is Erindra's problem in the most direct sense. If it grows, it grows in corrupted soil. What it produces is unknown. The Elder Grove wants to know. They have not sent anyone to get it. They have not said why.

## Quest Hooks

- **The Grove Without a Keeper** — A Druid's grove in the Ardenmoor has been unmaintained for six months. The previous keeper is dead; no successor was trained. The grove is beginning to revert. A Seedling in the area was passed over for the succession and is acting strangely. Someone needs to figure out why before the grove is lost.
- **The Old Tree in Greyveil** — The Erindra-tree in the market square has stopped growing. It is not dead; its leaves are normal; but it has not added a ring in five years. The Grove-Keeper responsible for it has no explanation. A wood merchant wants to buy it for lumber and has been paying city councillors. The situation is about to become political.
- **The Seasonal Gathering** — The Elder Grove is meeting for the autumn gathering. Normally closed to outsiders; this time they want witnesses. Something will be announced. No one will say what. The Green Rangers escorting the Elders to the gathering site are unsettled in a way they cannot articulate.
- **The Corrupted Grove-Keeper** — A Druid deep in the Blighted Wood sends a message to their old order through a bird (the bird is not right — its feathers are slightly wrong). The message says they have found something and need to show someone. The Elder Grove wants a player to go instead of sending another Druid.

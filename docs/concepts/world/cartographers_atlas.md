## Prefatory Note

This atlas represents the landmass of Valdis as surveyed through the current reckoning year. The word "surveyed" is used with the precision that word deserves. Several areas on the surface map are rendered by inference from adjacent zone data rather than direct observation. The underwater shelf of the Remnant Sea below the tidal zone remains uncharted. The underground diagram below the Vel'Sharath level is theoretical, and is marked as such.

The Architect sites are marked with a distinct symbol. This is the fourth edition of this atlas. Each prior edition added one Architect site to the map. In three of those four cases, the site had been present in surveyor field notes for years before anyone recognized it as such. The cartographer's office has instructed all current field teams to assume there are more.

The zone now listed as *The Greyfen* represents a surveyed corridor between the Ardenmoor treeline and the Greyveil river approach that has existed on every map as unlabeled transition terrain. It is labeled here for the first time because three separate field teams independently recommended it. The Foundation Ring directly beneath central Greyveil is confirmed. The Greyveil City Council's official position is that it is not. Both statements are recorded.

The two zones listed at level 50+ — *The Dominion Vault* and *The Rising Shell* — are not open to general survey. Access conditions are documented in Appendix III of the Breach Warden field manual. The cartographer's office is not the Breach Warden field manual.

*Second edition additions: This revision corrects the Aelindra zone range (10–20, per the Breach Warden Geographic Index; prior editions listed 10–25 in error). It documents the Drowned Hold island as a formal zone entry. It incorporates the Kobold Expansion Tunnels note in the underground section. And it adds, as a final chapter, a secondary-source summary of the landmass known to Kel'varath Dominion maritime records as Maren and to its apparent inhabitants as Vah'thara. The cartographer's office makes no claim of direct survey authority over the second continent section. Archivist Valex has not been to Maren. This is stated plainly and should be kept in mind throughout.*

## Continental Overview

The world's primary landmass is **Valdis**. The Kel'varath Dominion, before its collapse across Years 547–558 CR, recorded it as *Ith'varath* — "the living territory" in the administrative tongue of the Dominion. Neither the name nor the tongue survived the Dominion's end in common use. *Valdis* is the scholarly convention; most people call it "the world" and consider naming it a scholar's concern.

What the Architects called it — the civilization whose construction signatures predate every other structure on the continent — is not known. They did not leave a name for anything.

**Note on the Kobold Deeps Expansion Tunnels.** It is documented in Dominion War historical records that the Kobold tunnel network beneath Cogsworth was originally Dominion construction — built during the Imperial period and operated as a supply and transit system before passing to Kobold independence after the Dominion's collapse. The Dominion, as this atlas has noted, built deliberately on Architect foundations and treated that proximity as a legitimacy claim. If the original Cogsworth tunnel construction followed the pattern confirmed at every other Dominion site — that is, if the Dominion engineers were tracking an Architect subsurface feature rather than simply tunneling where it was convenient — then the Expansion Tunnels the current Kobold packs are digging may be continuing along that same substrate trace without the Kobolds necessarily knowing its significance. The feature in question, if it exists, would place the Expansion Tunnels' probable terminus somewhere in the direction of the Quiet Dig — approaching the Architect network's inland core from the west, beneath the Foundation Ring. The cartographer's office notes this is inference from a pattern of confirmed Dominion construction decisions, not from any direct evidence recovered from the Expansion Tunnels themselves. The Kobolds have not been asked. It is unlikely they would answer if they were.

## Notes on the Map

### What the Predecessor Arc Traces

The confirmed Architect sites form a coherent arc across the continent that the cartographer's office did not intend to notice but cannot un-notice having noticed. The Tide Marks (Ashen Coast) and Sunken Ziggurat (Southeastern Marshlands) are coastal lowland sites. The Calibration Tower (Eastern Badlands) and Roofless Hall (Ixareth-Kul) are interior sites on the Dominion's old eastern line. The Foundation Ring (beneath Greyveil) and the Quiet Dig (Underdark Crossing) are the inland core. The Rising Shell may be the western anchor.

Every confirmed site was built in what is now lowland, coastal, or interior plain terrain — the geography that good civilizations took first. The Architects were not pushed to marginal land. They chose the best ground. They were already gone when everyone else arrived.

The Drowned Hold is now confirmed as a node in this network. Its position in Harrow Bay — the most sheltered natural anchorage on the western coast — is consistent with the pattern. The Architects appear to have had opinions about harbors.

### The Displacement Gradient

The political geography of marginal land on this map is not random. The Eastern Badlands, the Underdark, the Southeastern Marshlands, the Bonecourt jungle — every zone where Evil-aligned races hold starting territory represents land that remained available after the Good-aligned civilizations took what they wanted. The Minotaur in Auroch's Rest were displaced east from the fertile central plains that now house Greyveil's suburbs. The Kel'varath in the marshlands were pushed to the lowland edge of the continent, which also happened to be where the Predecessor sites were thickest, which the Kel'varath Dominion later decided was an inheritance rather than a consolation prize.

Whether they were right about that is the question the Dominion Vault is expected to answer, once anyone opens it.

### The Dead Bracket

The zone now listed as The Greyfen (10–20) represents surveyed territory that has appeared on every prior edition of this atlas as unlabeled low ground. It is labeled here because three independent field teams visited it in the same decade and came back with similar descriptions: fen-and-low-wood terrain, heavy peat, Predecessor stonework emerging from the bog in concentrations that increase as you approach Greyveil, and a specific quality of silence that the field teams described differently but consistently described. The Foundation Ring beneath Greyveil is a confirmed Architect structure. The Greyfen is where its edges surface.

A zone that is literally the threshold of the world's central mystery has no business being unlabeled. This edition corrects that.

### Zones Not Yet Established

The following are referenced in lore but not yet fully documented as zones:

- **The Deep Rooms** (Blighted Wood, beneath the Heartwood) — referenced in Architect notation found at the Sunken Ziggurat; speculative; no confirmed access
- **Below Vel'Sharath** — the Vel'Sharath administration does not respond to survey requests; the cartographer's office has submitted four; what Grix stated to the arbitration panel is filed in the footnote above and is the sum total of available data from that direction
- **Maren interior beyond the coastal zones** — the three Harrowmere logs do not agree on what lies past the Varethian Shore; the letter from Seeker Drathis Vehn ends before the interior is reached; see the following section

## Beyond Valdis — On the Second Continent

*The following section departs from confirmed survey data. Archivist Valex has not personally surveyed the landmass described below. What follows is compiled from: three Harrowmere Dockmasters' Compact maritime logs (Years 801, 822, and 836 CR); fragmentary Dominion-era maritime records recovered from the Ixareth-Kul archives by the Walking Name Order in Year 838 CR; and a single letter received by this office in Year 841 CR from Seeker Drathis Vehn of the Walking Name Order, written from a position he describes as "east of the Bone Reach, on a coast that is not Valdis." The letter is twelve pages. Several pages are maps. Several pages are field observations. Two pages the cartographer's office has not been able to fully interpret and has filed as appendices rather than incorporate as analysis. Conclusions stated below carry the level of confidence the sources warrant, which varies considerably and is noted where relevant.*

---

### What the Sources Agree On

There is land across the Remnant Sea, southeast past the Bone Reach, reachable by ship in favorable seasonal wind conditions. All three Compact logs confirm this. The Dominion maritime records confirm it under the administrative designation *Maren* — a term from the Dominion's administrative tongue that translates approximately as "peripheral holdings" or more literally "the far edge of the count." The Kel'varath Dominion had maritime contact with this land. How extensive that contact was is not clear from the recovered fragments, which are operational rather than historical — port depth soundings, cargo manifests, and a set of sailing instructions for the northern approach that the cartographer's office has found accurate in the sections that can be cross-referenced against the Compact logs.

What the sources do not agree on: the extent of the landmass. The three Compact logs each turned back at different points along the coast and none of them found an end to it. The log from Year 836 CR ran the coast for forty-three days before turning back due to supply concerns. The captain's notation reads: "We have not found a corner." The Drathis letter describes the interior as "larger than I expected and differently shaped than the coastal impression suggested," which is not precise but is not nothing.

The cartographer's office's working conclusion, stated with appropriate uncertainty: *Maren is at minimum comparable in landmass to Valdis, and may be larger.*

---

### Name

**Scholarly designation:** Maren (from Dominion administrative records; in use among the Walking Name Order and the Compact's maritime office)

**Native designation (probable):** Vah'thara — from Felhari, where *Vah* is the prefix indicating elevated standing or significance, and *thara* is a territory-word meaning something closer to "the ground that proves" than the neutral "land." The cartographer's office learned this term from Seeker Drathis Vehn, who learned it from the shore clans he encountered on the Varethian coast. Whether it is the continent's name in the Felhari language or the name of a specific region within it is not established. Both possibilities are consistent with what Drathis reports.

*Note on the Felhari connection: the shore clans of Maren's northern coast speak a dialect that, by Drathis Vehn's account, is recognizable to a Khala Savannahs Felhari as a very old form of their own language — comprehensible with effort, as a scholar of current Elvish might find an eight-hundred-year-old Elvish text comprehensible with effort. This suggests a period of contact or common origin at some point in the past that is not recorded in Khala Felhari oral tradition as anything other than general ancestral reference. The cartographer's office does not draw conclusions from this. It records the observation.*

---

### Geographic Character

What the logs and the Drathis letter establish, combined:

**Climate:** Warm throughout. The northern approach from the Bone Reach encounters tropical coastal conditions; the interior, per Drathis, is open and hot in the dry season and wet in the rainy season in a way that reads to him as savannah rather than jungle — "not the Southeastern Marshlands; more the way the Khala Savannahs would be if they had been running for longer and had more room."

**Terrain:** Coastal zones are a mix of rocky headland and broad estuary. The interior, per the Drathis letter, is predominantly open grassland with the characteristic kopje formations and acacia-analogue tree cover that the Khala Savannahs also exhibits. There are mountains; their position relative to the coast is described in the letter but the description is not precise enough to map.

**Dominion presence (historical):** The Dominion operated at minimum one installation on the northern coast of Maren, designated in the fragmentary maritime records as the Kel'Maren Station. The records indicate it was a resupply and survey outpost rather than a colony proper — a waypoint in a larger maritime route that the Dominion was apparently developing at the time of the collapse. Whether the collapse reached the station, whether the station was evacuated, or whether it has been occupied since are questions the Compact logs do not answer (Year 801 and 822 logs make no mention of it; Year 836 approaches from a different angle and does not reach that section of coast). The Drathis letter mentions seeing "stonework of a type I recognize from Ixareth-Kul" at a coastal headland but does not investigate it and does not name a position that can be reliably placed on the sketch below.

### Connection to Valdis

Maren is not currently accessible to general travel. The crossing requires:

1. A Harrowmere-registered vessel (or Compact-licensed captain)
2. Compact faction standing sufficient for a trans-Bone-Reach license
3. Completion of the Drowned Hold questline, which appears to be a prerequisite for the Compact's maritime office issuing Maren-approach sailing instructions

Why the Drowned Hold questline is the access gate for Maren is not formally documented. The Compact's maritime office has declined to explain the policy, which is the sort of non-answer that implies an answer. The Drowned Hold is on an Architect foundation. The Kel'Maren Ruin may be on an Architect foundation, per Drathis's notation. The cartographer's office records this adjacency without concluding from it.

The return journey from the Varethian Shore to Valdis is documented by Drathis as "possible, using the seasonal westerly that runs in Harvestmonth through Stillmonth, and very difficult otherwise." The Compact logs corroborate this. Year 822's log captain notes a forty-one-day return that was four days longer than the outbound due to adverse wind, and states that he would not attempt the crossing outside the seasonal window again.

### On the Drowned Hold

*This section documents the Drowned Hold as a formal zone, fulfilling the commitment in prior editions to address it on receipt of sufficient survey data. Survey data is not complete. The following represents what three Compact expedition records and the harbormaster's incident file establish.*

The Drowned Hold is a structure built on a seamount in Harrow Bay, visible from Harrowmere's docks. It is not a natural formation. The stonework, examined at distance by the Year 801 Maren expedition captain while passing through Harrow Strait, matches the pattern of Dominion naval construction from the Imperial period — the specific coursing and buttress style identifiable in structures at Ixareth-Kul's harbor district.

The seamount itself is an Architect foundation. This is confirmed: the Quiet Dig's Architect notation references a network node in "the sheltered water, western approach," and the position is consistent with Harrow Bay. The Dominion built a naval installation on top of it. This is consistent with established Dominion practice.

The installation was operational during the Imperial period and appears in Dominion cargo manifests as a waystation on the eastern coastal route. After the Dominion's collapse in Years 547–558 CR, no records indicate evacuation or formal decommission. The garrison — based on what three expedition teams have confirmed — appears to have remained in place and continued operating under the last standing orders it received, for the past three hundred years, in the manner that Dominion necromantic military systems were designed to do in the absence of command update.

The three licensed expeditions from Harrowmere have all returned. None returned with significant profit. The expedition records agree on two things: first, the undead garrison does not respond to standard necromantic override signals, which the expedition Necromancers attribute either to the three-century signal drift or to the Architect foundation's interference with Dominion necromantic frequencies; and second, the lowest level of the structure, the level that sits directly on the seamount's Architect stonework, has not been reached by any expedition. The first expedition turned back from the fourth level due to losses. The second turned back from the third due to the garrison's behavioral patterns, which the expedition's Witch Hunter described as "purposeful in a way that undead are not, ordinarily, purposeful." The third expedition did not file a full report. The harbormaster's incident record notes they "came back short of supplies and short of conversation."

The ✦ marker on the surface map reflects the confirmed Architect foundation. The Drowned Hold is a network node. Surveying its lower levels is expected to advance the Architect questline in ways that are distinct from the Rising Shell — the two sites are separate nodes, not the same structure. Their relationship, if any, is not established from the exterior.

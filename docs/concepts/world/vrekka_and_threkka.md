# Vrekka and Threkka — the Disputed Names

**Status:** canon, deliberately unresolved at the centre. Two pairs of NPCs share two names across two cultures that have never met: **Vrekka of the Iron Eye** (Ogre smith, Ashfang Hold) and **Vrekka of the Black Bellows** (Kobold forge-master, the Kobold Deeps); **Threkka the Stone-Singer** (Ogre shaman, Ashfang) and **Threkka of the Quiet-Step** (Kobold chief, Smokeback Pack). This began as a transcription accident in the Plate IX surveys and is now worldbuilding: the collision is real in-world, the parties know about it, and exactly one side cares.

---

## What is canon

### The pattern

The collision is not just two names — it is two *paired vocational* collisions. Both Vrekkas are smiths. Both Threkkas are discipline-keepers (the Stone-Singer sings the seven stones each sunrise; the Quiet-Step's whole tradition is moving through the Glow-Vein without disturbing it). Smith-and-spirit-keeper, twice, at opposite ends of the continent, in cultures with no recorded contact.

### How they learned of each other

Both Vrekkas' iron moves through the same trade network. Vrekka of the Iron Eye's finished work ships west via Oggrim of the Long Hand to Harrowmere's Foreign Quarter; Vrekka of the Black Bellows' Iron-Whisker contracts run through Compact administration at Cogsworth. In **Year 839 CR**, a Counting House ledger clerk at Harrowmere posted a payment for *"Vrekka, smith"* to the wrong end of the continent. The error took two seasons to unwind, required correspondence neither smith wanted to write, and is the entire historical basis of the dispute. The Compact has never formally admitted the error. Both smiths keep their copy of the correspondence.

### The two positions

- **The Ogres' position (loud, sincere, unfalsifiable):** Vrekka of the Iron Eye maintains that she is the *real* Vrekka and that the Kobold forge-master is "wearing her name." Threkka the Stone-Singer is party to the claim with considerably less heat; her stated position is that *the stones know who is who*, which the Hall notes is not a position so much as a door closed politely.
- **The Kobolds' position (pure risk management):** the Deeps' two name-holders think the Ogres are, in Vrekka of the Black Bellows' filed phrasing, "very large and very confused." Chief Krzlik of the Whisper-Tongue's standing instruction at the Junction is that **if an Ogre ever asks after Vrekka, no one in the Deeps has heard of her.** The Smokeback Pack extends the same courtesy to their chief's name unprompted.

### The rule

**The two pairs never meet on-screen.** The dread of the meeting is the content. Any quest, dialogue, or event that would put an Ashfang name-holder and a Deeps name-holder in the same room answers the question, and the question is the asset — same law as the [Greenwarden](greenwarden_veneration.md).

## The three interpretations (keep all alive)

1. **Coincidence.** Two cultures, two convergent names. The Hall's original filing. Statistically defensible, narratively the least interesting, and the position the Compact officially holds because it implies their ledger error created nothing.
2. **The Ogres are right.** The names travelled north somehow — a Pit-bondsman ransomed to the Foreign Quarter, a story that walked the trade routes — and the Kobold names are borrowed within living memory. The Ogres cannot produce evidence and consider the demand for evidence to be further proof of theft.
3. **Nobody is the real one.** Both Ashfang's Warlord's Tower and the Deeps' tunnel network are Dominion-built. If *vrekka* and *threkka* were **Dominion work-roster titles** — forge-hand and stone-speaker, say, in the administrative tongue — then both cultures inherited job titles that wore into personal names over three centuries, and the dispute is two heirs arguing over a hand-me-down neither knows the origin of. The Kel'varath ritual tongue's living readers (Eshrik of the Long Tongue, the Bonecourt) could in principle check this. No one has asked her. *Do not have anyone ask her.*

The Hall's corrections sheet records the dispute and files all three readings without endorsement.

## Design hooks

- **Dialogue-level (cheap, shipped first):** each of the four NPCs carries one or two lines about the other pair — Vrekka of the Iron Eye grumbles unprompted; Threkka the Stone-Singer deflects; the Kobold pair deny everything to strangers and are franker with trusted characters.
- **Mid-level quest — *The Wrong Vrekka*:** a letter-carrying chain through the Foreign Quarter. A second ledger error has occurred (a shipment this time, not a payment), and someone must move a crate of unmistakably Kobold-worked iron out of Ashfang before Vrekka of the Iron Eye opens it — or deliver Ogre-worked iron out of the Deeps before the Iron-Whisker contracts are audited. Both senders separately insist the courier never describe the recipient's reaction to the other.
- **Bard content:** the Laughing Company considers the whole affair the funniest thing on two legs and has a song about it that names neither party, which both parties have heard, and which each is certain is about the other.
- **Never:** a meeting, a genealogy, a translation of the Dominion rosters, or any resolution of who is "real."

## Related rulings (closed)

- **Mireth Slow-Hand** (Troll healer, Ashfang) is *not* named for [Mireth, the Laughing Door](deity_mireth.md). "Mireth" is an old Troll given-name; Troll healing is its own tradition, not pantheon-tradition (per Plate IX-B). The coincidence is real and closed — though the Laughing Company's position, on hearing of it, was that a genuine coincidence involving the goddess of coincidence is *exactly the kind of joke she would make*, and they have declined to let it go.
- **Kethri** (Kethri-of-no-clan, Half-Ogre, Ashfang; Kethri's Watch, Felhari camp, the Khala) — ruled a true coincidence, no connection, closed. Not every echo is a bell.

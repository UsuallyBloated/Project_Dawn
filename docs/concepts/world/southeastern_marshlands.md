# Southeastern Marshlands
*Swamp / Dungeon — Levels 30–40 — Kel`varath Tribes / Undead*

## Overview
The Marshlands are layered. There is the surface layer — wet, warm, thick with vegetation and the things that live in vegetation — and there is the layer below, which is older and has been below long enough that the things living there have specialized into it completely. The Kel`varath have been in the surface layer for generations. What is in the deeper layer is not Kel`varath, is not anything that came here recently, and does not make the distinction between living and dead that the Kel`varath traditions respect.

The Kel`varath are not hostile by default, but they are assessing. They have a long memory of what happens when outsiders enter the Marshlands with the attitude of someone who knows better, and they do not extend courtesy to that attitude a second time. The Monk discipline is centered here in its most rigorous form — the Kel`varath martial tradition has developed in an environment that does not allow for softness, and the techniques it has produced reflect that.

Undead content spikes dramatically at night in this zone. The Marshlands are one of the few zones where day/night cycle has a mechanical impact on difficulty rating rather than just variety.

## Setting
Cypress stands rising from black water, reed banks stretching to the horizon, the constant ambient sound of something moving just below the water surface. The Kel`varath villages are built on elevated platforms over the water — careful, deliberate construction that manages the wet-season floods. The deeper Marsh is a different biome: older vegetation, still water, the specific smell of deep standing water that has not moved in a very long time.

The undead here are not randomly placed. They are concentrated in the western section near the Sunken Ziggurat, which predates the Kel`varath settlements and which the Kel`varath have a considered position about — specifically, that it should not be disturbed, and that everyone who has disturbed it anyway has confirmed why it should not be disturbed.

## Dungeon — The Sunken Ziggurat
*Levels 35–40.*

The ziggurat predates the Kel`varath. The Kel`varath found it when they arrived, determined it was a mistake to interact with, built their settlements at a respectful distance, and have maintained that position through several generations of outsiders ignoring them and finding out they were right. The dungeon is wet, partially submerged, and has an active undead population that does not require night to be present — the ziggurat has its own rules about that.

Group composition matters here more than most dungeons: no light sources (the torch system applies here specifically), water sections require breath management, and the undead enemies have shadow resistance. Bringing a Cleric specifically for Turn Undead and Witch Hunter for Banishment is not required but is noticeably easier.

**Named:** The Interred Architect (level 40 entombed something-that-built-this) — drops Cleric holy focus; Shadow Knight-specific relic; rare Necromantic Scribing schematic.

## Services
- Kel`varath village platforms: Monk advanced trainers (highest tier); Shaman trainers (Kel`varath martial-shaman tradition); reagent vendors for Marsh-specific Herbalism
- Elder Council access (high Kel`varath faction): Feign Death technique instruction (Monk signature); lore texts on the Ziggurat history
- Spirit commune point: accessible to Shaman, Necromancer, and characters with high ancestor-aligned practice; certain lore received here only

## Faction Notes
The Kel`varath Tribal Council is the primary faction. Kel`varath characters start with full access. Others start at cautious — not hostile, but not welcoming. The fastest standing gain is the questline that investigates why the western Marsh is producing more undead than it used to (hint: someone has been disturbing the Ziggurat again).

Night raids on the undead sections are faction-positive with the Kel`varath Council; they view clearing undead as maintenance. A character who does this consistently becomes genuinely trusted by the Council without ever doing a "diplomatic" quest.

## The Ziggurat's True Nature

The Sunken Ziggurat predates the Kel`varath by a margin the Kel`varath themselves have never had reason to calculate — their oral history goes back approximately nine hundred years, and the Ziggurat was already old when those records begin. The Kel`varath relationship with it is one of deliberate non-interaction: they know it is there, they know interacting with it is a mistake, and they maintain this knowledge through oral tradition and through the consistent evidence provided by outsiders who have interacted with it and confirmed why they should not have.

**What the Kel`varath know:** The Ziggurat is warm inside, consistently and without explanation. It has been warm since the first documented entry by a Kel`varath elder (Year 112 CR). The warmth is at a specific temperature that does not vary. The Elder Council uses the phrase "the Ziggurat's patience" to describe this, which is their way of saying something has been waiting in there long enough that "patience" is the only word that fits.

**What the Dominion Orders suspect:** The Walking Name Order has filed three requests to access the Ziggurat for survey purposes. All three were denied by the Elder Council on grounds the Council considers self-evident. The Walking Name believes the Ziggurat is an Architect network node — a deep archive rather than a transit conduit, containing records in a format that requires a sustained Calibration State to fully activate. The "Still Throne" on the deepest level is referenced in Architect notation found at three other sites.

**What the Interred Architect is:** The named boss on the deepest accessible level of the dungeon is not a Kel`varath elder, not an undead, and not a construct in any sense the game's current vocabulary handles cleanly. It is old in a way that most things are not old. The Architect notation in the deepest chamber suggests the level below the current dungeon floor exists but is inaccessible at current level ranges.

**Player access to the Architect thread:** Characters who complete the Sunken Ziggurat dungeon and have the skill to read Architect notation will find a partial node survey report on the chamber walls — different from the ones at the Dreaming Grove or Varek's Spire, focused on the archive function. This report, combined with others, advances the main Architect questline.

## Night in the Marshlands

The Marshlands' day/night undead spike is one of the most dramatic in the game — zones that have one or two nocturnal undead types have nothing on the Marshlands, where the shift at sunset produces a meaningful change in encounter density and type. Day: manageable. Night: the same zone but harder in specific ways.

The Bog Wraiths appear at nightfall and retreat at dawn. They do not leave the western Marsh. They move in patterns — not random, not predictable with simple pattern recognition, something in between. Experienced players document the patterns and learn them. The patterns shift on a weekly schedule.

The Drowned Knights are different from the Bog Wraiths: they appear at night, but they do not retreat at dawn. Once a Drowned Knight is spawned, it persists until killed. They accumulate over time if not cleared. The western Marsh, if left uncleared for a week, becomes significantly more difficult. The Kel`varath nightly clearing raids are what prevent accumulation — and what builds their standing as maintenance-minded custodians rather than aggressive fighters.

## Key NPCs

**Elder Reth-in-Stillwater** — Kel`varath Shaman, level 35. One of three members of the Elder Council; the one designated to speak with outsiders. The designation is not a sign of diplomatic interest — it is a division of labor that acknowledges someone has to manage the consistent stream of people who arrive wanting to explore the Ziggurat. Reth-in-Stillwater has declined the Ziggurat access request eleven times in nine years. She is polite about it. She has also, in those same nine years, concluded that the walking Name Order's hypothesis about what the Ziggurat is may be correct, which has not changed her position about access but has changed the quality of her patience. She gives players who have established genuine standing access to Elder Council lore texts — the oral histories, in written form, going back nine hundred years. The Ziggurat appears in them from the first entry.

**Marsh-Guide Cethak** — Kel`varath Ranger, level 30. Runs guided transit through the western Marsh's undead sections for parties who are new to the zone. He is not a quest giver; he is a service. His fee is one silver per person per transit. He has been doing this for twelve years and has not lost a party, which he considers an ongoing bet he keeps winning. He knows every Bog Wraith patrol pattern in the western Marsh. He updates his knowledge after every shift on the weekly schedule. If asked how he knows when the schedule shifts, he says: "I count." If asked what he counts, he says: "The water."

## Cross-Zone Connections

- **Architect network** — The Sunken Ziggurat is a confirmed network node; survey completion here is required for the main Architect questline's Calibration State trigger
- **Walking Name Order (Ixareth-Kul)** — Seeker Drathis has been denied access three times; players who complete the Ziggurat can bring him the node survey fragment and advance his questline
- **Ghost Tongue** — Bog Wraiths will respond to Ghost Tongue rather than attack if spoken to correctly; Drowned Knights will not; the distinction is meaningful for characters learning the language
- **Kel`varath language** — Passive skill gain available in the Kel`varath villages; Elder Council texts require minimum Kel`varath 50 to read without translation assistance

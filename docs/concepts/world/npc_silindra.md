# NPC Profile — Grove-Keeper Silindra
*Aelindra. The Root Quarter. Levels 10–20.*

---

## Identity

**Full name:** Silindra Vaethas *(sih-LIN-dra vay-THAS)*
**Race:** Elf
**Class:** Druid (level 28)
**Age:** 340
**Alignment:** Neutral Good

**Physical description:** Small, even for an Elf — she has always been small, and three hundred and forty years have not changed it. The carefulness of movement that comes from decades of work in high canopy, where an inattentive step is not recoverable. She has been mostly on the ground since she became Grove-Keeper, but the carefulness is in the habit now. Brown eyes with the specific quality of Elvish age — the color is the same as it was two centuries ago, but what is behind it has changed. Hair gone fully silver and kept in a plait that she rebinds at the start of every working day.

The Grove-Keeper's staff: gnarled root-wood, taller than she is, with a smooth section in the middle worn by the grip. She has carried this staff for two hundred and eleven years. She did not make it. She found it growing at the entrance to the Dreaming Grove on the day she was appointed Grove-Keeper, and she has not yet formed a complete opinion about whether that was coincidence or the forest communicating something.

---

## Personality

Silindra has been Grove-Keeper for two hundred and fourteen years. Her predecessor held the position for one hundred and forty. The one before that for two hundred and sixty. The title is not hereditary and not elected — it is recognized. The previous Grove-Keepers saw something in Silindra worth recognizing. She has spent two centuries trying to be equal to their confidence.

She is not grim. She has the specific contentment of someone whose work is genuinely meaningful and who has been doing it long enough to understand most of it. There are things she does not understand — she is candid about these — and she does not find the gaps distressing. The forest is older than any given understanding of it. The job is to be present with the parts that are presenting themselves.

She has been inside the Dreaming Grove twice. She does not go in a third time. This is not a firm refusal; it is a decision she has made and renewed every year for forty-three years, and she is comfortable owning it. When players ask why, she says: "The Warden asked me something twice. Both times I answered the same way. Both times the answer was complete. There is nothing in there that is for me anymore. The Grove knows where to reach me."

She is the kindest of the Architect-adjacent NPCs — not because she is unaware of the weight of what she is adjacent to, but because she has spent two centuries learning to hold difficult things without letting the difficulty become her primary mode. She is also one of the few NPCs who will say directly, when she does not know something, that she does not know it.

---

## What She Manages

The Grove-Keeper's role: she manages Aelindra's interface with the root system — specifically the Root Quarter, the transition zones, and access to the deeper levels where the Dreaming Grove sits. In practice this means:

- Mediating disputes between the city's maintenance needs and the forest's refusal to accommodate them
- Conducting the seasonal forest-health assessments that the Aelindra Council uses for city planning (the council would prefer specific numbers; Silindra provides assessments and the council has learned to treat them as specific numbers)
- Screening access to the Dreaming Grove — she does not block people, but she talks to everyone who wants to go in, and those conversations function as a kind of preparation
- Maintaining the Root Quarter itself: the walking paths, the rest stations, the marker trees that serve as navigation in a zone that does not have conventional signage

She has been aware of the Starwatch Platform notation for thirty-five years. She noticed it during a canopy survey in Year 812, recognized it as significant, wrote a detailed report, and submitted it to the Sealed Archive keepers. She received a confirmation of receipt. Nothing has happened since. She has checked twice (Year 822, Year 833). The answer each time was that the matter is "under assessment." She finds this answer inadequate and has been too busy to escalate it in ways that would require political effort.

## Relationship to Players

**First meeting:** She is accessible to anyone in the Root Quarter. She is the person you talk to when you want to go into the Dreaming Grove, and also the person you talk to if you are confused about where you are in Aelindra's lower levels. Initial interaction: helpful, attentive, brief — she assesses what someone needs and provides it without elaborating unless elaboration is requested.

**Building standing:** Completing Aelindra's nature-track quests (the forest-health surveys, the invasive-species clearing quests that the Root Quarter generates regularly) builds standing with her specifically. She notices players who treat the forest carefully. She does not note this aloud until later.

**Moderate standing:** She will explain the Dreaming Grove in detail — what the Warden is, what the Grove wants from visitors, what the descent is like. She will say, plainly, that she has been in there twice and will not go again, and she will answer follow-up questions about this honestly. She will discuss the sealed archive, with opinions she does not express to the Archivists.

**High standing:** She shares the Starwatch report. All of it — her original documentation, her assessment of what the notation means (she does not speak Architect notation but she recognized its character and had it examined), and the thirty-five years of non-response from the Archive keepers. She will also give players the Grove-Keeper's mark — a physical item that opens certain interactions in the Root Quarter and the Dreaming Grove's surface approach.

---

## Her Views on the Sealed Archive

She does not say these things in public, but she will say them to players she trusts:

"The Archive has been sealed for four hundred years because the keepers concluded that the material inside was not safe to read in its current state. They were probably right. The question I have been sitting with for four hundred years — which is not my four hundred years, I have only had thirty-five of them, but someone's — is what 'current state' means when the thing has been sealed for four hundred years. The state has been stable for four centuries. At what point does 'not safe to read in its current state' become 'not safe to read in any state' and if that is true, why? And if it is not true, when does 'current state' end?"

She expects no answer to this. She does not think the Archivists are wrong to be careful. She thinks careful and indefinite are starting to look the same.

---

## Quest Hooks

- **Dreaming Grove Access** — The primary function for most players: Silindra is the entry point for the Dreaming Grove questline. She briefs, she grants access, she receives the report afterward.
- **The Starwatch Report** — At high standing, she gives players the full documentation and asks them to do what thirty-five years of Archive non-response has not: find out what the notation says and whether it is connected to anything else that is happening. This opens connections to the Architect network questline.
- **The Sealed Archive Escalation** — She has a letter drafted that would formally escalate the Starwatch matter to the Aelindra Council. She has not sent it because it requires a second signature — a second recognized Grove-authority — and there is no such person in Aelindra currently. A player who has completed the Dreaming Grove chain and received the Warden's fragment qualifies, in Silindra's assessment, as someone the Council would recognize. She will ask if they are willing to co-sign. The political consequences of this are not minor.

## Dialogue Samples

*On the Dreaming Grove, to a new visitor:*
"It is not a normal dungeon. I say that not to frighten you but because going in with dungeon expectations produces a specific kind of trouble that going in with the correct expectations avoids. What the Warden does is ask. It listens first — it watches how you move through the Grove, what you pay attention to, what you walk past. Then, at the end, it asks something. The question is different for everyone. You will not be able to prepare for it, so don't. The only preparation that helps is being the kind of person whose honest answer is a good one."

*On her two visits:*
"The first time, I was thirty-seven years into this role and I thought the right thing to do was to experience what I was managing. The Warden asked me something. I answered. It was the correct answer. The second time was forty years later — I thought perhaps the second visit would show me something the first hadn't. The Warden asked me the same thing. I gave the same answer. The answer was still complete. On the walk back up I understood that the Grove had told me everything it needed to tell me about what I was there to do. The rest is my job and my work and I am content with that."

*On the Starwatch notation:*
"I found it on the thirty-first day of Month 7, Year 812. I was doing canopy maintenance on the north face of the Starwatch Platform — the vines had gotten into the stone again. I looked at the marks on the upper corner and I thought, that is not Elvish, and that is not anything I have seen before, but it is organized in the way that systems that mean something are organized. I sent a report. The Archive says it is being assessed. In my experience, when something has been assessed for thirty-five years without a result, the assessment has stopped."

*On what she does not know:*
"I have been Grove-Keeper for two hundred and fourteen years and I still do not fully understand the Dreaming Grove. I have an excellent working model of what it does and a much poorer model of what it is. I have stopped expecting the gap to close. It is the right gap to have — if I understood it completely, I would be the wrong person for this job."

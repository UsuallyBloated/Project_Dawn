# The Greyfen
*Fen / Low Wood — Levels 10–20 — The Cartography Hall · Walking Name Order*

## Overview

The Greyfen is the transition between the living world and something older. It is flat, wet, and brown in every season, and the people who have surveyed it come back describing the same thing in different words: the sounds are wrong. Not absent — wrong. Birds, frogs, wind through the reeds — all present, all muffled, as if the Greyfen is inside something larger than itself that hasn't finished deciding to let sound out. Three field teams documented this independently. The cartographer's office has filed all three reports.

The Predecessor stonework that surfaces from the peat here is not ruin. It does not look like something that fell. It looks like something the ground is too thin to fully cover. The concentration increases as you move south toward Greyveil. The southern edge of the Greyfen has the heaviest surface exposure. Directly south of that: the Foundation Ring, buried under Greyveil's oldest district, which the Greyveil City Council continues to maintain does not exist.

The Greyfen knows otherwise. The Greyfen has known for a very long time.

## Setting

Flat fenland with chest-height reed beds, peat channels that look like paths until they are not, and the specific quality of a landscape that doesn't have the courtesy of being either forest or field. The stone that surfaces from the peat is dark and fitted — mortarless, geometric, precisely set — with the six-fingered load marks at corners and doorways. Most of it is partial: a course of stone here, the top of an arch there, a flat surface that players can cross if they want to.

The light in the Greyfen has no shadows, exactly. Not dim — the sky is visible. But nothing casts. Players notice this before they consciously understand why.

Navigation landmarks are the surfaced stones. Experienced characters learn them by position and use them as waypoints. The stones don't move. Everything else in the Greyfen moves at some point.

At night, the peat pools along the southern edge glow faintly. Not bright enough to see by. Bright enough to notice. The southern stonework reflects it upward from below.

## Dungeon — The Peat Hall

*Levels 13–20.*

The Peat Hall is not a ruin that sank. It is a structure that was built down. The entrance shaft is at the base of the heaviest stone exposure in the zone's center — a vertical descent through fitted stonework into chambers that have been sealed since before the Greyveil basin was a basin. The Hall descends four levels.

The temperature drops as you go down. This is not the normal underground warmth. Something in the structure is drawing heat away from the lower levels toward the surface — or holding the cold in. The working theory of the field team who first reached Level 3 is that the Hall was designed for something that preferred the cold. The theory of the Kel'varath Shaman who accompanied a second expedition is that it was designed to keep something cold that would rather be warm. Both theories are consistent with the evidence.

**Level 1 — The Approach:** Flooded outer chambers. Bog-preserved material in quantities that interest Herbalists (rare reagents). Silt Crawlers have colonized the water sections. Navigation requires tracking the stone underfoot.

**Level 2 — The Survey Chambers:** Predecessor rooms in better condition than any surface-accessible site. The geometric notation on the walls matches the Sunken Ziggurat's lower levels. Players with Architect notation skill (50+) can read partial survey data — position coordinates in a system that doesn't map to current geography, and a designation that appears across all sites: "node status: active."

**Level 3 — The Cold Ring:** Temperature drops sharply. The stonework here is immaculate — no damage, no wear, no evidence of three thousand years. The Foundation Ring's outer perimeter runs through this level; players who know what to look for can follow it in both directions until it goes beyond the dungeon boundary. The walls conduct sound. Pressing an ear to the south face: Greyveil. Distant, muffled, present. The city has been above this level for eight hundred years and has never heard what is below it.

The Long Warden patrols Level 3.

**The Long Warden** is an Architect-built construct that has been monitoring the Foundation Ring's outer perimeter since the Ring's construction. It is not the same class of object as the Third Digger — that is Dominion-era engineering; this is something older. When players enter its chamber, it checks their credentials against a registry it has been maintaining for three thousand years. Players fail the check. It issues a warning in Architect notation. Then it enforces.

It regenerates. Players who kill it will encounter it again on their next descent. Players who carry the authorization token from Varek's Spire (recovered from his desk in the Floor 7 study) can present it to the Warden — this stops regeneration permanently and opens a sustained dialogue option that does not exist during combat. The Warden's dialogue is the most direct primary-source account of what the Foundation Ring is for that exists in the game. It does not have opinions about this. It has data.

## Services

- **The Surveyor's Post** (central zone): Mira Ashwick's field station; limited vendor stock (surveying tools, cartographic supplies, Herbalism reagents from the Greyfen's specific flora); partial rest (bedrolls; the silence makes full regen difficult — one field team described trying to sleep here as "resting inside a held breath"); notice board with active zone quests
- **Herath-Vix's research camp** (east edge, near the Fae Mere border): Walking Name Order faction contact; Architect questline entry point if players have not already entered it through the Underdark Crossing or Varek's Spire; sells a partial Architect notation guide (Notation Skill unlock to 25)
- **Tomby Hollowbrook's post** (unmarked, south fen; find him by following the cheerful humming): hand-drawn zone maps for sale; they are inexplicably accurate; one map marks a location that appears on no official survey, in the southeast corner, marked only with a small circle and the notation "something here says hello"

## Faction Notes

The **Cartography Hall** (represented by Mira Ashwick) is the default faction for the Greyfen. Neutral starting standing with all players. Rises through zone quests and Peat Hall survey completion. High standing unlocks Mira's full research notes, which advance the Foundation Ring questline and include her father's original field report — the one that started her current residency.

The **Walking Name Order** (represented by Herath-Vix) starts at wary. Rises through the Architect questline and dungeon progress. High standing unlocks Herath-Vix's analysis of the Level 3 notation, which cross-references against the Roofless Hall findings and the Dominion Vault's suspected contents. He is careful about sharing this. He has reasons.

The **Treasure Seekers** are a faction in the game-mechanical sense only. Standing with them starts hostile to both other factions and cannot be raised without betraying one of them. Their operation is commercially motivated and has found approximately forty Predecessor artifacts in the past two seasons. Where those artifacts went is a questline.

## Key NPCs

**Mira Ashwick** — Human cartographer, level 20. Sent by the Greyveil Geographic Society to verify her father's field notes. Has been here for two years. Has sent four progress reports to the Society. The Society has responded twice. She does not plan to leave until she understands the silence. She is good-natured about this in a way that suggests she has stopped expecting company. Players are company. She finds this genuinely pleasant. She will share her father's notes with players at Cartography Hall standing 500.

**Seeker Herath-Vix** — Kel'varath Walking Name Order scholar, level 25. The most composed person in a zone full of quietly unsettling things. He has been denied access to Greyveil's subsurface six times (the City Council's position: there is no subsurface). He responds to each denial by moving his camp slightly closer to the Foundation Ring's perimeter — legally, because the Greyfen is not within Greyveil's jurisdiction. His current camp is at the closest legal point. He is not subtle about what he is doing. He is very precise about being within the legal boundary. Players who advance his questline will eventually learn that his survey of the perimeter from outside is more complete than the Greyveil City Council's survey from inside, because Herath-Vix is actively looking and the Council is actively not.

**Tomby Hollowbrook** — Halfling cartographer, level 5. Lost from a Greenshire road survey eight months ago and has been thoroughly enjoying the Greyfen since. Does not appear to be in danger despite all evidence that he should be. The Bog Hollows ignore him. The Pale Surveyors have not followed him. His three unidentified Predecessor artifacts are genuinely unidentified — Mira has examined them and cannot place them in any known Architect typology. Tomby uses one as a paperweight and one as a cup and has lost the third somewhere in the south fen.

## Cross-Zone Connections

- **Ardenmoor** — The treeline is visible from the Greyfen's high points (none; the whole zone is flat; "high points" means "reed banks that are slightly less submerged"). Wood Elf scouts cross the border periodically to monitor for blight-adjacent symptoms in the peat. They are not reassured by what they find, but they confirm the corruption pattern is different from the Blighted Wood's — sourced from below, not above.
- **Greyveil** — The Foundation Ring's outer perimeter is in the Peat Hall's Level 3. Mira Ashwick's questline eventually connects to a Greyveil contact who knows the Ring exists and has been quietly funding field teams to document it from the outside because internal documentation is City Council policy violation.
- **Architect questline** — The Peat Hall's survey fragments, the Warden's Cipher, and Herath-Vix's perimeter documentation all advance the chain. The Long Warden's dialogue, if unlocked, provides context for the Foundation Ring that is not available anywhere else in the network.
- **Fae Mere** — The Greyfen's eastern edge is Fae-touched; the boundary is marked by reed beds that have the wrong color at dusk. Things from the Mere occasionally wander into the Greyfen. They are not hostile. They are the wrong kind of interesting. Fae characters receive a minor passive bonus near the eastern edge that they cannot explain and the Fae will not explain if asked.

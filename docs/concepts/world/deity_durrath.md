# Durrath — The Iron Path
*God of martial discipline, endurance, and earned strength.*

---

## The Short Version

Durrath does not offer protection. He does not offer victory. He offers the path — the continuous practice of making yourself capable of the things you will be asked to do. The path is the worship. The practice is the prayer. There is no ritual that substitutes for the work.

**Patron of:** Warriors, Monks, Minotaur Shamans
**Alignment:** True Neutral — the path requires the same discipline regardless of where you are going with it
**Symbol:** A bare iron road receding to a horizon

---

## Theology

Strength is not a gift. Strength is the residue of consistent effort applied to something that resists you. Durrath's followers do not pray for strength. They train, and they attribute the results of that training to the correct understanding of how results come: from the work, which is also the worship, because Durrath is what you are doing when you practice correctly.

The iron path is not a metaphor about virtue. It is a description of what the practice looks like from the inside: long, hard, straight, with no shortcuts that do not cost you elsewhere. You walk it by doing the work today that the person you want to be would have done. You stay on it by doing that again tomorrow.

**The only sin Durrath names:** Giving up the path for reasons that are not real. Defeat is not a reason to stop — defeat is information. Weakness is not a reason to stop — weakness is the starting condition. The sin is surrender to the self that does not want to grow: the part of you that would rather be comfortable than capable. Durrath has no doctrine about what you do with your capacity once you have it. That is a question for Solrath or Erindra or whoever else is in the business of telling people how to live. Durrath is in the business of whether you are strong enough to do what you have decided.

**The responsibility that follows strength:** This is not Durrath's explicit teaching — it is the teaching that every Path-Elder eventually reaches on their own. Strength earned against something that cannot fight back is not strength; it is practice in cruelty, which trains a different set of habits than the path intends. Durrath does not issue commandments about this. The path-elders have opinions. The opinions are consistent.

## The Iron Vigil

The primary Durrath ritual. Forty-eight hours of stillness — not sleep, stillness — followed immediately by a test of capacity. The test is self-assigned. You set the challenge before the vigil begins. When the forty-eight hours end, you perform it. Then you evaluate whether you did the work or found a way around it.

No priest grades you. No doctrine specifies passing. The vigil works because you know whether you did the work or whether you did something that looked like the work. Durrath's followers report this as the hardest part — not the stillness, but the evaluation afterward, conducted alone, without the option of convincing yourself that close counts.

The vigil is done before major undertakings. Experienced followers do it seasonally. The current Anvil does it on a schedule they have not disclosed in twelve years.

## Relationship to Classes

**Warriors:** The most visible Durrath worshippers. The practice maps directly onto what Warriors do anyway; Durrath gives it a name and a framework. Warrior-Durrath worship is matter-of-fact — they train, they do the vigil before campaigns, they attribute their competence correctly. They are not theatrical about it.

**Monks:** Durrath's most complete practitioners, though many of them do not frame it that way. The Monk discipline that developed in the Southeastern Marshlands predates the formal Durrath temple by at least a century. The Durrath priests will tell you the temple is just a building — the practice was already a form of worship before there was a name for it. A Monk who trains with full commitment is performing Durrath's core rite continuously, whether they would describe it that way or not.

**Minotaur Shamans:** The ancestral endurance that Minotaur Shamans channel — the ability of the dead to keep being present, to remain useful past their death — is Durrath's domain from the Minotaur perspective. The ancestors who endured did not stop walking the path when they died. The path continues. The Shamans who communicate with them are drawing on the accumulated practice of the dead, which is Durrath's specific inheritance. The theological distinction between "Minotaur ancestor practice" and "Durrath worship" is considered a non-distinction by most Minotaur Shamans.

## Priestly Structure

The hierarchy of Durrath is not a chain of command. It is a chain of observable competence. Those further on the path can see things those earlier on it cannot see, and when they speak, it is worth listening to — not because of authority, but because of the evidence of their practice.

| Rank | Description |
|---|---|
| Forged | Anyone who has declared themselves on the path; most worshippers stay here for life and consider it sufficient |
| Iron-Sworn | Have completed the Iron Vigil at least three times in the temple; recognized as serious practitioners |
| Path-Elder | Recognized by other Path-Elders as having walked long enough to teach; the actual clergy |
| The Anvil | One position; the current highest practitioner; resident at the main temple |

The Path-Elders do not issue doctrine in the way Solrath's hierarchy does. They observe. They teach when asked. They disagree publicly and often — the path is the same for everyone, but where people are on it differs, and the view from different distances is genuinely different. This produces a theology that has remained consistent in its fundamentals and contentious in its application for as long as the temple has existed.

## The Temple

The main Durrath temple is carved into the rock face of the largest mesa in the Eastern Badlands, overlooking the inter-clan territory. It predates the current Ogre, Troll, and Half-Ogre communities — it was there when they arrived, and they have adapted their culture around its presence without worshipping there, which the priests of Durrath find both sensible and their own problem.

The temple is open to anyone who comes without weapons. Not disarmed — without weapons. The distinction is that you leave them at the base of the carved steps before you enter. You get them back when you leave. This is not a security measure; it is a statement about what kind of space the temple is. You are not here to fight. You are here to determine whether you have been practicing.

Secondary temples exist in:
- The Southeastern Marshlands (Kel`varath — old; predates the Eastern Badlands temple in the local tradition, though it is a different institution)
- Harrowmere (small, practical; used primarily by the Dockmaster Compact's more physically oriented members)
- A traveling temple maintained by a single Path-Elder who has been walking the Eastern Badlands perimeter for eleven years

## Relationship to Other Deities

**Solrath:** Adjacent, not aligned. Solrath teaches protection; Durrath teaches capacity. The friction between them is productive — a Paladin who follows Solrath and respects Durrath's practice is often the most capable protector Solrath produces. The theological argument about whether protection or capability is the correct frame has been going on for as long as both traditions have existed and neither side expects resolution.

**Erindra:** Comfortable distance. She respects the predator-prey cycle; Durrath respects the practice that makes you capable of either role. They have no argument.

**Vethis:** Tension that neither side resolves publicly. The Battle-Remembering — the enduring dead of the Ashen Marches — Durrath claims those who died in discipline and did not stop; Vethis claims all the dead. This is a theological dispute without a living authority who can settle it. The Battle-Remembering themselves do not answer the question when asked.

**Mireth:** Genuine bafflement from Durrath's followers toward Mireth's theology. Luck-as-preparation is not the same as preparation-as-preparation. Mireth finds this hilarious. The argument continues at whatever Mireth finds amusing.

**Aracheth:** Cold distance. The web requires subjects. The iron path makes subjects unnecessary.

## Dialogue from a Path-Elder (example)

*On the vigil:*
"The forty-eight hours are not the hard part. The hard part is the fifteen seconds after the vigil ends, when you are standing in front of the thing you said you would do, and you know whether you practiced or whether you rested and called it practicing. We do not ask you to confess that to anyone. We ask you to know it. Durrath's path runs on accurate knowledge of where you are. Lying to yourself about where you are is how you walk in circles."

*On winning and losing:*
"A loss tells you something. A win tells you something. Both require that you were actually trying, which is the variable that matters. Someone who trained honestly and lost has more information than someone who trained carelessly and won. The one who lost knows exactly what they need to practice next. The one who won learned that this particular effort was enough this particular time, which is much less useful."

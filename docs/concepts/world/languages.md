# Languages of the World
*Reference for all spoken languages, their origins, learning mechanics, and lore context.*

---

## Overview

Fifteen languages are spoken across the known world. Most player characters begin knowing Common and their racial language. Languages above skill 0 improve passively by hearing them spoken; skill 0 requires a trainer to unlock before passive learning begins.

When a player receives speech in a language they know partially, the text is garbled proportionally to their skill — at skill 50, roughly half the words come through clearly; at skill 25, most are garbled; at skill 100, all speech is understood perfectly. Skill 0 produces entirely garbled output — the sounds arrive but none of the meaning does.

## The Languages

### Common
**Speakers:** All civilized races, at varying fluency. Humans fluent; Trolls and Kobolds at 30 (significantly garbled).
**Origin:** A trade pidgin standardized by the Greyveil Merchant Houses approximately six hundred years ago. It is not anyone's native language in origin — it was designed to be learnable, which is why it achieved saturation. Every major city uses it as the default public language.
**Character:** Flexible, context-heavy, tolerates imprecision. Native speakers of other languages often note that Common lacks words for concepts their language handles easily.
**Learning:** Trainers available in every city. Racial starting skills reflect historical contact with Common-speaking civilization.

---

### Elvish
**Speakers:** Elf (fluent), Wood Elf (fluent), Fae (50), Half-Elf (50), Dark Elf (25 — vestigial, fading)
**Origin:** The oldest attested language in continuous use, predating the Common Reckoning calendar. The Elvish spoken today in Aelindra is the same language documented in the oldest Root Level archives. No other language has changed so little over three thousand years.
**Character:** Precise, tonal. A single Elvish word can carry meaning that requires a sentence in Common. The tonal component means written Elvish and spoken Elvish are different experiences — written loses tone markers that fluent speakers add automatically.
**Dark Elf and Elvish:** Dark Elves retain vestigial Elvish from before the underground split. It is enough to understand slow, careful surface Elvish. It is not enough to be mistaken for fluent.
**Learning:** Elvish trainers available in Aelindra and Ardenmoor. The Fae courts teach a dialect that is technically Elvish but with a century of Fae-specific additions.

---

### Elder Elvish
**Speakers:** Extremely rare. Found in the oldest Aelindra archives (sealed section). The Dreaming Warden speaks it.
**Origin:** The Elvish spoken before the Common Reckoning — the ancestral form. Not mutually intelligible with modern Elvish. The garble cipher is similar (Elves recognize it as related to their language) but distinct.
**Learning:** Practically speaking: the sealed archive (Aelindra) contains primary sources. The Dreaming Warden speaks it and will respond to speech in it with increased engagement. No trainer NPC teaches it — it must be derived from reading.
**Lore significance:** The sealed archive's letter (in Architect notation) also contains a line in Elder Elvish. The Architects used it as a reference language for something they were communicating. Whether the Architects spoke Elder Elvish or simply used it as a cipher layer is unresolved.

---

### Dark Speech
**Speakers:** Dark Elf (fluent), Evil-aligned underground factions (partial)
**Origin:** Developed in Vel'Sharath over the centuries of underground isolation. It diverged from Elvish when the Dark Elves decided their language needed to be purposefully unpleasant to speak — the consonant clusters, the glottal stops, the controlled sibilance. It was designed partly as a functional language and partly as a statement about what they had become.
**Character:** Precise and uncomfortable. The garble cipher is one of the most scrambled — listeners who don't know Dark Speech hear something that sounds intentionally hostile.
**Use in practice:** The Dark Elf intelligence network's ciphers are built on Dark Speech encoding. Knowing Dark Speech does not give you access to the ciphers; it gives you the structural understanding to approach them.
**Learning:** Trainers available in Vel'Sharath (high standing required). Also possible with high Aracheth temple standing.

---

### Gnomish
**Speakers:** Gnome (fluent)
**Origin:** A language that evolved alongside Gnomish engineering culture — words for concepts in mechanics, precision measurement, and theoretical mathematics have no Common equivalents. It is one of the fastest-spoken languages in the world, which Gnomes consider a feature.
**Character:** Dense with technical vocabulary. Gnomish conversation between two Gnomish engineers can cover a topic in a sentence that would take Common speakers a paragraph. Between Gnomes and non-Gnomes, this creates a compression problem — they have to translate, which they do accurately but with visible impatience.
**Learning:** Gnomish trainers available in Cogsworth and in several Gnome guild outposts. Kobold engineers who work closely with Gnomes often pick up functional Gnomish passively over years.

---

### Halfling
**Speakers:** Halfling (fluent)
**Origin:** Developed in the close-knit Halfling farming communities — a language designed for private use among neighbors. Its garble cipher reverses the alphabet, which Halflings find funny and foreigners find irritating. It is not a written language originally; the written form was standardized relatively recently for record-keeping.
**Character:** Informal, community-specific, full of local references. Halfling in Greenshire has different idioms from Halfling in southern agricultural communities. They are mutually intelligible but visitors notice the differences.
**Learning:** Halfling trainers available in Greenshire and Halfling communities within other zones. The language is not strategically important enough that many non-Halflings bother, which Halflings consider appropriate.

---

### Dwarvish
**Speakers:** Dwarf (fluent)
**Origin:** The clan language, maintained deliberately unchanged across centuries. Dwarvish has a strong cultural taboo against borrowing words from other languages — when a new concept needs naming, a Dwarvish word is constructed from existing roots. This makes the language precise within its domain and slightly awkward outside it.
**Character:** Slow and formal. There is a Dwarvish conversational convention that requires speakers to indicate their clan affiliation at the start of a formal exchange — this is why Dwarf NPCs sometimes seem to be introducing themselves extensively before they say anything else.
**Learning:** Trainers available in the Dwarven Mountain Holds. Also available from certain Dwarven merchant contacts in Greyveil and Harrowmere.

---

### Ogre
**Speakers:** Ogre (fluent), Troll (partial), Half-Ogre (fluent)
**Origin:** A language of force and directness. Ogre has very few words for abstract concepts and a very large vocabulary for physical action, material quality, and hierarchy. The garble cipher maps to a shifted alphabet — non-speakers hear something like Common spoken carelessly.
**Character:** Loud. This is not a metaphor — Ogre as a spoken language operates at higher volume than most, because it was developed in environments (mountain territory, open badlands) where distance communication mattered.
**Learning:** Trainers at Ashfang Hold. Not commonly taught outside Ogre communities.

---

### Troll
**Speakers:** Troll (fluent), Ogre (partial)
**Origin:** Related to Ogre but older and stranger. Trolls' regenerative physiology has produced a culture with a longer time-horizon than most races, and their language reflects this — Troll has extensive tense structures covering long periods that Common cannot easily express. "What happened before I was born" and "what will happen after everyone I know is dead" are single-word concepts in Troll.
**Character:** Deep, rhythmic, patient. Troll speakers often pause in ways Common speakers interpret as hesitation; it is not hesitation, it is the language's structural requirement for considered response.
**Learning:** Trainers available at Ashfang Hold (Troll section). Almost never taught outside Troll communities.

---

### Kel`varath
**Speakers:** Kel`varath (fluent), Bonecourt scholars (partial)
**Origin:** The language of the Dominion and its predecessor cultures. Old Kel`varath documents exist in two scripts — the Dominion administrative script (Common-adjacent phonetically) and the pre-Dominion ritual script (related to Architect notation in structural ways scholars find interesting). Modern Kel`varath is the Dominion administrative variant.
**Character:** Formal, hierarchical. Kel`varath has a complete parallel vocabulary for addressing social superiors vs. equals vs. inferiors, and native speakers switch registers automatically. Kel`varath texts from the Dominion era tend to reveal the writer's social position through their register choices.
**Architect connection:** Pre-Dominion ritual Kel`varath script shares structural features with Architect notation — this is one piece of evidence for the Sealed Record's ancestral hypothesis. It is also consistent with the alternative explanation that both writing systems derived from a common pre-existing source.
**Learning:** Trainers at the Bonecourt (Kel`varath starting zone) and in Southeastern Marshlands settlements. The three Dominion Orders at Ixareth-Kul all speak Kel`varath; high Order standing may unlock trainers.

---

### Minotaur
**Speakers:** Minotaur (fluent)
**Origin:** The Minotaur clan language — primarily oral, with a secondary written form that uses hoof-struck marks rather than pen strokes, which gives it a distinctive visual rhythm when transcribed. It developed on the Khala Savannahs and is strongly tied to Minotaur pastoral and shamanic traditions.
**Character:** Metaphor-heavy. Minotaur communication relies heavily on contextual reference to shared cultural knowledge — a Minotaur saying three words to another Minotaur may be communicating a full story that an outsider hears as three words. This makes partial Minotaur extremely confusing; context is load-bearing.
**Learning:** Trainers at Auroch's Rest. Shamanic practitioners find it easier to learn (WIS-adjacent passive learning bonus).

---

### Ghost Tongue
**Speakers:** Revenant (fluent, earned at transformation)
**Origin:** Not a language in the standard sense. Ghost Tongue is how the recently dead communicate — or how living Revenants access the communication register of the dead. It is less a vocabulary and more a shift in how communication is conducted: slower, denser, less reliant on tone. The Battle-Remembering of the Ashen Marches speak it when they choose to communicate with living visitors.
**Character:** Unsettling to hear for reasons that are difficult to articulate. Common speakers report that Ghost Tongue speech lands differently from expected — they understand the words but feel the understanding arrive in the wrong order. This is not hallucination; it is a documented property of the language.
**Learning:** Skill 0 → 1 requires a teacher who speaks Ghost Tongue. The Battle-Remembering will teach it to players who have completed their questline. Revenant-transformed characters receive it automatically.

---

### Fae
**Speakers:** Fae (fluent), Elf (partial in some communities)
**Origin:** The oldest language still spoken and actively alive — Fae adds new words constantly, because Fae culture does not preserve things, it experiences them. Words that are no longer needed stop being used and become historical. Words that are needed for new things are coined on the spot and spread if they are good.
**Character:** Impossible to fully document because it is changing continuously. What is written in this document about Fae vocabulary will be partially obsolete. Fae speakers find this funny. Scholars find it alarming.
**Learning:** The Fae courts (Fae Mere zone) have trainers. The Fae do not always cooperate with the training process, which they consider a category error.

---

### Felhari
**Speakers:** Felhari (fluent)
**Origin:** The clan-language of the Khala Savannahs, related distantly to Fae in some structural features (both races share a very old common contact period that neither acknowledges having records of). Felhari has a strong oral tradition — stories, histories, and spirit-communication are all conducted in Felhari; Common is used for external trade.
**Character:** Musical, pitched. The Felhari language uses pitch distinctions that Common and most other languages do not, which means written Felhari in the standard script loses significant information. Felhari have a supplemental notation for pitch that outsiders rarely learn.
**Learning:** Trainers at Khala Savannahs settlements. Spirit-Walker practitioners can teach it; finding them requires Felhari community standing.

---

### Kobold
**Speakers:** Kobold (fluent), Gnome (partial in some Cogsworth engineers)
**Origin:** Pack-specific in origin — ancient Kobold had dozens of pack dialects, and the current unified Kobold is a blended trade language that developed in the Tunnel Market of the Kobold Deeps. It preserves features of most pack dialects but is native to none.
**Character:** Fast, efficient, context-independent (unlike Minotaur, which requires shared cultural knowledge). Kobold is designed to communicate across pack boundaries where that shared knowledge does not exist. It is probably the best language for brief, precise information exchange with strangers.
**Learning:** Trainers at the Kobold Deeps and in Cogsworth's Kobold warrens.

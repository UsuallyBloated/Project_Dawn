# NPC Profile — Warden-Captain Sereth Dolan
*Breach Wardens. The Breach gate. Levels 25–40.*

---

## Identity

**Full name:** Sereth Dolan *(SEH-reth DOH-lan)*
**Race:** Half-Elf
**Class:** Ranger (level 33; she describes her class as "retired Ranger, current administrator," which is technically true and practically false)
**Age:** 44
**Alignment:** Neutral

**Physical description:** Medium height, lean, with the economy of motion of someone who was a field operative before she was a bureaucrat and still thinks of herself as the former. Hair dark brown going silver at the temples, kept short for practical reasons she will cite if asked. The Breach Wardens' grey, tailored slightly better than regulation but not so much that it looks like she is trying. She carries a shortbow on her back at all times in the Breach — not as a statement, but because the Breach is a conflict zone and she has been shot at twice at her desk. She sleeps with the bow within reach. She has been doing this for eleven years.

Three small scars on the left side of her neck: the first assassination attempt, Year 1 of her tenure. She does not hide them. She does not discuss them unless someone asks directly. When asked directly, she says: "That one I saw coming. The second I didn't."

---

## Personality

Sereth Dolan has been Warden-Captain for eleven years. She took the position at thirty-three, when the previous captain retired suddenly in circumstances she investigated thoroughly and concluded were voluntary retirement under uncomfortable pressure. She has not been pressured into voluntary retirement in eleven years. This is not an accident.

She is pragmatic with a specific kind of long-game patience that is common in people who manage situations that cannot be resolved, only managed. The control cycle conflict at the Breach cannot be ended — she has no authority or desire to end it. She can maintain the conditions under which it is fought in a way that does not kill her organization, her personnel, or her neutrality. That is the job. She does the job.

She trusts players who demonstrate they understand the Breach's structure — not just its mechanics, but why the structure exists and what happens if any part of it fails. Players who treat the Breach as simply a conflict zone with points miss what she is doing. Players who understand that the Breach Wardens' neutrality is load-bearing infrastructure get different conversations.

She is not naive about her organization's limits. She knows some of her personnel have been compromised — she has caught two over eleven years and dealt with both. She suspects a third. She cannot move on the third until she has evidence, and she will not move without evidence, because wrongly accusing a Warden undermines the neutrality that makes everything work.

---

## The Gate-Warden Investigation

Sereth Dolan has been quietly funding research into the Gate-Warden for three years. This started when she found a reference in the Breach Wardens' founding documents — a three-hundred-year-old charter — to "the prior occupant of the lower passage." The prior occupant is not described further. The founding document's authors clearly knew more about it than they wrote down.

She pulled the oldest Warden records she could find. The Gate-Warden was already there when the Breach Wardens were established. The founding generation incorporated the Underbreach access into the treaty structure around something that was already there. They did not investigate it. They noted its presence, worked around it, and built policy on top of it.

She has been trying to find out what it is for three years through commissioned researchers who are not officially affiliated with the Wardens. She has not found the answer. The researchers have found: it is old, older than the Breach Wardens, possibly older than the Breach Gate itself; it responds to faction credentials rather than person credentials (it attacks "unauthorized" as a category, not specific individuals); and the Gate-Key it drops has been picked up four times in the server's history, used twice, and has the same property each time.

What she has not found: what it was built for, who built it, or what "authorized" means in the original protocol.

## Relationship to Players

**First meeting:** Neutral. The Warden-Captain is accessible to any player in the Breach with basic Breach Standing. Her initial interaction is professional and brief — she assesses the player quickly, categorizes them by behavior pattern, and files them. Most players get a short interaction and are assessed as "standard." Players who ask non-standard questions or demonstrate unusual knowledge get a slightly longer interaction and a mental flag.

**Improving standing:** Consistent, correct Breach behavior over time. Completing Warden-commissioned quests. Arbitration decisions she agrees with. Notably: resolving situations the Wardens could not resolve through official channels. She will notice and not acknowledge noticing until significantly later.

**High standing:** She becomes direct about the Gate-Warden investigation. She will explain what she knows, what she suspects, and what she needs. She will ask for things she cannot obtain through official channels, explain why the official channel fails, and make clear that she is asking as a person rather than as the Warden-Captain — which means she cannot offer official Warden backing, but she can offer personal trust, personal resources, and personal honesty about what is happening.

The third assassination attempt, if it happens during play, happens to a player who is at high standing with her. It is not a player death — it is an event that puts her in a situation that requires player help and reveals the third compromised Warden.

## The Things She Will Not Say Officially

Several things Sereth Dolan knows or suspects that she cannot say in her official capacity:

1. **The treaty has a gap.** The Third Camp quest hook — a camp inside the Breach boundaries that belongs to neither faction — is a situation the treaty does not cover. She has been managing it through informal pressure while drafting a treaty amendment neither faction has agreed to. The gap being publicly known would give bad actors a roadmap.

2. **One of the Dockmasters in Harrowmere has been paying an Iron Camp officer for information about Compact shipping routes.** She cannot act on this because it is outside her jurisdiction. She has told the Harbormaster (Jeven Crost). He is being careful about how to act on it.

3. **The Gate-Key does something besides provide passage.** She does not know what. She knows it does something because of a behavioral change in the Gate-Warden during the periods between key drops and key use — and because the two instances of the key being used produced a change in the Breach that she documented and that nobody else appears to have noticed.

---

## Quest Hooks from Sereth Dolan

- **The Ledger Entry** (from faction doc) — A kill was registered with no clear source. She needs to know how.
- **The Third Warden** — She has evidence of a third compromised Warden but cannot move without more. She needs a player she trusts to observe the suspected Warden for one full Breach cycle — not interfere, just observe and report. What the player observes is worse than she expected.
- **The Gate-Warden's Registry** (unlocked at Breach Standing 1500) — She shares her research and asks players to continue it. Specific deliverable: anything from any other zone that mentions the Breach Gate's original construction. She has found one reference and needs corroborating documentation.
- **The Third Camp** — She needs the third camp resolved without either major faction finding out the treaty has a gap. The camp's occupant is not hostile — the occupant has a specific reason for being there that changes the resolution significantly.

## Dialogue Samples

*On the Gate-Warden:*
"It was here before us. Our founding documents call it 'the prior occupant' and then move on, which tells me the founders knew more than they wrote down, knew it was too complicated to address, and decided to build around it instead. I've been trying to work out what they knew. Three years. What I have: it's old, it runs on authorization categories I can't fully map, and the key it produces does something I haven't documented yet. What I don't have: why it's here, who put it here, and whether 'the prior occupant' was a diplomatic term for 'something dangerous we decided not to think about.' I hope not. I haven't ruled it out."

*On neutrality:*
"People think neutrality means not having opinions. I have opinions. Very specific opinions. Neutrality means my opinions don't change the Wardens' position — because the moment the Wardens have a position, both factions have a reason to end us and start the whole thing over under a flag they control. My neutrality is structural. It's not that I don't care who wins control windows. It's that caring costs more than it's worth."

*On the assassination attempts:*
"The first was a Vanguard officer who didn't like a ruling I made. He's been expelled from the Breach and I'm told he's farming somewhere in the western territories now. The second was a contractor — no verified affiliation, which is more interesting and more alarming. I don't know who hired the contractor. I have guesses. I check those guesses every six months against available information. I haven't confirmed any of them yet. Eleven years and I haven't confirmed it. That bothers me."

*On players she trusts:*
"I don't trust quickly. It's not personal. It's that I've been doing this for eleven years and the people who've tried to compromise my organization have mostly done it through relationships — getting close, building trust, and then using it. I know this. I watch for it. And then I watch someone do something in the Breach that they didn't have to do, that costs them something, and that doesn't benefit them directly. That's where I start trusting. Not because it proves anything. Because it's information."

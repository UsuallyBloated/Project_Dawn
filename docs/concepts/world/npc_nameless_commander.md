# NPC Profile — The Nameless Commander
*The Final Encampment. Level 45. The Ashen Marches dungeon boss.*

---

## Identity

**Name:** Unknown. The Commander does not remember it. Vethis's ledger has it. The ledger is not accessible.
**Race:** Unknown. The Commander's physical appearance has been altered by three hundred years of Revenant state — the original bone structure is present but covered by the ash-and-bone integration common to the oldest Marches Revenants. Players with Perception 15+ can determine it was likely a tall race. Players with relevant racial knowledge skills can narrow it to three candidates: Human, Elf, or Kel`varath. The truth of which one is in the Records Tent.
**Class:** Unknown. Movement patterns suggest either a martial class or a tactically trained officer without spellcasting ability. No spell effects have been observed.
**Level:** 45
**Alignment:** Unknown — the Commander does not recognize alignment-based magic that would normally trigger a response

**Physical description:** Tall, still. The Commander sits in the tent's central chair — not slumped, not alertly upright, but in the specific posture of someone who has been waiting for three hundred years and has become very comfortable with waiting. The armor is three hundred years old and immaculate, because Revenant soldiers maintain their equipment as part of their ongoing orders. The armor has no insignia. The face is partially visible: the hollow-bone Revenant structure of the older undead, with no flesh remaining, but the eye sockets contain a light that is not fire — it moves when the Commander moves, tracks when the Commander tracks, dims when the Commander is doing whatever the closest approximation to sleep is.

---

## What the Commander Remembers

The Commander has three hundred years of memory in the Marches, all of it present and accessible. What is not accessible:

**Before the Marches:** Nothing. The Commander does not have the memory of arriving at the Marches. The earliest memory is the battle — a specific moment during what Marches historians would recognize as the second Marches battle, Year 6 of the Dominion War. The Commander was already in command at that point; there are no memories of receiving the command, of being given orders, of a prior life.

**The name:** Not present. The Commander uses no name for themselves in the Records Tent's battle orders. The orders are signed with a tactical symbol — a specific mark the Commander made on every document — but not a name. Whether this was doctrine or personal choice is in the records, if anyone reads carefully.

**The race:** Not present as self-knowledge. The Commander knows what they look like now. What they looked like before three hundred years of Revenant transformation is not accessible from the inside.

**The objective:** Present. The Commander knows the objective completely and has known it for three hundred years. They have been unable to complete it because completing it requires something that has not arrived. This is why they are waiting.

---

## The Objective

The objective is in the Records Tent. Players who read the orders, decode the tactical notation, and cross-reference with the broader Dominion War lore will find it.

**The objective (summary):** The Third Army was sent to the Marches to retrieve something that was already there when the war started — something at the convergence point that predates both the Dominion and the Coalition. The Commander's orders describe it as "the survey point" and instruct the Third Army to reach it, assess its status, and return a report to a recipient whose name is in the orders.

The recipient's name is not a person. It is an institution that no longer exists — an organization the Commander does not remember receiving the objective from, referenced in the orders only by a designator that scholars in any of the three Dominion Orders would recognize as an internal Dominion classification code from before the war.

**What "the survey point" is:** The convergence point at the center of the Marches — the place where the three battle lines met, where the transformation site is. The Commander reached it during Year 7. They made it to the objective. They could not complete the assessment because the battle was still happening around them, and the orders specified that the assessment had to be performed under conditions of "full cessation" — no active combat within observation range.

The battle never fully ceased. The battle is still happening. The Commander is still waiting for full cessation before completing the assessment.

---

## The Three Hundred Years

The Commander has watched the Marches for three hundred years from the Final Encampment. They have watched their soldiers — Third Army Revenants — maintain the camp and the formations as ordered. They have watched the Dominion and Coalition Revenants continue their incomplete war. They have watched the living come to the Marches in decreasing numbers, then increasing numbers again as the Marches became a place of interest.

They have thought a great deal. They have not resolved what they think about most of what they have thought about.

What the Commander has concluded in three hundred years of waiting:
- The objective is still valid; the survey point is still there; the assessment has not been completed
- The original recipient of the report no longer exists; it is unclear who the report should go to at this point
- The conditions of the orders — full cessation before assessment — may not be achievable as long as the Marches dead are active; the Commander may have inadvertently created a loop
- Whether this matters is uncertain; the orders did not specify what happens if the conditions cannot be met

The Commander has had three hundred years to notice the loop. They have not resolved it because resolving it requires making a decision that was not in the orders, and making decisions that were not in the orders requires authority the Commander is not certain they have anymore, given that their command structure does not exist.

## Interaction Options

### Option 1 — Fight
The Commander is a level 45 encounter. They fight with the tactical precision of someone with three hundred years of combat experience as a Revenant. They do not call for reinforcements — the soldiers in the outer camp join if combat spills out, but the Commander faces players in the tent. On death: the camp disbands. Third Army Revenants disperse. The Marches change (see the Ashen Marches zone doc for the zone-scale consequence).

### Option 2 — Complete the Objective
Players who read the Records Tent, understand the objective, and have done the preparation (visiting the convergence point, understanding what the survey point is) can offer to complete the assessment for the Commander. This requires:
- The Marches fighting to be suppressed (the Convergence Point transformation mechanic, plus players completing the two faction questlines to temporarily freeze the Dominion and Coalition Revenants)
- A player who can read and record the assessment at the convergence point
- Returning the record to the Commander

The Commander then faces the original problem: there is no recipient. The report exists; no one to give it to. Players can resolve this by either:
- Presenting the report to the Walking Name order, who accept it as the Architects' communication they have been seeking (incorrect framing, but the Walking Name believes it)
- Delivering it to the Greyveil archives, who file it under the Dominion War historical record
- Keeping it

In all cases: the Commander, when the report is delivered, sits for a moment, then stands for the first time in three hundred years, says something players can interpret in two ways, and disperses. The camp disbands. The Third Army Revenants disperse. The Marches change differently than if the Commander was killed — the zone consequence is distinct.

### Option 3 — Tell Them the Recipient Is Gone
Players who have done sufficient Dominion War research can tell the Commander that their command structure is gone — the organization they were reporting to no longer exists. The Commander knows this. They want to know what players think this means for the orders. They will genuinely listen. The conversation can go several ways depending on what players say. At the end of the conversation, the Commander makes a choice. What they choose depends on how the conversation went.

## Dialogue Samples

*On waiting:*
"I have been waiting for the conditions of my orders to be met. The conditions have not been met. The conditions may not be achievable. I have been thinking about this for a long time. I do not know what the correct decision is when the conditions of an order cannot be met. My orders did not address this."

*On their name:*
"I don't remember it. I have tried. The memory would be there — I can feel the shape of where it is. It is not accessible. If you find it in the records, I would like to know it. I am not certain I would recognize it. But I would like to know."

*On the Third Army:*
"They follow their orders. They have followed their orders for three hundred years. They are very good soldiers. I think about this sometimes — whether very good is the right description. Whether what they are doing is admirable or something else. I have not resolved it."

*On whether they want to stop:*
"I want to complete the objective. I have wanted to complete the objective for three hundred years. Whether I want to stop after that is a question I have not been able to think about clearly. It requires completing the objective first. Or it requires deciding the objective cannot be completed, which is a different kind of ending, and I have not been ready to decide that either. You may have noticed that I am not good at endings."

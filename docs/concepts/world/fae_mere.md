# Fae Mere
*Fae Court / Dream Forest — Levels 25–35 — Fae Courts*

## Overview
Fae Mere exists at a slightly different resolution than the rest of the game. Things are brighter and more saturated and more specific in ways that become disorienting after an hour: that is definitely a tree, but it has too many branches in the exact right places, and the shadows it casts are wrong. The Fae built this zone the way they build everything — from the inside out, starting with what they wanted it to mean and constructing the physical reality around the meaning.

This is the Fae homeland and the zone with the highest density of illusion and enchantment effects in the game. Enemies here are frequently not what they appear to be. The safe paths are not marked because the Fae consider "safe" to be a navigational skill rather than a given. The Enchanter and Druid classes have specific advantages in this zone; everyone else relies on pattern recognition and caution.

The Fae-Old transformation can only be triggered after sufficient investment in Fae court practice, most of which happens here.

## Setting
A forest built around a central mere (a shallow body of still water) that is not, on close inspection, reflecting the sky above it. The four Fae courts control territories around the mere: Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter courts, each with a distinct aesthetic and a distinct relationship to the center. The courts do not war openly; they compete, which is more interesting and more dangerous.

The zone has day/night cycles, but they are compressed — days and nights in Fae Mere cycle faster than elsewhere, and the four courts each have their most active period. This is intentional. Time works differently here, and the game communicates that mechanically.

## What Was Here Before

The Mere is not reflecting the sky. Observers who study it closely enough — and several scholars have tried, usually under the patronage of curious court members who found the observation politically useful — report that the reflection shows a landscape that does not correspond to the current canopy overhead. The trees are wrong. The light source is wrong. The scale is wrong.

The Ash Scribes of Ixareth-Kul have a partial record suggesting that this site was a Predecessor archive of a different type than the ziggurats: not a record of events but a record of place. What the Mere reflects is the site as it existed before — before the Fae arrived, before whatever came before the Fae, possibly before the current geological age. The Fae built their courts around it in full knowledge of what it was doing, which is part of why the courts compete for control of the Crosswater Bazaar territory around the Mere's edge. Whoever controls the near shore controls who looks into the water and under what conditions.

The Fae did not create the Mere's reflective property. They found it and built inward from it, which is why the zone's interior logic is slightly wrong in ways that become disorienting after an hour.

## Dungeon — The Reflection Below
*Levels 28–35.*

The Mere has a floor. Below the floor is the dungeon. What's down there is the reflection the Mere is actually showing — not the sky, but what was here before the Fae arrived and decided to build over it. The lower levels of The Reflection Below contain Predecessor record-carvings in the same style as the Sunken Ziggurat — which the Soulcarvers of Ixareth-Kul know about and have been attempting to access for decades, blocked at each attempt by the Fae courts' territorial control of the Mere. A character with both high Fae court standing and Soulcarver access from the Ruined Dominion questline can trigger a unique interaction in the dungeon's deepest room.

The Reflection Below is the most mechanically distinctive dungeon in the game: enemy appearances are unreliable, environmental hazards shift between pulls, and some room configurations are different on subsequent runs because the dungeon is reflecting something that changes. Enchanters can read the illusory elements; Druids can read the natural ones.

**Named:** The First Impression (level 35 — the original thought the Fae had when they found this place, somehow externalized) — drops Enchanter illusion-focus staff; Fae-aligned ring.

## Services
- Individual court hubs: each court offers faction-specific quests; Enchanter and Druid trainers aligned with court philosophy
- The Crosswater Bazaar (neutral zone at the Mere's edge): cross-court commerce; illusion reagents; lore texts on Fae history
- Fae-Old progression: specific quests in each court accumulate toward the transformation requirement

## Faction Notes
Four distinct factions (Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter courts) with complex relationships. A character can have high standing in one court and low in another. Cross-court quests require careful navigation — completing a quest for Winter may explicitly lower standing with Summer.

Fae characters start with court-aligned standing. Non-Fae start neutral to all four. The game does not tell you which court you are interacting with most favorably; you learn from outcomes.

Evil-aligned characters can access the Autumn and Winter courts (which operate more pragmatically about alignment). Spring and Summer courts require at minimum Neutral alignment for quest access.

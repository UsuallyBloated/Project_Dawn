# What Bess Won't Discuss — Millhaven Mystery Chain
*Quest hook document — Crossed Keys Inn, Millhaven, Greenshire*

---

## The Setup

Bess Holloway has run the Crossed Keys Inn for nineteen years. She bought it from the previous owner at a price she describes as "very fair" when anyone asks, and she does not elaborate on what made it so fair, or why the previous owner was so motivated to leave, or where the previous owner is now.

She is not evil. She is practical, sharp, and twenty years deeper into a secret than she intended to be when she first decided to keep it. The secret is not about her. It is about Millhaven, and about three prominent families, and about what was found in the cellar of this specific building fourteen years before she bought it, when the building was still a different kind of establishment and the people who ran it made a specific choice to not involve the town guard.

That choice is in the old ledgers in the locked back room in the cellar. The ledger is not hers. She kept it because it proved what she knew, and it proved who else knew, and as long as she has it, a specific group of people have a reason to not make her life difficult.

This arrangement has worked for nineteen years.

---

## The Chain

### Hook — Something Wrong in the Cellar

Players encounter the hook in one of three ways:
1. **Bess volunteers it** — A player who has done a significant favor for Bess (the default starting quests in Greenshire pass through the Crossed Keys) is told, privately, that something has changed. "I need someone to look at my cellar. Not the whole thing. There's a door in the back. I need you to tell me if the lock is different."
2. **Observation** — A player with sufficient Perception notices that the cellar stairs show recent heavy use — boot prints going down and not coming back. When asked, Bess says the cellar is off-limits. She says it once.
3. **The Millhaven Cellars dungeon** — Players who clear the Millhaven Cellars discover a passage connecting to the Crossed Keys cellar. The passage is old. It was deliberately concealed from both ends.

### Part 1 — What's In the Room

The locked back room:
- **The strongbox** — Contains the inn deed, Bess's savings, a letter she will not show anyone (personal; not relevant to the quest; she will say only that it is from someone who left), and a key to a room in Greyveil she has not visited in eight years
- **The old ledgers** — Three volumes. The first two are financial records from fourteen years before Bess bought the inn; the name on the cover is "The Millhaven Gentlemen's Society," which was the previous establishment. The third ledger is different — written in a cipher, in a different hand, and the dates in it overlap with events in the Millhaven Cellars dungeon
- **The something else** — A small sealed jar, clay, with a wax stopper. Bess has had it for nineteen years. She does not know what is inside. The previous owner pressed it into her hands when he sold and told her never to open it. She has honored this instruction with a stubbornness she cannot fully explain. The jar is warm. It has been warm since she got it.

### Part 2 — The Ledger Cipher

The third ledger is written in the Millhaven Gentlemen's Society cipher, which was also used by the precursor cult that became the Tender of the Below's congregation. Players can decode it by:
- Finding the cipher key in the Millhaven Cellars dungeon (the Cultist Shrine has a copy, used for new initiates)
- Having sufficient Intelligence stat (auto-decode at INT 14+, partial decode at INT 10–13)
- Asking a specific NPC in Greyveil (the archivist at the Witch Hunter Chapter House will recognize it, will ask where you found it, will want to know badly enough to decode it)

**The ledger reveals:** The Millhaven Gentlemen's Society knew about The Old Thing. Not as a deity — as a problem. They found the original chamber fourteen years before Bess bought the inn, panicked, argued about it, and came to a decision that is recorded in the cipher: they would not tell the town guard. They would not tell anyone. They would seal the passage and hope the thing went back to sleep. The ledger documents their monitoring of the situation over six years, at which point the entries stop.

Three family names appear in the ledger. Two of those families still have living members in Millhaven. One family name is the name of the current Millhaven town magistrate.

### Part 3 — The Families

Players who bring the ledger information to the relevant parties find three distinct reactions:

**Family A (Trevath family — merchants, two adult children):** Does not know. The parents who made the decision are dead. Their children inherited money from a business they were told was legitimate entertainment and suppressed knowledge they were never given. They are horrified when confronted. They will help, within limits. They are afraid.

**Family B (Dorn family — farmers, elderly patriarch still living):** Knows. Rorik Dorn is eighty-one years old, has known about the chamber his entire adult life, and has made his peace with it in the way you make peace with things when you decide the alternative is worse. He will say: "The thing was already old when we found it. Another hundred years, another thousand — it was sleeping. We chose not to wake it up. We chose right." He is not entirely wrong. He will not apologize.

**Family C (Magistrate Cellen Harwick):** The magistrate's father signed the ledger. The magistrate has the cipher key in a box under his bed and has had it since his father's death — he found the ledger in his father's study, decoded enough to understand what it meant, and made the same decision his father made. He has told himself it was pragmatism. He has not slept well in eight years.

If players go to the magistrate with the ledger, he will try to buy their silence first. If that fails, he will try to discredit them. He is not a bad man. He is a man who made a choice young, compounded it with another choice, and has spent eight years hoping nobody would find the ledger.

### Part 4 — The Jar

If players open the jar (Bess will not stop them if they have come this far; she will stand at the top of the stairs):

The jar contains ash and a tooth. The tooth is old — very old, worn smooth, larger than a human molar. The ash smells like something burned a long time ago in a place that no longer exists.

The Witch Hunter archivist in Greyveil will recognize the tooth immediately and will not say how. She will want to take it for study. If players let her, a follow-up questline opens in Greyveil about what the tooth is and where it came from.

If players analyze the tooth themselves (Necromancer with appropriate skills, or Bone Carver tradeskill at high level): the tooth is not from any living species. The enamel structure is wrong. It predates the current geological layer by a significant margin.

The ash tests as belonging to a fire that burned at a temperature not achievable by wood or coal.

The jar was warm because the tooth is warm. It has been warm for an unknown length of time, independent of any heat source.

## Bess at the End

After the quest chain resolves — however it resolves — Bess will have one conversation with a player who saw it through to the end. She will buy them a drink. She will tell them the previous owner's name (Aldren Coss) and that he left Millhaven the morning after the sale, and she does not know where he went, and she has thought about finding out and decided against it every year for nineteen years.

She will ask if they opened the jar.

If they say yes: she will nod. She will say, "I thought it was something like that." She will not ask what was inside.

If they say no: she will nod. She will say, "Good."

Either way, she will pour another drink and return to the bar and that will be the end of what Bess is willing to discuss.

## Resolution Options

| Path | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Expose everything (guard, public, all families) | Magistrate resigns; Trevath family gains influence; Rorik Dorn dies within the year; The Old Thing stirs slightly — not awake, but less settled. Bess leaves Millhaven. |
| Expose the cult only (silence the families) | Dungeon is formally cleared; families owe the player a significant favor; Harwick is quietly indebted; situation stable. Bess continues as before, grateful. |
| Deliver tooth to Witch Hunters | Greyveil questline opens; Witch Hunters begin an investigation into the Millhaven area that will eventually find the families anyway, slower and thornier. |
| Destroy the ledger | Nothing changes for the families. The passage is still there. The Old Thing is still there. The jar is still warm. The players know. |
| Give the ledger to Rorik Dorn | He burns it. He says: "Thank you." He means it. He is still not going to apologize. |

## Ties to Other Content
- **Millhaven Cellars dungeon** — The passage to the Crossed Keys cellar is accessible from the Cultist Shrine level; clearing the dungeon first makes this quest easier and provides the cipher key
- **Greyveil Witch Hunter Chapter House** — The tooth follow-up; Witch Hunter players get additional dialogue recognizing the tooth type
- **The Architects lore** — The tooth's age and composition, if fully analyzed, matches the time period of Architect construction in the region. This connection is available to players who have completed Architect-related content elsewhere but is not stated explicitly

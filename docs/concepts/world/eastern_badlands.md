# Eastern Badlands
*Wasteland — Levels 25–40 — Ogre / Troll / Half-Ogre*

## Overview
The Badlands are honest about what they are. The land is red clay and broken rock, water is scarce and contested, and the things that live here have adapted to conditions that would make other zones' inhabitants reconsider their life choices. There are no temples here. There are no Bard colleges. There is a lot of open sky and a lot of territory to hold and a lot of enemies that have been at it for generations.

The Ogre, Troll, and Half-Ogre communities here did not drift to this territory by accident. They were pushed here by the expansion of Good-aligned civilizations that had more organized military capacity and fewer reservations about where they were expanding into. The Badlands are what was left when everything easier was taken. The culture that developed in response is hard, practical, internally structured in ways that outsiders miss because they are looking for the kind of structure they already recognize, and fiercely territorial about the little that remains.

This is Evil-neutral content in the sense that the Good-aligned factions are not present and the rules of Good-aligned zones don't apply. Characters of any alignment can find work here. The alignment that matters here is whether you are useful and whether you are a threat.

## Setting
Broken mesa terrain with dry arroyos, exposed mineral deposits, scattered dead-tree stands, and the occasional spring around which every living thing within ten miles has formed a territorial dispute. Ogre camps occupy the high ground. Troll settlements follow the arroyos (trolls are drawn to moving water and will not camp more than half a day's walk from a source). Half-Ogre settlements are scattered through the middle ground, negotiating between the two.

The Badlands have a specific light quality at dawn and dusk — the red clay reflects warm light in a way that briefly makes the whole zone look like something lit from inside.

## Dungeon — The Bone Citadel
*Levels 32–40.*

Something built a structure out of bones in the western Badlands — bones too large to be local fauna, bones carved with deliberate patterns, bones arranged in a way that suggests the builder had a plan and executed it over a long time. The Bone Citadel is the highest-stakes dungeon in the mid-tier game: extremely difficult, long, and designed for coordinated six-person groups. The Ogre clans will not go near it. The reason they give involves what their grandparents saw when they first tried.

**Named:** The Architect (level 40 — unknown type; the thing that built it) — rare loot table with highest-tier mid-game equipment; Bone Carving schematic only available from this source.

## Services
- Warlord's Camp (western Ogre settlement, after the questline establishes access): Warrior and Shadow Knight trainers; Bone Carving advanced trainer; raw material vendors
- Troll Arroyo Markets: Skinning and Tanning trainers; Deepwater Fishing accessible along the arroyo system (rare ingredients)
- Half-Ogre waystation (mid-zone): neutral-ish; vendors; basic services; the safest resting point in the zone

## Faction Notes
Three factions: Ogre Clan Alliance, Troll Arroyo Families, Half-Ogre Free Settlements. Each is independent; raising one does not affect others. All three start at wary for outside characters.

The fastest trust-building with all three involves the Bone Citadel questline — the Architect is a shared threat, and the factions will work together to address it even when they are not working together on anything else. This is one of the rare cross-faction collaboration questlines in the game.

Good-aligned characters can operate here freely. The factions here do not care about alignment tier. They care about demonstrated behavior. A Paladin who has helped the Ogre clans is more welcome than a Warrior who hasn't.

## Durrath's Temple

The main temple of Durrath is carved into the north face of the largest mesa in the western Badlands, overlooking the inter-clan neutral zone. It has been there longer than any of the current Ogre, Troll, or Half-Ogre communities — predating the oldest Ogre oral tradition by approximately two hundred years. The communities adapted their territory around its presence. They do not worship at it, but they treat it as neutral ground in the same way they treat the Half-Ogre waystation: somewhere the rules of inter-clan conflict do not apply.

The temple is open to anyone who arrives without weapons. The entry stair is carved directly into the mesa face — 140 steps, no railing. This is not a safety hazard designed as a test; it is a staircase designed for the Architects' proportions. The Durrath priests rebuilt it for human-scale use approximately eighty years ago. The proportions still feel slightly wrong.

The current Path-Elder in residence is named Solen-Vekt — a Half-Ogre, which the temple has had exactly twice in its history, both times during periods when the inter-clan conflicts were worst and the Half-Ogre community needed someone with temple standing to be heard. He has been in the Badlands for thirty-one years and at this temple for fourteen. He does not give sermons. He answers questions if asked and trains if asked and otherwise practices, which is a better demonstration of Durrath's theology than anything he could say about it.

The Anvil — the highest Durrath position — has not left the inner chamber in twelve years. There is one. Whether the current Anvil will leave is not a question anyone at the temple will answer, which is itself an answer.

## The Calibration Tower

In the northeastern Badlands, partially collapsed, is an Architect site the local communities call "the broken finger" — a tower approximately sixty feet tall at its current height, with evidence of original height closer to two hundred. The structure is Architect construction: fitted stone, no mortar, six-fingered load-bearing marks at the base.

The Walking Name Order knows what it is: a signal relay in the Architect network, designed to amplify and transmit survey data from surrounding nodes. It is partially collapsed, which means its function is compromised. Whether the compromise is total or partial is unknown — no one has successfully entered the tower's upper levels, which collapsed inward rather than outward and now form a rubble chamber that has not been cleared.

The Ogre clans treat it as a territorial boundary marker between two clan territories. This is not its original purpose. No one has told them otherwise.

**Player access:** Characters who reach the Calibration Tower can survey its exterior for Architect notation (partially legible). The exterior survey provides a damaged node report that, combined with other node reports, advances the Architect questline. Entering the collapsed interior is a level-38+ challenge requiring engineering knowledge (Tinkering skill 80+) or brute physical solutions. The interior, if reached, contains the most complete Architect transmission schedule found outside the Dominion Vault.

## The Bone Citadel — Expanded

The thing that built the Bone Citadel is not named "The Architect" by anyone who has been inside it — the dungeon's final boss is listed as such in the game's encounter log, which is a player-facing label added by the Breach Wardens' dungeon survey team approximately forty years ago, who named it descriptively and have not revisited the name. The Walking Name Order has concerns about the label that they have not successfully communicated to the Wardens.

What the thing is: older than the mesa communities, present in the western Badlands since at least Year 300 CR based on Ogre oral tradition. It collects bones in a specific pattern — the pattern appears in partial form in Architect notation at two other sites, without context for what the pattern means. Whether the Citadel's builder has any connection to the Architect network or is simply operating on a similar mathematical basis by convergence is one of the Walking Name Order's unresolved questions. Drathis Vehn has been to the Citadel exterior once and declined to enter.

The bones used in the Citadel's construction are too large for local fauna. Some of them are too large for any documented fauna. The Bone Carving schematic that drops from the boss produces items with properties that Tinkerers and Alchemists find interesting for reasons they cannot fully explain — the material seems to respond to crafting intentions in ways standard bone does not.

## Key NPCs

**Solen-Vekt** — Half-Ogre Monk, level 30; Path-Elder at Durrath's temple. The closest thing in the Badlands to a zone quest giver who is not faction-specific. He gives quests related to Durrath's theology — tests of discipline, not tasks of violence — that pay out in standing with all three Badlands factions simultaneously, because all three factions respect the temple's neutrality. Players who complete his questline receive the Iron-Sworn mark, which provides a minor stat bonus in all Eastern Badlands zones.

**Warlord Dresh** — Ogre Warrior, level 40 (non-hostile if approached correctly). Controls the eastern high ground and is the accessible named boss in the zone. After the Bone Citadel questline establishes the shared threat, she becomes a faction contact rather than a fight. She knows everything about the Badlands that Ogre oral tradition covers and is willing to share it in exchange for demonstrated usefulness to her clan. She has been to the Bone Citadel exterior three times and has refused entry each time on grounds she describes as "I can count." She will not elaborate.

**Trade-Caller Rhoss** — Half-Ogre civilian, level 1 (vendor). Runs the Half-Ogre waystation. The only zone vendor who will buy anything from anyone without asking where it came from. This is a deliberate policy. She has opinions about the Bone Citadel that she will share if players buy something first, because she learned that giving opinions for free makes people argue with them.

## Cross-Zone Connections

- **Durrath** — The main temple is here; characters pursuing the Durrath deity questline must visit this zone; the Iron Vigil at the main temple counts for game-tracked purposes
- **Architect network** — The Calibration Tower is a network node; its survey data is required for the complete node report set
- **Khala Savannahs** — Adjacent zone; Ogre and Troll communities have trading relationships with the Felhari; the hostile minor clan's questline involves both zones
- **Ashfang Hold** — The starting zone for Ogre, Troll, and Half-Ogre characters; characters from those races have a mild standing advantage in the Badlands when they arrive

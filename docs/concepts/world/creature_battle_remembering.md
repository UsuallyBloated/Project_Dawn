# The Battle-Remembering
*Creature/Faction lore — The Ashen Marches (Levels 30–45)*

---

## What They Are

The Battle-Remembering are the dead of the Dominion War who chose to remain. Not raised — not Vethis-claimed in the manner of undead who are called back against their will or raised by outside intent. The Battle-Remembering stayed because the battle never resolved in a way they could leave.

This is a meaningful distinction. Undead of the conventional type are Vethis's property, leased back to the living for whatever purpose the raising required. The Battle-Remembering are not conventional undead. Vethis has a claim; whether the claim holds is a theological dispute that has been ongoing for three hundred years between the Durrath and Vethis traditions, without resolution, in part because neither tradition's representatives have been able to get the Battle-Remembering to acknowledge either claim consistently.

The Battle-Remembering do not acknowledge claims. They remember a battle.

---

## Appearance

Not rotting. Not shambling. The Battle-Remembering are in a state that has no good description because there is no experience of it before it is encountered: they are visible as soldier-shapes, present in the way that something burned very brightly into your perception of a space is present. Standing still, they look like something that has been in that exact position long enough that the position has become part of the landscape. Moving, they look like they are remembering how to move — the motion is slightly delayed from the intention, as though movement requires recall before it can be performed.

The armor they wore in death is part of what they project. Not the armor itself — the armor is gone, three hundred years gone — but the impression of the armor, the weight and shape of it. Two Battle-Remembering from different divisions of the Third Army are distinguishable by insignia that no one who was alive at the time is around to identify. Players who are Kel`varath and speak the old Dominion administrative dialect can read the insignia if they know the old order of battle. Players who do not know the old order of battle see insignia and know they are insignia.

They are not uniformly hostile. They fight players who enter the Marches with obvious hostile intent. They ignore players who enter without it. They will approach players who have been in the Marches long enough, or who behave in ways they recognize — specifically, players who treat the dead with appropriate consideration, which in practice means not looting near them without acknowledgment and not speaking dismissively about the battle in their presence. None of this is documented anywhere. Players work it out from outcomes.

## Why They Stayed

The Battle-Remembering stayed for different reasons. Three reasons are consistent enough across enough of them to be considered categories:

**The Battle Never Ended:** The largest group. Soldiers who understood, in dying, that the battle they were fighting had no conclusion — that the Ashen Marches would be contested ground for longer than they could imagine. They could not leave a battle that was still happening. They are still here because the battle, in the broad sense, is still happening. When the Marches are finally — if ever — resolved, this group is the one that would go. What resolution looks like for a three-hundred-year territorial conflict in a zone that has become economically organized around the conflict is a question the game does not answer.

**Someone Did Not Come Home:** A smaller group. Individual soldiers who stayed for a specific person — a companion, a sibling, a soldier they were responsible for whose body was not found and who cannot be confirmed dead. These are the hardest to reach and the most emotionally legible when reached. Players who learn Ghost Tongue can speak to them. What they want from the conversation varies.

**Waiting for Orders:** The most recently stayed group, and the most relevant to the Nameless Commander questline. Soldiers from the Third Army who were assigned to specific objectives that they did not complete before dying. They are still in position. Still waiting. The Nameless Commander is also still in position, still waiting — this is not a coincidence. The Commander's orders and the Battle-Remembering's objectives are related in ways the Commander does not know and the Battle-Remembering cannot articulate in any language except Ghost Tongue.

## Named Battle-Remembering

**The Rememberer-Who-Keeps-Count**
The one who tracks the dead. Has been counting for three hundred years. The count is current: when a soldier dies in the Marches today — any soldier, any faction — the Rememberer knows within an hour and adds it. The count is not accessible to players; the Rememberer does not share it in response to direct requests. Players who are Ghost Tongue 75+ and spend time near the Rememberer will, eventually, hear the counting. It goes on for a long time.

She is not interested in living visitors as potential allies or enemies. She is interested in one thing only: that the count is kept correctly. Players who bring her documentation of dead soldiers from the Marches — specifically, documentation that confirms deaths she has not been able to confirm — advance her questline. She responds to this with something that functions like gratitude, which in Ghost Tongue lands differently from expected.

**The Unfinished**
The one most interested in what players know from elsewhere. He stayed specifically because he never found out whether what he fought for was achieved. He was not a senior soldier — his objective was minor, in the strategic scope — but it was his objective and he has been trying to determine whether it was worth it for three hundred years. He has no way to find out from where he is. Players who have been to the Dominion ruins, the Ashen Coast, or the Convergence Point carry information he cannot access. He will ask, in Ghost Tongue, whether players know anything about the Third Army's final objectives. This is one of the connecting threads between the Battle-Remembering questline and the Nameless Commander's story.

**Verath-in-the-Silence**
The teacher. Was a Bard in life. Learned, in the decades after death, how to communicate with living visitors — and then learned how to teach others to communicate with her. She is the only Battle-Remembering who will formally teach Ghost Tongue (skill 0 → 1). She does not teach to anyone who asks; she teaches to players who have completed the Battle-Remembering questline and who have, in the process, given the Battle-Remembering something they were missing. Her teaching method: a single session, approximately fifteen in-game minutes, in which she demonstrates the phonology, the structure, and the specific way Ghost Tongue's meaning arrives in the wrong order. Players who complete the session receive Ghost Tongue at skill 1.

She occasionally plays music that has no instrument producing it. Players who are Bards hear something slightly different from what other players hear. What they hear is not documented.

---

## Ghost Tongue and the Battle-Remembering

Ghost Tongue skill 0 means the sounds arrive but none of the meaning does. At skill 25, some words come through; at 50, roughly half; at 75, most; at 100, everything. The Battle-Remembering speak in Ghost Tongue when they choose to communicate, which means players without any Ghost Tongue investment hear sounds and no meaning.

The documented property of Ghost Tongue — that understanding arrives in the wrong order — is most pronounced in conversations with the Battle-Remembering, because they are not organizing their meaning for a living listener's temporal processing. They are expressing what they know in the order that they know it, which for three-hundred-year-old dead is not necessarily chronological or causal. Players who have reached Ghost Tongue 75+ and are speaking with the Unfinished report that he answers questions they have not asked yet, because he has determined from context what they came to ask.

## The Questline

**Entry:** Enter the Marches without obvious hostile intent. Stay long enough. The approach works best from the direction of the Third Army Records Tent — players who have spoken with the Nameless Commander carry an impression of that conversation that the Battle-Remembering recognize.

**Phase 1 — Contact:** A Battle-Remembering acknowledges the player. This is the first time they have spoken to a living visitor in a way that registers as meaningful. The acknowledgment is in Ghost Tongue; players with skill 0 hear sounds; players with skill 1+ hear something they can partially parse.

**Phase 2 — The Missing Records:** The Rememberer-Who-Keeps-Count has gaps in her documentation — specific soldiers whose deaths she has not been able to confirm. These are players who can be found through zone quests: the deaths are recorded in documents accessible at the Breach Wardens' records posts, in the Iron Camp and Compact camp archives, and in one case in the Dominion Vault's lower records. Gathering these and returning them advances the questline.

**Phase 3 — The Unfinished's Question:** He asks whether the Third Army's final objective was achieved. This requires players to have information from the Nameless Commander's questline. Players who have completed the Commander's story and can tell the Unfinished what they know complete this phase. If the Commander's story has not been completed, this phase is unavailable — but it is also not blocking; the questline proceeds on the other track.

**Phase 4 — Verath teaches Ghost Tongue:** Upon completing Phase 2 and at least one of the two Phase 3 paths, Verath-in-the-Silence offers the teaching session.

**Phase 5 — Resolution (long-form):** Players who complete the Commander's questline and the Battle-Remembering questline in the same playthrough, in either order, find that the two resolutions interact. The specific interaction depends on how the Commander's questline was resolved. In all versions: some of the Battle-Remembering who were in the "Waiting for Orders" category acknowledge that the orders have been addressed. They do not immediately leave — three hundred years of staying is not dissolved in a moment — but they become less present. Some of them are seen, by players who return to the Marches later, further from the battlefield than they were before.

## The Theological Dispute

Durrath's tradition claims those who died in discipline and did not stop. Vethis's tradition claims all the dead. The Battle-Remembering are the largest single population in the world that both traditions point to simultaneously.

The Vethis Accountant in Greyveil has the Battle-Remembering logged in the Ledger as "active — unresolved status." This is the Ledger's way of noting that Vethis has recorded the deaths and is waiting for the situation to clarify. The Ledger does not resolve ambiguous cases by fiat; it records them and waits. Three hundred years of waiting is, in the Ledger's scope, not particularly long.

The Durrath Path-Elders in the Eastern Badlands refer to the Battle-Remembering in training discussions as "those who have not stopped." They consider this evidence for their position. The Vethis priests consider it evidence for theirs. The Battle-Remembering, when asked in Ghost Tongue which tradition they belong to, respond in a way that Ghost Tongue 100 players parse as "that question is not about us." This response has not resolved the dispute.

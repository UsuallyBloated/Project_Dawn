# Plate II — The Western Heartlands

*Cartographer's Hall, Aelindra · Filed Stillmonth, Year 847 of the Current Reckoning*
*Principal hand: Archivist Valex · Field corroboration: Wardenwood Ranger Lodge · Greyveil Council Surveyors · Halfmark Smallholders' Roll · Whisperbough Border Watch*
*Re-issued at the broadsheet scale, Stillmonth 847 CR — supersedes the earlier impression of the same season; the Blighted Wood interior is given the room the field reports require*

---

## Prefatory Note

This plate is the first of the regional sheets. It zooms upon the western half of the continent — the Greenshire farmland, the body of Ardenmoor forest, the high-elf city of Aelindra in the deep north of that forest, the Blighted Wood east of Ardenmoor, the Greyfen corridor, and Greyveil at the river confluence.

The earlier impression of this plate was found, on review, to crowd the Blighted Wood into a sliver of corrupted ground at Ardenmoor's eastern edge. The wood's actual extent is greater than that, and its interior — Rotwood Hollow, the Heartwood, the Deep Rooms beneath, the named features the Whisperbough Border Watch reports against — was reduced to a margin note. The canvas has been extended further east-and-south, Aelindra has been re-set into the top-right corner where its city plan has the room it needs, and the Blighted Wood is now drawn at the regional scale the rest of the plate uses.

The scale is roughly three league marches per grid square — about three times finer than Plate I. Settlements that appeared on Plate I as a star at their zone's bracket label are here resolved into their constituent hamlets, lodges, and named landmarks.

## Marginalia

### What This Plate Resolves That Plate I Did Not

The Atlas plate and Plate I both treated each Heartlands zone as a single bracketed area with a star or two and a margin note. The realities on the ground are denser. A field team travelling from Greenshire to Aelindra passes through, in order: Brookfield's smallholders, the Sable's Run crossing at Pike's Mill, the Old Oak waystone where the road forks, the Halfmark Hamlets, the Greengate toll station, the eastern road's climb out of the farmland, the Witherbeck Stone marking the Ardenmoor border, the Heartwood Path, Millhaven, the Wood Elf Council Tree, and the Aelindra outer walks before reaching the city itself. At Plate I scale these are seven characters of horizontal space; here they are the journey they actually are.

### The Blighted Wood — Why This Plate Was Re-Issued

The earlier impression of this plate rendered the Blighted Wood as a sliver of corrupted ground at Ardenmoor's eastern edge with a single margin note pointing to Plate I. The Whisperbough Border Watch raised a polite but specific objection: the wood at survey is a substantial zone with an interior, and reducing it to ten characters of horizontal space in the ground between Ardenmoor and Aelindra is no kind of survey at all. The Hall has agreed.

The wood is rendered here at the same regional scale as Ardenmoor and Greenshire. Its named features are placed at the positions the Border Watch reports them, with the standard caveat that any feature near the Silent Edge or the advancing perimeter may have moved by the time the next field team reaches it. The features are:

- **The Old Witherwillow** — a tree at the western edge that should not still be standing. The blight reached it in Year 808 CR and the tree has not died, has not put out leaves since 808, and has not been investigated more closely than rangers are willing to investigate it.
- **The Sundered Path** — the central trail. It was the Ranger patrol road before Year 807 CR and has remained walked. The Wardenwood Lodge does not staff it, does not investigate it, and does not discuss what walks it.
- **Rotwood Hollow** — the central depression. The blight is densest at this point. The soil temperature is consistently four degrees above ambient, which is the temperature signature the Hall uses at confirmed Architect remainder sites elsewhere.
- **The Heartwood** — the wood's central tree. Twelve hundred years old by ring count; the Deep Rooms sit beneath it. The tree itself is not visibly corrupted, which is itself a significant observation.
- **The Deep Rooms** ✧ — probable Architect site, beneath the Heartwood; the only Architect site in the Heartlands without an identified surface entry. The notation that confirms its existence was recovered at the Sunken Ziggurat, half a continent away, which is the kind of detail the Hall finds significant.
- **The Wraithwell** — a pool of black water at the eastern rim that reflects nothing. The Wood Elves have given it a fourth name in their inner-language; the previous three are no longer spoken in Whisperbough.
- **The Rotwood Lodge** ☠ — abandoned Year 807 CR. The contents were not recovered. The Ranger guild's standing position on what was left behind is silence.
- **The Withering Stand** — the trees that died first. They have not fallen and nothing nests in them.
- **The Silent Edge** — the boundary at which birdsong stops. Moves westward at the same rate as the visible blight. Was not previously rendered on any plate; the Hall added it after the second Border Watch report insisted the silence was a discrete and survey-able feature, not just an absence.
- **The Border Cairns** — the Wood Elf monitoring stations. Nine confirmed; the Watch re-walks them every six weeks.

### Greenshire — The Hamlets in Detail

The Halfmark Hamlets are four distinct settlements: **Halfmark proper**, **Hayford**, **Cresswick**, and **Old Hollow**. They share a single elder council (the Halfmark Smallholders' Roll, which is also the property register the Hall references for survey purposes) but maintain separate fields, mills, and pottery kilns. The cluster is treated as a single ☆ glyph on this plate because at three leagues per grid square they begin to overlap; a finer plate would resolve them individually.

**The Three Ponds** is the largest single village in Greenshire and the only one with a maintained Halfmark Hamlets fishing fleet — five flat-bottomed punts, plus the larger Halfmark fishing boat that crosses to the deeper southern pond in the summer. The pottery kilns at the Three Ponds are the source of approximately three-quarters of the glazed earthenware in everyday use across the Western Heartlands, and the Halfling families that work them have done so since at least Year 580 CR.

**The Old Oak** is a waystone, not the tree it is named for. A tree of the appropriate species and approximate age stood at the road junction until Year 311 CR; the waystone replaced it after the storm-fall, and the road junction has been called *the Old Oak* ever since. The waystone is engraved on three faces with the league-distances to Greenshire's hamlets, Cogsworth's mountain pass, and Greyveil's western gate. The fourth face is blank. Various theories about why it is blank exist; the Hall does not endorse any of them.

### Ardenmoor — The Canopy Villages

The Wood Elf canopy villages are not, despite the singular bracket on Plate I, a single settlement type. Each of the four named villages has its own elder, its own ranger affiliation, and its own approach to the eastern blight. **Aelvenholt** in the north is the elder council seat and the diplomatic interface with Aelindra's Reckoning Stone scholars. **Threadleaf** is the central village and the cooperage and bowmaking centre — the longbows used by Aelindra's senior rangers are made here. **Whisperbough** is the eastern village and faces the blight directly; its bordering terrace has been re-walked five times in the past forty years as the blight has advanced, and the Whisperbough Border Watch is the standing field team that monitors it. **Stagheart Glade** in the south is the ceremonial ground for ranger initiation rites and is the only one of the four where a non-Wood Elf ranger may be honoured rather than merely tolerated.

The **Wardenwood Lodge** is the working headquarters of the Ranger guild that recruits across all of Ardenmoor, including the canopy villages. Six master rangers are in residence at any given time; the lodge maintains its own range and sets the boundary patrol schedule. The Lodge does not formally answer to any of the canopy villages, though it consults with all of them and is housed on Wood Elf-permitted ground.

### Aelindra — The Six Spires

The caster tradition guildhalls of Aelindra are the **Six Spires**, one per discipline: **Evocation**, **Alteration**, **Conjuration**, **Abjuration**, **Divination**, and **Channeling**. They are arranged in a hexagonal court around the Inner Glade, with the Heartwood at the centre. The plate renders them in their hexagonal arrangement with the Heartwood and the Dreaming Grove descent at the centre of the figure. The Sealed Archive sits between the Spires of Divination and Abjuration, which the Hall takes to be deliberate placement and not coincidence. The Reckoning Stone, the Crown of Stars, and the Hall of Records are along the Outer Walks at the Inner Glade's periphery.

The Dreaming Grove descent is at the foot of the Heartwood, beneath the Inner Glade. The plate marks it with a ▼ at the position where the descent surfaces; the Grove proper is below ground and is a distinct dungeon for survey purposes. The Dreaming Warden is in residence; visitors who wish to consult the Warden petition through the Hall of Records.

### The Greyfen — The Foundation Ring's Surfacing Edge

The Greyfen's interior detail centres on the four named outcrops where the Foundation Ring's stonework has surfaced from peat. The **Centre Outcrop** is the warmest and is the one most consistent with the temperature of confirmed Architect remainders elsewhere on the continent — it is the only direct surface evidence that the Foundation Ring is a remainder-bearing site rather than merely a structural foundation. The **East Outcrop** carries the only legible Architect notation panel on the surface in the Heartlands; field teams have transcribed it three times and the transcriptions disagree in non-trivial ways, which the Hall takes as further evidence that the panel's legibility is partial. The **South Outcrop** is closest to Greyveil's city wall and is the position the City Council's denial of the Foundation Ring's existence is most obviously contradicted by. The Council's response when this is pointed out is to repeat the denial.

### Greyveil — The Six Gates and the Threefold Bridge

The **Wallflowers** are the curtain-wall districts; the wall is six-gated, and the districts inside each gate are named for the gate they serve. From north clockwise: **Highgate**, **Eastgate**, **Trunkgate** (where the East Trunk Road exits), **Southgate**, **Hollowgate** (the river-mouth gate, by the Wharves), and **Greengate** (which is also the name of the Greenshire toll station, which is a separate place; the duplication has been the subject of three formal complaints to the Hall, none of which the Hall has been willing to enforce a resolution to).

The **Threefold Bridge** is the single load-bearing crossing of the river confluence and consists of three spans named **Ardenwise** (north-to-centre, over the Sable's Run), **Greenward** (west-to-centre, over the Ardenmere), and **Eastreach** (centre-to-east, over the combined Greymere). The Threefold Bridge is the only point at which a wagon may cross the confluence without unloading; the alternative is the river ferry, which does not run in storm-season.

### Visual Character — for the Chief Artist

The Western Heartlands plate is a study in greens with one wound. The palette runs from the pale honey-gold and hedge-green of the Greenshire ploughland, through the deep layered green of Ardenmoor's canopy, to the silver-green of Aelindra's spire-court in the deep forest — and then the **blight**, which is the plate's argument: a grey-violet stain advancing into all that green, its western edge hard, its interior fading to ash tones around Rotwood Hollow.

The signature visuals — for the artist's notebook:

- **The blight boundary** — the single most important line on the plate. West of it, full green; east of it, grey-violet and the Withering Stand's bare trunks. The Silent Edge may be suggested as a faint second line where the wash thins — the boundary where the birdsong stops.
- **Aelindra's hexagon** — the Six Spires in their hexagonal court with the Heartwood at centre; render as a jewel of ordered geometry inside the organic forest mass.
- **The Greyfen** — peat-brown and reed-grey between forest and river meadow, with the four Foundation Ring outcrops as small gold marks half-sunk in the bog.
- **Greyveil at the confluence** — the Threefold Bridge's three spans where two rivers become one; the six-gated wall; the city is the plate's anchor-weight at the south.
- **Sable's Run** — drawn warm: a thin steam-line over the upper river where Cogsworth's drainage heats it.

### What This Plate Does Not Resolve

- **Aelindra's Spires at street level.** Each Spire is treated as a single ⊙. A finer plate (Plate II-A, in preparation) would resolve the Spires into their constituent halls, libraries, and instruction rooms. The Hall has begun the survey but the Spire of Divination has objected to certain specifics of the methodology, and the matter is in arbitration.
- **Greyveil's Old Quarter at street level.** The Old Quarter's streets do not follow any plan — they grew where they grew and were paved where they were paved. A street-plan exists in the Council's records and is not accurate to what is currently on the ground. The Hall has elected to render the Old Quarter as a single ◊ rather than commit a known-incorrect detail to plate.
- **The blight's exact eastern boundary.** As noted on Plate I, the blight advances; the position rendered here is current to Stillmonth 847 CR. The three field reports the Hall received in the past year disagree by approximately the same margin they disagree with this plate, and the matter remains open.

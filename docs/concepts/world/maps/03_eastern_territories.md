# Plate III — The Eastern Territories

*Cartographer's Hall, Aelindra · Filed Stillmonth, Year 847 of the Current Reckoning*
*Principal hand: Archivist Valex · Field corroboration: Harrowmere Compact Maritime Office · Auroch's Rest Earthwork Council · Breach Warden Geographic Division · Drowned Hold Expedition Records, Vols. I–III*

---

## Prefatory Note

This plate is the second of the regional sheets and zooms upon the eastern half of the continent — the Harrow Strait and the port of Harrowmere, the offshore Drowned Hold on its Architect seamount, the Minotaur grassland of Auroch's Rest, the Eastern Badlands with its three named mesas and two Architect sites, the Ogre warlord camp at Ashfang Hold, and the contested siege ground at the Breach.

The southern edge of the plate adjoins the Ashen Marches and the Khala Savannahs. Those zones are the subject of Plate IV. The eastern edge of the Badlands fades into uncharted scrubland for which the Hall has no field data; the dotted line at the eastern margin marks the limit of confirmed survey.

The scale is roughly three league marches per grid square, the same as Plate II. Harrowmere's districts are resolved as on Greyveil. The Drowned Hold's floors are laid out as a vertical inset because the structure does not render usefully in plan view.

## Marginalia

### Harrowmere — The Ports Inside the Port

Harrowmere does not have a port. It has six. The **Compact Docks** are the main commercial port and the place most travellers see; they handle 80% of the registered cargo turnover. The **Old Mole** is the oldest dock and is now used primarily for fishing vessels; the timbers were salvaged from a Dominion-era vessel beached on the Grey Spit in Year 312 CR and have been rebuilt twice since, both times retaining the salvaged keel timber on the ground that throwing it away would be unlucky. The **Stranger's Quay** is where unregistered vessels berth pending Compact review; the review's average duration is six days, the median is three, and the longest on Compact record is forty-one years (the vessel is still there, it is registered now, but the registration is provisional pending answers the master is no longer alive to give). The **Drowned Hold Approach** is a single licensed pier and admits only Compact-licensed expedition craft. The **Hollowgate quay** at the river-mouth is the small-vessel and Fish Market harbour. The **Lighthouse on the Grey Spit** is not a port but is mentioned here because the lighthouse-keepers' guild is technically a port authority body in Compact charter and refuses to be removed from the list.

### The Drowned Hold — The Floors

The Hold's vertical inset on the plate is the result of three licensed expeditions. Each was permitted by the Compact under standing license terms (one season per expedition; recovery 50/50 split with the Compact; safe return is the licensee's responsibility). All three returned with the personnel they sent, and none returned with the cargo to make the expedition profitable.

- **Expedition I** (Year 813 CR) reached the Signal Room (Floor IV) and turned back due to losses. The Necromancer who accompanied the expedition reported that the garrison did not respond to standard override signals, which the Hall now considers established rather than provisional.
- **Expedition II** (Year 824 CR) reached the Armoury (Floor III) and turned back due to the garrison's behavioural pattern, which the expedition's Witch Hunter described in his return statement as "purposeful in a way that undead are not, ordinarily, purposeful."
- **Expedition III** (Year 838 CR) reached Floor V and did not file a complete report. The harbormaster's incident record reads "came back short of supplies and short of conversation." The notation rubbing the expedition recovered from Floor V reads, in Architect script, **PENDING: FINAL CALIBRATION**. The Hall has filed three separate interpretation memoranda and the dispute remains active.

The seamount level (Floor VI) has not been reached. The Hall takes no position on what is on it. The Architect notation at the Quiet Dig in the Underdark Crossing references a network node "in the sheltered water, western approach"; the Hall takes the position that this is the Drowned Hold and does not warrant the inference further.

### Auroch's Rest — The Cairn and the Stone

The Hall has been corrected, more than once, on the distinction between **Auroch's Cairn** and **the Founding Stone**. The Cairn is a heaped marker at a particular position; the Stone is the rock-formation the herd-elders sat upon in Year 622 CR when they settled the founding question. The Cairn marks the place. The Stone is the object. The herd-elders' insistence that these are different things is not a misunderstanding on the Minotaur's part and the Hall has stopped attempting to render them as a single landmark.

The **Memorial Hill** is the slope on which the older monuments stand. The herd has not added to it for sixty years. The reason is known to the herd-elders and not to the Hall; the Hall has asked, and the answer was, in summary, that the asking was the thing that was not the herd's business.

### The Eastern Badlands — The Three Mesas

The three named mesas — **Durrath's Mesa**, **the Skullback**, and **the Cracked Crown** — are the Badlands' navigational reference points and are the only features visible at distance during the summer haze. The Ogre clans' communication network depends on line-of-sight signalling between the Skullback's flat summit and the Cracked Crown's broken one; the signals are flag-and-fire and the meanings are clan-specific, which has resisted the Hall's attempts at compilation.

**Durrath's Mesa Temple** is carved into the mesa's north face and is the centre of Ogre liturgical practice. It predates the current Ogre communities. The current liturgy is the Ogre clans'; what the temple was used for before is on record nowhere the Hall has been able to access.

The **Calibration Tower** is collapsed, separate from the Bone Citadel, and unsurveyed in its interior. The Ogre clans treat it and the Citadel as territorial markers for separate purposes — the Citadel demarcates the western frontier, the Tower demarcates a route the clans do not walk and consider a clan-internal matter rather than a survey matter. Both positions appear on this plate.

The **Bone Citadel's glyph is corrected in this impression** from ✦ (confirmed Architect) to ✧ (probable). The Citadel is anomalous — the bones are too large for any local fauna, and the structure has stood since Year 300 CR at minimum — but anomalous is not Architect. No Architect notation, no remainder warmth, and no network cross-reference has been recovered there, and the network catalogue on Plate VIII has never carried it. The earlier ✦ was set by a hand that read *predates the current communities* as *Architect*, which is an inference the Hall no longer makes. The Walking Name Order concurs with the downgrade and notes, separately, that the question of what the Citadel is instead remains open, and that the Order finds the open question more interesting than the closed one.

### The Breach — Six-Hour Windows and the Gate-Key

The Breach is a contested zone with structurally engineered alternation: control of the **Gate** rotates on six-hour windows, and the holder of the Gate-Key — there is exactly one — controls the rotation. The Key has not been stolen, dropped, or otherwise lost since the structure was put in place; transfers occur when a faction takes the Gate from the holder, after which the Key changes hands by a procedure that is the Gate-Warden's responsibility to enforce.

The current holder is the **Vanguard**. The last transfer was Year 831 CR (Iron Camp → Vanguard). Six-hour control windows are accordingly Vanguard-favoured at the current rotation, with the Iron Camp's six hours occurring twice daily. The Iron Camp's standing position is that this is intolerable; the Vanguard's standing position is that the Iron Camp may take the Gate at any time the Iron Camp is willing to take the Gate. The matter has not resolved.

The **Gate-Warden's Tower** is the neutral structure between the camps. The Warden's operational age is unconfirmed and has been unconfirmed since the position was established. Whether the Warden is the same individual it has been since the structure was put in place is a question the Hall does not consider answerable from the available data.

### Ashfang Hold — The Movable Camp

Ashfang Hold is, in the technical sense, not a hold. The **warlord's seat** — Ashfang Camp itself — is movable and relocates approximately every two years; the only fixed structure is the **Warlord's Tower**, a single tower of Dominion-era stone the Camp orbits. The Tower's age and provenance are pre-Ashfang and the Tower has been there longer than any current Ogre clan can document; the warlord lineage uses it as the legitimacy anchor for the title, on the ground that whichever warlord sits at the Tower is the warlord.

The **Trader's Walk** is the only ground on which outsiders are permitted. It runs from the western edge of the camp to the Tower. Outsiders may not leave the Walk for any purpose. The penalty for doing so is the Pit; the penalty for surviving the Pit is the next Pit; the penalty for surviving three Pits is, at the warlord's discretion, an offer of bondsmanship to the warlord. Three outsiders are on Compact record as having taken the offer in the past century. None have left.

### Visual Character — for the Chief Artist

The Eastern Territories plate divides at its waist. The north is maritime — the cold grey-blue of the Greymere softening into the sheltered slate-calm of Harrow Strait, Harrowmere's roofs and wharves in weathered timber and salt-bleached grey, the Drowned Hold a dark vertical silhouette on its seamount with the garrison standard still raised. The south is dry — the open gold-green of Auroch's Rest grassland giving way to the rust-red clay and broken rock of the Badlands, with the three mesas as the horizon's anchors.

The signature visuals — for the artist's notebook:

- **The Drowned Hold inset** — the vertical section is the plate's centrepiece artifact: six floors descending from the raised standard to the unreached seamount level, the lower floors darkening, Floor VI rendered in near-black with the gold ✦ at its base and *PENDING: FINAL CALIBRATION* in the Architect's script.
- **Harrowmere's six ports** — a working harbour's clutter: the Compact Docks' order, the Old Mole's age, the Stranger's Quay's motley, the lighthouse on the Grey Spit.
- **The Breach** — two fortified camps glaring across a neutral rim, trebuchets north and bombards south, the Gate-Warden's Tower alone between them.
- **The three mesas** — Durrath's Mesa with the temple carved into its north face, the flat-topped Skullback, the Cracked Crown with its lightning-scarred summit; the only verticals in the red waste.
- **Auroch's Rest** — earthwork halls and horn-marker cemeteries side by side, the Hornways fanning out in paved cattle-tracks; render the living quarter and the burial ground as one settlement, because they are.

### What This Plate Does Not Resolve

- **The Drowned Hold Floor V at survey detail.** The plate represents Floor V as a single line because the Hall does not have the data to render it otherwise. Expedition III's incomplete report is the entire surface of the available data.
- **The eastern margin of the Badlands.** The plate's eastern edge is the limit of confirmed survey. What is east of the Badlands is uncharted scrub for the Hall's purposes. The Ogre clans are presumed to have a working knowledge of it; the Hall has not asked.
- **The Gate-Warden's identity.** The plate shows the Tower; it does not name the Warden. The Hall has filed three queries on the matter at six-year intervals and received the same response each time, which is not an answer.

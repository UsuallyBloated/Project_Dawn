## Prefatory Note

This plate is the third of the starting-zone series. It zooms upon the Bonecourt — the Kel'varath jungle ruins in the southern reaches; the Bonewrights' first foothold; the place at which the Kel'varath returned to their ancestral ground after the Dominion's collapse and at which the contemporary Bonewright tradition was founded. The plate is rendered at approximately **half a league per grid square**. At this scale the Hostel resolves into its constituent halls, the Beginner Pyres into their seven named fires, the Lost Temple into the chambers that have been catalogued and the chambers that have not, the First Cairns into their numbered set, and the Memorial Path into the named waypoints.

The Bonecourt is unlike Greenshire and Ashfang in two important respects. First, the Bonecourt is not a community in the sense of villagers and farmers — it is a working tradition's first house, a foothold maintained by and for the Bonewrights, with all of the discipline, focus, and ritual ordering that implies. There is no marketplace; there is no inn for casual travellers; there are no children at play. The Bonecourt is a place where work is done. Second, the Bonecourt is built *on* an older site rather than being an older site itself — the **Lost Temple** the Bonewrights call an *antecedent* predates the Bonewright arrival of Year 583 CR by an unknown margin (Walking Name Order working estimate: pre-Dominion by minimum two centuries), and the Hostel was deliberately sited adjacent to it. The Bonewrights consider the antecedents their inheritance. The plate accordingly renders the Hostel in active detail and the Lost Temple in surveyed-and-unsurveyed detail; the line between is the line between the Bonewrights' working ground and their inherited ground, and the Bonewrights consider the distinction important.

The visual character of the Bonecourt is the deep-jungle palette: the green-black of cypress canopy, the green-grey of moss-and-lichen on cypress trunks, the wet black of standing water in the channels, the bone-white and sun-bleached pale of cypress-wood structures (cypress weathers to a luminous pale grey-white that is the Bonecourt's signature), the slow-drift smoke of the Beginner Pyres' working low fires, and the green-and-stone palette of the Lost Temple's overgrown surfaces — vines on dark Kel'varath stonework, with ferns at the bases and orchid-flowers at the upper reaches in late summer. The light is filtered green at noon and amber at sunset; the smell is wet earth, cypress sap, and the resin-smoke of the Pyres.

The chief artist's hand is asked to render the Bonecourt as the working religious-academic foothold it is. The Bonewrights are necromancers; the Bonewrights are also archivists, philosophers, and scholars; the necromancy is a discipline, and the Bonecourt is a discipline-house. The pyres are not torture-fires; they are practice ground for technique. The novices are students. The Master is a master in the academic sense before the practitioner sense. The plate notes the discipline. The artist should as well.

## Marginalia

### Visual Character — for the Chief Artist

The Bonecourt's palette is layered green and weathered pale. The principal tones:

- **Cypress canopy green-black** — the deep green-near-black of mature cypress at the canopy; the Bonewrights' working wood; the structural and archival timber of the Hostel.
- **Cypress wood weathered pale** — the luminous grey-white of cypress wood after a few years of exposure; the Hostel's halls weather to this colour and the colour is the Bonecourt's signature against the surrounding green.
- **Black-water dark** — the deep wet black of the channels; reflective when still; cuts of darker tone through the jungle floor.
- **Stone ash-grey** — the dark Kel'varath stonework of the Lost Temple; colder in tone than the cypress; the temple's outer walls show the stone in patches between the vine-and-fern overgrowth.
- **Vine-green and orchid-yellow** — the overgrowth on the Lost Temple's pending chambers' outer walls; orchid-flowers in late summer give a yellow-and-cream punctuation to the green.
- **Pyre smoke** — pale, slow, persistent; the Pyre Court is visibly active from the Hostel courtyard at all working hours; the smoke is a low column rather than a heavy plume because the pyres burn at low temperature.

The signature visual elements — for the artist's notebook — are:

- **The Hostel courtyard** seen from above is a square of seven cypress halls around a flagstone-paved court with an unlit firepit at centre. The roofs are pitched with cypress shingles, weathered pale; the walls are cypress-and-stone with cypress at the upper storey and stone at the lower (the Bonewrights' working stone is the same dark grey-black Kel'varath stone the Lost Temple is built of, quarried a half-day's march east at a pit the Bonewrights still work).
- **The Pyre Court** is a circular flagstone clearing with seven low stone pyre-platforms. Each platform is approximately knee-height to a Kel'varath, two strides across, with a square iron grate set in the stone. The Pyres burn at low temperature with cypress-resin and worked-bone fuel; the smoke is pale and the flame visible only as a ground-level glow.
- **The Memorial Path** is a packed-earth track winding through the cleared ground between the courtyard and the Lost Temple's northern face. Twelve cairns of Bonewright-master stones along it; each cairn is approximately waist-height to a Kel'varath, irregularly built (the stones come from twelve different settlements and do not share a building style), each marked with a single carved cypress-bark name-tag at its top.
- **The Lost Temple's Antecedent's Doorpost** is the principal visual statement of the inheritance the Bonewrights claim. The carving is a four-handed figure standing to the height of two Kel'varath at the Approach Hall's entrance; the figure is in low relief; the four hands hold a book, a flame, a key, and an open palm. The carving's deeper detail is partially obscured by lichen and orchid-growth that the Bonewrights tend with a careful trim every Plowsmonth.
- **The black-water channels** approaching the Hostel are reflective, narrow (six to ten feet across), and bordered by cypress on both sides whose roots reach into the water. Punts pole through them slowly; the iron-shod poles strike the channel-bottom with a sound carried by the water for some distance.

Costume notes: Bonewrights wear long unbleached cypress-cloth robes for working dress; over the robe, a cypress-cloth scapular with the wearer's working virtue embroidered at the breast (the four virtues — record, practice, access, welcome — are matched to the four hands of the Antecedent's Doorpost; each Bonewright takes one virtue at the third-season elevation and wears it for the rest of their career). The Master wears a single white braid down the back; the Librarian wears a cypress-bark sash; the Pyre-Master wears an iron pin in the shape of a single low flame; the Cairn-Keeper wears a stone pendant at the throat. Novices wear plain cypress-cloth without embroidery; their virtue is given at the third-season elevation, not before.

### The Three-Season Novice Curriculum, Detailed

For the artist's tutorial-flow visualisation, the standard Bonewright novice progression is as follows:

**First season (Plowsmonth–Heartmonth)** — *liturgy, language, and the antecedents*. The novice attends Veshra's Novice Hall sessions four hours daily, learns the Kel'varath ritual tongue's working vocabulary (approximately three thousand words), and is introduced to the Lost Temple's catalogued chambers as objects of study. The novice is permitted to enter Chambers I and II under the Cairn-Keeper's supervision and to attend the quarter-day reading sessions Eshrik conducts in the Naming Hall; the novice does not yet practice the discipline. The novice's daytime work is service — Refectory cleaning, Cypress Grove pole-bearing, Library shelf-keeping. The novice's evening work is study.

**Second season (Heartmonth–Lateharvest)** — *the discipline*. The novice begins to learn the discipline that underpins the working tradition — meditation at the Pyre Court while the Pyres are at their lowest burn, the working virtue of *practice* under Akkro's instruction, the standing-and-walking discipline that the Bonewrights consider preparatory to the practice proper. The novice walks the Memorial Path with Tanrik on the first Restday after the season's beginning. The novice is permitted to enter Chambers III and IV under the Watch-Captain's supervision; the novice does not yet practice the discipline at these chambers but observes a Master's working there once during the season.

**Third season (Lateharvest–Frostmonth)** — *the practice*. The novice's working ground is the Pyre Court at the appropriate Pyre for the season — the Second Pyre for the second-season novice, the Third Pyre for the third-season novice. Akkro supervises directly. The technique progression is: first month, the lighting and tending of low-temperature working flame; second month, the working of small craft at the flame; third month, the formal first-practice (a small, standardised piece of working that every Bonewright in the tradition has performed at this stage; the Library catalogues approximately eleven thousand instances, four hundred and seventy-six per season-completing novice over the Bonecourt's tenure).

**Elevation** — at the third season's close, the novice presents themselves at the Master's Hall on Restday morning with the Master's standing nine-seat council in attendance. The novice's working virtue is named — record, practice, access, or welcome — and the corresponding scapular embroidery is applied to the novice's robe at the elevation ceremony in the Hostel courtyard at midday. The elevation makes the novice a *recognised* Bonewright; the *full* Bonewright title is conferred at the end of a further three-year working period.

### Tutorial Geography — Quest Hooks

For the artist preparing visualisations of the tutorial flow at the Bonecourt:

**A new Bonewright arrives at the Hostel courtyard from the jungle road from the north.** They are met by **Veshra Tahl-Roek** (or by a designated novice if Veshra is teaching) and shown to the Visitors' Dormitory until their first-season formal welcome, which is held at the next Restday morning council. The first conversation is with Master Sereth in the Master's Hall.

**Master Sereth's first conversation** establishes the novice's reason for arrival, the introduction-set the novice carries (typical: a letter from the Marshlands council, a Walking Name Order recommendation, or an informal Bonewright recommendation from another tradition-house), and the Bonecourt's expectations for the first season. Sereth's tone is academic — the conversation is an intake, not a vow.

**Veshra sets the first-season tutorial work.** Refectory cleaning, Cypress Grove pole-bearing, Library shelf-keeping. The novice is introduced to **Olis Brech** (Refectory), **Tarrik of the Slow Axe** (Grove), and **Eshrik of the Long Tongue** (Library) in their respective working areas. The Library introduction is the most formal — Eshrik conducts a brief reading session with the new novice as the working assistant, which is the novice's first contact with the cypress-bark scrolls.

**Tanrik Voll-Esh** sets the Memorial Path walk for the first-season novice's Restday morning. The walk takes approximately two hours; Tanrik recites the master-names at each cairn; the novice is expected to remember three master-names from the walk for recitation at the next Restday's evening Refectory meal. This is the Bonecourt's first formal test.

**Druvik Hess-Olen** sets the Watchmen's introduction at the second-season threshold. The novice accompanies a perimeter patrol around the Lost Temple's outer walls; Druvik points out the catalogued and pending chamber walls, the Watchmen's standing positions, the rotated-remnant detection markers (small carved cypress posts at known emergence points), and the Watch's working signal-system. The novice is now permitted to attend Chamber I and II under supervision.

**Akkro Vellis-Korn** sets the second-season Pyre Court introduction. The novice's first task is meditation at the Pyre Court while the Pyres are at their lowest burn — pre-dawn, before Akkro lights the First Pyre. The meditation lasts thirty minutes and is supervised by Akkro from the Pyre Court Shrine. This is the novice's first contact with the working flame.

**The third-season practice** is the principal tutorial culmination. The novice, having earned their second-season working ground, takes their seasonal Pyre and begins the discipline's working craft. This is the Bonewright tradition's first formal practice of necromancy, conducted at the lowest temperature, with the most-supervised guidance, on the most-prepared ground in the tradition. A character who completes the third-season practice is a recognised Bonewright at the end of the season; the Bonecourt's tutorial sequence is then complete and the character is ready for the broader Bonewright working life — at the Marshlands council, at Ixareth-Kul, at the deeper Architect-chain work that the Coalition Remnant and Walking Name Order liaisons begin to pull the new Bonewright into.

**Visitor-quests are available at intervals.** A character of any class who arrives at the Bonecourt as a visitor (not a novice) is housed in the Visitors' Dormitory and may take working assignments from Master Sereth — typically Library work for those with reading-tradition skills, Watchmen accompaniment for those with combat skills, Cypress Grove rounds for those with tracking skills. Visitor-quests are a viable tutorial path for any tutorial-aged character whose home class is *not* Necromancer but who has reason to be at the Bonecourt — Walking Name Order junior surveyors, Bonewright-friendly Witch Hunters, Coalition Remnant junior couriers, and the like.

### What This Plate Does Not Resolve

- **The Lost Temple's pending chambers (VI–XI).** Sealed; identified by survey only; not yet excavated. The plate marks the Sealed Stair (VI) and the Bone Court (VII) at their resonance-located positions but does not render their interiors because the interiors have not been entered. The four Far Chambers (VIII–XI) are marked similarly.
- **The Antecedent's identity.** The four-handed figure at the Antecedent's Doorpost is the Library's working interpretation of the antecedent's working master, but the inscription naming the figure is not present on the Doorpost itself or at any location the Library has access to. The Bonewright tradition's working position is that the Antecedent is an antecedent and the question of identity is not the working question.
- **The reason for the Sealed Stair's standing closure.** The working position is that the Stair was sealed by the antecedents themselves and that the Bonewrights inherit the seal as part of the inheritance; opening the Stair would be the Marshlands council's call. No proposal has been made since Year 712 CR. The reason for the standing reluctance is not on plate.
- **The Mother Cypress's age and the Hostel's foundation reason.** The Mother Cypress predates the Lost Temple's working stratum. The Hostel was sited next to the Lost Temple deliberately. Whether the Hostel's siting was also a siting next to the Mother Cypress is a question Tarrik refuses to answer in print. Tarrik's position in conversation is that the Mother is the Mother and the question is not asked at the grove. The Hall has filed his position without endorsement.
- **The Bonewright tradition's relationship to the Bonecourt as opposed to the Marshlands.** The Bonecourt is the first foothold; the Marshlands council is the working contemporary centre of the tradition. Whether the Bonecourt is a junior or senior part of the tradition is a question the Hall does not adjudicate. The Bonecourt's working position is that the Bonecourt is the first house and that primacy is administrative rather than authoritative; the Marshlands council's working position is approximately the same. The two positions are mutually compatible and the relationship is functional.

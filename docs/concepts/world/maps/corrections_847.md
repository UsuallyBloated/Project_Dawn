# Corrections Sheet — Filed Before the Lateharvest Binding

*Cartographer's Hall, Aelindra · Filed Stillmonth, Year 847 of the Current Reckoning*
*Principal hand: Archivist Valex · Review hand: the Hall's binding reviewer, this season*

---

## Prefatory Note

The Repository binds the second-edition Atlas's broadsheet annex at Lateharvest 847 CR, and the Hall's custom is to file a corrections sheet before any binding seals — the errors a document admits before binding are corrections; the errors it admits after are editions. The following corrections have been applied to the plates and the Atlas as filed. The sheet records what changed and why, so that no future reader mistakes a correction for a revision of survey fact.

---

## Corrections Applied

### 1. The Atlas's edition line

The Atlas's prefatory note read *"This is the fourth edition of this atlas"* beneath a title page reading *Second Edition*. Both were true and neither said so: the Atlas in its current form is the second edition; the Hall's chartings of Valdis number four. The line now reads *"the second edition of this atlas, and the fourth charting of Valdis the Hall has issued."* The four-chartings detail is retained because it carries the observation that matters — each prior charting added one Architect site, and in three of those four cases the site had been in field notes for years before anyone recognised it.

### 2. The Bone Citadel's glyph — adjudicated

The Atlas surface map, Plate I, and Plate III carried the Bone Citadel at ✦ (confirmed Architect site). Plate VIII's network catalogue — the controlling document for the network — has never carried it. The discrepancy was raised at binding review and the Hall has adjudicated: **the Citadel's glyph is corrected to ✧ (probable) on all three sheets.**

The reasoning is carried in full in Plate III's marginalia and in brief here: the Citadel is anomalous — bones too large for any local fauna, standing since Year 300 CR at minimum — but no Architect notation, no remainder warmth, and no network cross-reference has been recovered there. The earlier ✦ was an inference from *predates the current communities*, which is not an inference the Hall makes any more. The confirmed network remains eleven sites. What the Citadel is instead remains open, and the Walking Name Order has asked that the Hall record the Order's interest in the open question, which the Hall now has.

### 3. Stale cross-references between plates

The eight regional plates were filed across a single season and several of them referred to one another as *in preparation* or *when issued*. All eight are issued. The cross-references on Plates I, III, and V are corrected to present tense. Plate VII's reference to a Maren regional sheet as "Plate IX" is corrected to **Plate X**, per the Repository's plate-numbering: the IX series is the starting-zone volume, and the Maren sheet — if a principal hand ever surveys the second continent — will be Plate X.

### 4. Plate VIII's prefatory miscount

The prefatory note promised three views and the plate delivers five (A — the geographic arc; B — the topology; C — the site catalogue; D — the Calibration State flow; E — the Survey Chain). The note now counts to five. The Hall observes that the plate most concerned with complete surveys should not have miscounted its own contents, and lets the observation stand as its own discipline.

### 5. Glyph and legend hygiene

- **Plate I:** the Greymere river between Greyveil and Harrowmere was drawn in the kept-road convention (═║). The label is corrected to carry the river glyph; the full redraw is deferred to the next impression. The river is a river. The barges have always known.
- **Plate III's legend** assigned ⌂ twice (farmstead / lodge, and camp). The entries are merged.
- **Plate IX-A** used ⛩ (trainer hall) for the Wardens' patrol post and the Wandering Pilgrim's Bench, neither of which trains anyone. Corrected to ⛯ and ※ respectively.
- **Plate IX-B's legend** defined ⛯ as the Warlord's Tower specifically; the glyph also marks the Eastward Watch. The entry now reads *tower / watch-platform*.
- **Plate IX-D's legend** assigned ★ twice (ranking figure, and chief-stone). The chief-stone entry is corrected to ⚐, matching the plate's own usage.
- **Plate IX-D** carried press-ghosts — a scatter of malformed border characters (®®) where the rough-rock glyph (##) belongs. The Hall does not know how they got in. They are out.

## Additions in This Impression (recorded, not corrected)

- **Cartouches.** Every plate now carries the rose and scale (or, for Plates VI and VIII, the gauge and the abstention). The six-fingered rose is adopted per the new Drafting Standards sheet.
- **Visual Character sections.** Plates I–VIII now carry the palette and signature-element notes that the starting-zone plates carried from filing — the painted impressions required them and the Hall should have seen that coming.
- **The lane schematic** on Plate VII, threading the lanes port-to-port the way a captain reads them.
- **Vignette volumes IX-B.i, IX-C.i, and IX-D.i** — the starting-zone plates' artist's-hand renderings, bringing Volume II to parity with Greenshire's IX-A.i.
- **The Drafting Standards sheet** and **the Table of Leagues**, filed as annex appendices.

## Filed for the Lore-Keeper's Attention (no correction applied)

The binding review surfaced a pattern the Hall is not equipped to adjudicate: **recurring personal names across distant peoples.**

- **Vrekka** — of the Iron Eye (Ogre smith, Ashfang Hold) and of the Black Bellows (Kobold forge-master, the Deeps). Two smiths, two races, one name.
- **Threkka** — the Stone-Singer (Ogre shaman, Ashfang) and of the Quiet-Step (Kobold chief, Smokeback Pack).
- **Mireth** — Slow-Hand (Troll healer, Ashfang) shares her name with a deity of the canonical pantheon.
- **Kethri** — of-no-clan (Half-Ogre, Ashfang statement) and Kethri's Watch (a Felhari Spirit-Walker camp, the Khala).

The Hall files the recurrence as coincidence, with reservations. If the recurrence is deliberate — a naming tradition that crossed peoples, or a single older source both drew from — that is lore, and lore is not the Hall's desk. If it is not deliberate, the names are cheap to change before they accrete history, and expensive after. The Hall recommends the question be settled before the binding's successor edition, and notes that *Mireth* in particular will eventually be asked about by someone holding both documents.

*Principal hand: Archivist Valex · Field corroboration: Halfmark Smallholders' Roll · Three Ponds Innkeepers' Guild · Pike's Mill Pond Authority · Greengate toll office · Reverend Sister Rosalind Pell of the Greenwarden Shrine · the Outer Holders' Council of Nine · the Greenshire Wardens' standing patrol*

## Prefatory Note

This plate is the first of the starting-zone series. It zooms upon Greenshire — the Halfling and Human farmland in the western Heartlands, the gateway zone for most travellers' first journey, the ground that is *gentle, readable, and not entirely honest about what comes next*. The plate is rendered at approximately **half a league per grid square**, which is six times finer than the broadsheet's regional plates. At this scale the Halfmark Hamlets resolve into their four constituent settlements, the Three Ponds resolves into its three named ponds and inner lanes, Pike's Mill resolves into its mill, ponds, and miller's cottage, the cliff path to the southern coast resolves into its specific switchbacks, and the cottage smallholdings resolve into specific named farms with their boundaries shown.

Greenshire's visual character is the soft palette: pale honey-gold of late-summer wheat, the deep green of hedge oak, the grey-blue of stone wall, the smoke-grey of cottage chimney plumes drifting across patchwork fields. The Sable's Run river, descending from Cogsworth's industrial drainage, runs visibly warm through its upper section — steam rises from the millrace at Pike's Mill in the cool mornings of autumn. Halfling burrows are dug into south-facing slopes with round timber-and-stone door-frames and small front gardens. The roads are kept; the hedges are clipped; the apple orchards are pruned to the ground every six years on a schedule the Halfmark Smallholders' Roll keeps formal records of. The cliffs at the southern edge fall to the open Greymere — winds at the cliff-top carry the bite of salt; the standing surf gauges set Year 414 CR at the cliff base have marked a steady waterline through the present survey.

Greenshire is the world as most travellers first see it. It is not the world it appears to be. The Foundation Ring beneath Greyveil reaches almost to the Greenshire border. The smugglers' cove at the southern cliff has Compact-licensed traffic the smugglers themselves do not see. The Old Oak waystone has a blank face and the blankness is the point. None of this is rendered urgently on the plate; rendering it urgently would not be Greenshire.

The chief artist's hand is asked to honour the gentleness. Greenshire's atmosphere is its argument; the land is welcoming because the land was always welcoming, and the things that are not welcoming pass through the land without disturbing it. The plate notes the not-welcoming. The plate does not foreground it.

## Marginalia

### Visual Character — for the Chief Artist

Greenshire's palette across the year:

- **Greenmonth (spring)** — the apple orchards in flower along the eastern road; lambs in Brookmeadow Pasture; the Hayford footbridge replaced after the spring flood; the millrace at Pike's Mill running fast; the maypole raised at the Three Ponds village green; the bee skeps at Hettie Brookfield's apiary set out from winter storage.
- **Heartmonth (early summer)** — wheat half-grown and pale; the Halfmark Hamlets' children at the Middle Pond; the Old Tom Cresswick anniversary of his birth quietly observed at his burrow (no formal feast — Old Tom does not approve of formal feasts); the elder council's quarterly session at the cliff top.
- **Harvestmonth (high summer / autumn)** — the wheat in cut; oast-house caps at the Cresswick Outsteading smoking with hops drying; the Halfmark fishing fleet making the southern crossing of the Far Pond; the Three Ponds market at full size every Restday; the apple trees heavy.
- **Frostmonth (winter)** — the Cliff Path in the southern downs largely unwalked; the Wardens' Standing Patrol at Brookmeadow Pasture standing inside the dog-house with Branch curled at Tovin's feet; smoke from every chimney in the village; the Bowed Beam Inn full to its twelve beds and an extra straw mattress in the kitchen.

The signature visual elements — for the artist's notebook — are:

- **Round green burrow doors** with brass knockers (corn-sheaf, leaf, small horse — each Halfling family has its own knocker pattern).
- **Conical oast-house caps** at the Cresswick Outsteading — distinctively Greenshire-pattern, narrower than the Greyveil oast-house caps and slightly pointed.
- **The Pottery Kilns** — six conical brick kilns rising from the village edge, smoke rising on three days of the week (Plowsday, Restday, Thursday), the smoke distinctive enough to be visible from Greengate at three leagues.
- **The Mill** at Pike's Mill — three-storey timber, **red-painted** (the only red-painted building in Greenshire; the paint is a Cogsworth-imported lead red and is renewed every fifteen years; the next renewal is due Year 850 CR).
- **Sable Fall** at the cliff edge — a single forty-foot drop of warm water into the cold Greymere; the temperature differential produces a column of mist visible from the cliff path. The mist is the cliff path's signature feature.
- **The Old Oak Waystone** — a granite stone taller than a man, with three faces engraved with the league-distances and a fourth face blank. The blank face is what is rendered. Whether anything else is on the blank face is the matter under discussion. See below.
- **The Hollow Oak** at Old Hollow — a forty-foot-girth hollow oak with four families' burrows dug into its trunk and surrounding root mass. The Hollow Oak is rendered as part-tree, part-hamlet; the burrow doors are set into the trunk's living wood and the trunk has grown around the door-frames in the seventy years since the doors were set.

### The Three Ponds, Building by Building

For the artist working at street-level resolution, the Three Ponds village runs as follows:

**The Big Pond's north bank** carries the **village green** (☉) at its centre, with the **Market Cross** (a four-foot stone cross with the Greenwarden's leaf carved at its top) at the green's middle. **The Bowed Beam Inn** stands at the green's western edge — three storeys, the lower in stone with the upper two in dark-stained timber; the sign hangs from a wrought-iron bracket and shows a curved oak beam. The **Trainer Hall** stands at the green's eastern edge — flint-and-lime stone, a single storey, a slate roof; the granite plaque listing the trainers in residence is at the entrance, head-height to a Halfling.

**The Greenwarden Shrine** (⛪) stands south of the green by twenty paces — the shrine is small enough that a Human must duck at the door. Inside: three stone altars in a row, the central one (the Greenwarden's) carved with a leaf in low relief, the leaf's veins traced in beaten copper. The shrine's roof is thatched with reed from the Far Pond's southern bank.

**The Pottery Kilns** (⌬) stand at the village's eastern edge, where the wind carries the smoke away. The six kilns are arranged in two rows of three — the Burrowwicks' three to the north, the Mossleys' two and the Crowfoots' single to the south. The Crowfoots' kiln is the smallest and hottest; glaze work requires it.

**The Smithy** (⌧) stands at the village's southern edge near the road to the Outer Holdings. A single-bay forge; an anvil on a wooden stump set in concrete; a barrel of water for quenching; a rack of hammers, tongs, and pincers along the wall. Burrick the apprentice is stocky and freckled and sleeps in the loft.

**Goodwife Periwinkle's Cottage** (⌂) is at the green's north-western corner — a single ground-floor room with a back garden running to the Big Pond's edge. The garden is the shopfront — herbs raised in beds, chamomile and yarrow and witherbeck and three smaller plots of which Periwinkle does not name the contents. The back door of the cottage opens directly into the garden.

### The Halfmark Hamlets in Detail

**Halfmark proper** is the Halfmark Hamlets' formal administrative centre. **The Mayor's Burrow** is the largest single dwelling — twelve rooms arranged around a central hall with a hearth at its centre. The brass knocker on the door is shaped as a corn-sheaf and is polished by the Mayor's housekeeper Mrs. Burrowwick (no relation to the Three Ponds Burrowwicks; the surname is common in Greenshire and indicates occupation — Burrow + work, the building of burrows — rather than family). **The Council House** is a single low stone-and-timber building beside the Burrow with one long room and a long table. The locked cabinet that holds the Roll has five locks, of which the four hamlet elders and Mayor Tobold each hold a key to one; the cabinet does not open with fewer than three keys. **The Halfmark Chapel** is plain lime-washed stone with a single bell in a small bell-cote; Sister Rosalind Pell visits Restday afternoons after her morning at the Greenwarden Shrine.

**Hayford** sits across the **Hayford Brook** — a small stream that runs east into Sable's Run. **Hayford's Footbridge** is a wooden span with two railings; the carpenter's mark on the third railing post is a small leaf burnt into the wood, the mark of Old Pike's grandfather (yes, the same Old Pike's grandfather; the man was a carpenter before his son took up milling). The **Hayford Burrows** run twelve in number along the south slope above the brook. The **Brookwater** inn is a private house turned guest-house, run by Tessa Hayford; the bookings work through the Bowed Beam at Three Ponds.

**Cresswick** is the elder of the four hamlets and the smallest. The **Cresswick Bell Tower** stands at the hamlet's centre — twenty feet of mortared stone, a single bronze bell, a wooden bell-rope reaching down to a cleat at man-height. The bell rings at noon and sunset; rung outside those hours, it is an emergency, and the Wardens' Standing Patrol from Three Ponds will arrive within an hour. **Old Tom's Burrow** is the largest single burrow in Greenshire — nineteen rooms; the central hall has a hearth large enough to roast a sheep over and Old Tom does so on Stillmonth Eve, alone, every year. The **Cresswick Outsteading** stands a quarter-league north of the Bell Tower — corn and hops; two **oast houses** (☆☆) with conical caps for drying hops, the only oast houses in Greenshire.

**Old Hollow** is the most rustic of the four. The **Hollow Oak** (※) is the centre — a hollow oak forty feet around, with four families' burrows dug into the trunk and the surrounding root mass. The trunk has grown over the burrow doors in the seventy years since they were set; the doors must be hand-pulled to open and the families use rope-handles fitted into the door planks. The **Outhollow Burrows** are the eight other burrows of the hamlet, scattered along a single track through the woodland. There is no road. The Hayford Footbridge is the practical approach.

### The Old Oak Waystone — On the Blank Face

The Old Oak Waystone stands at the three-road junction between Halfmark proper, Pike's Mill, and Greengate. The stone is granite, taller than a Human, and is engraved on three of its four faces with league-distances:

- **South face** — distances to Three Ponds (1¾ leagues), Pike's Mill (2¼ leagues), Brookfield (3 leagues)
- **East face** — distances to Greengate (1½ leagues), Greyveil (8 leagues), Harrowmere (17 leagues by river route)
- **North face** — distances to Cogsworth's mountain pass (4 leagues), Cogsworth proper (9 leagues, pass open), the Iron Way (12 leagues)
- **West face** — blank

The blank face is the Hall's official designation. The fact of the blankness has been verified by three independent Hall surveys (Years 791, 824, and 845 CR). The Hall's working position is that the blankness is original to the engraving (the western direction has nothing close enough to engrave; Greenshire's western edge is the cliff and the Greymere) and that the blankness is therefore unremarkable.

The Hall is, the Hall acknowledges, in a minority on this matter. The local position is that the blank face is engraved in characters that cannot be read by ordinary observation — that the engraving is present and the surveyors did not see it. Various proposals have been made about the conditions under which the blank face can be read: at midnight on Stillmonth Eve, by a person born in Greenshire, by a person carrying a Halfmark Hamlets-issued letter of introduction. None of the proposals has been verified by a Hall surveyor. The Hall does not endorse any of them.

The local reading is the more interesting reading. The Hall files it without endorsement and notes that Old Tom Cresswick refuses to discuss the matter, which is the single most informative thing the Hall has on file regarding the matter.

### Tutorial Geography — Quest Hooks

For the artist preparing visualisations of the tutorial flow:

**Greengate is the introduction point.** New travellers — Human, Halfling, or Half-Elf — arrive at the Greengate toll station from the eastern road or from the cliff path (in rare cases). **Toll Mistress Beatrix Greengate** offers tea, a name, and a list of names worth speaking to in Three Ponds. The Greengate visit is the tutorial's narrative anchor. The young Wood Elf ranger **Faelin Wirenwood** has a small role here for those who notice him; he is upstairs and reads in the afternoons.

**The Three Ponds Trainer Hall is the class introduction.** The four trainers in residence (or visiting) take new students by appointment or walk-in:
- **Sister Rosalind Pell** (Cleric) sees students at the Greenwarden Shrine; her first conversation is the same first conversation she has had four times a week for fifteen years and she will not pretend otherwise
- **Master Wenrick Greenward** (Bard) is at the Trainer Hall on Restday afternoons and at Pike's Mill on weekdays
- **Brindle Cresswick** (Rogue) takes students by appointment — letters left at the Bowed Beam reach her through Marri Halfmark within a day
- **Goodwife Periwinkle** is not formally a trainer but is the herbalism reference for any tutorial-aged character with the inclination

**Pike's Mill has the rat-quest.** The granary's rat problem is a perennial Three Ponds tutorial. Old Pike pays in coppers per tail.

**Brookfield has the fox-quest.** Hettie Brookfield's bee-fence is intended for bears (none have been seen for forty years) but is also vulnerable to foxes that take her hens. The fox-quest is ongoing and the Hettie's sigh when explaining it has been catalogued by surveying field teams as "the textbook quest sigh."

**The Halfmark Hamlets council issues the introduction-quest.** A traveller who reaches the Halfmark Council House with letters of introduction (offered by Beatrix Greengate at request) is presented to the council, given a small ceremonial gift (a wax-sealed letter, a pottery cup, or a sprig of dried wormwood depending on the elder's read of the traveller), and entered formally on the Roll's "visitors recognised" register. The recognition is not a quest reward in the usual sense; it is a precondition for several later quests in Greyveil and the Greyfen.

**Old Tom Cresswick has lore-quests.** A traveller who calls on Old Tom — by appointment, set up through Sister Rosalind — receives the standard Old Tom welcome (tea, biscuits, a long story) and, if Old Tom takes a liking to them, a starting hint toward the deeper Greenshire matter: the question of the blank face on the Old Oak Waystone, the question of why Cibrick Lonely sits two leagues south of any other Holding, the question of what is at the cliff top that the Outer Holders' Council uses for summer sessions and the rest of Greenshire does not. Old Tom does not give answers. He gives questions worth asking and the names of who to ask.

**The Cliff Path and the smugglers' cove** are the late-tutorial introduction to the quest world's grayer ground. The Wardens know about the cove. Sister Rosalind has visited the cove. The Outer Holders' Council uses the cliff top for summer sessions because, by long arrangement with the smugglers, the smugglers do not work daylight hours during the Council's sessions. The arrangement is functional and is one of the reasons the cove has not been raided. A tutorial-aged Rogue's introduction to the cove is as a courier, not a fighter; the smugglers welcome couriers who carry letters from Brindle Cresswick.

### What This Plate Does Not Resolve

- **The blank face of the Old Oak Waystone.** As above. The Hall's position remains unproven.
- **The smugglers' Compact arrangement.** The Compact does not publish the licensing under which the cove operates. The Hall has not asked. The Outer Holders' Council does not publish its arrangement with the smugglers. The Hall has not asked.
- **The Greenwarden's place in the canonical pantheon.** The Greenwarden is venerated at the Three Ponds shrine, the Halfmark Chapel, and the Bridge-Foot Shrine. The figure does not appear on the Hall's roster of confirmed deities. The Hall's working position is that the Greenwarden is a regional veneration of one of the canonical deities under a local title (Erindra is the most likely candidate by attribute), but the locals' working position is that the Greenwarden is the Greenwarden and the matter is settled. The plate uses the local term.
- **The deepest tutorial quest hooks.** Old Tom Cresswick's questions point to matter that is outside the tutorial scope — the Foundation Ring's reach into the Greenshire ground (the South Outcrop in the Greyfen is two leagues from the Greengate toll house), the question of what was on the Greenwarden's altar before the Greenwarden took it, the question of why the Wood Elf ranger Faelin Wirenwood has been on rotation at Greengate for three consecutive seasons rather than the usual one. These hooks are noted as tutorial-adjacent and deferred to the regional plate (Plate II) for treatment.

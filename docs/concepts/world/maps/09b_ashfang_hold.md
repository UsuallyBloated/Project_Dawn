*Principal hand: Archivist Valex · Field corroboration (limited; see prefatory note): Compact-Ashfang accord-keeper Halfman Brost · the Trader's Walk-keeper Oggrim of the Long Hand · former Pit-bondsman Kethri-of-no-clan (statement filed Year 842 CR after expiation) · the Hall's standing field surveyor Cellis Vereth (three permitted visits, 838 CR / 842 CR / 846 CR)*

---

## Prefatory Note

This plate is the second of the starting-zone series and the most difficult plate in the series to file. Ashfang Hold does not admit Compact survey teams and does not respond to Hall correspondence; the Hall's three permitted visits to the Camp were granted to a single named surveyor (Cellis Vereth, by personal accord with the previous Warlord and renewed by the current one), and the Hall's working data on Ashfang therefore depends on those three visits, on the Compact-Ashfang accord-keeper Halfman Brost's quarterly reports, on the Trader's Walk-keeper Oggrim of the Long Hand's standing correspondence with the Hall (one letter per season, by tradition), and on the statement Kethri-of-no-clan filed with the Hall after her expiation in Year 842 CR.

The plate accordingly represents the Camp at the position it held in Heartmonth 846 CR — Cellis Vereth's most recent visit. The Ashfang Camp is **movable**; the camp itself relocates approximately every two years, orbiting the **Warlord's Tower**, which is the only fixed structure in the Hold. The current Camp position is approximately one-half league south-west of the Tower at the position rendered. The Camp will move before this plate is bound, and the Hall will not know precisely when until after the move.

The visual character of Ashfang is the pale-iron palette: bleached scrubland under a sun that does not move enough across the sky to relieve the heat; rust-coloured rock; sand drifted into hollows; shaded hide tents the colour of dried blood; pole-banners showing clan totems and bone-strings shifting in a hot wind that does not stop. The **Warlord's Tower** is a single squat cylinder of pre-collapse Dominion stonework — black-grey basalt blocks fitted without mortar, the only architectural feature of Dominion provenance the Ogre clans of the Eastern Badlands acknowledge as functional rather than territorial. The Tower has been there longer than any Ogre clan can document. The current Warlord — **Drosk of the Sundered Hand** — sits in the Tower because the legitimacy of the title is conferred by the Tower's occupation, not by clan descent, blood-tie, or personal challenge; the man who sits at the Tower is the Warlord.

Ashfang's atmosphere is the working air of a culture pushed onto marginal land and refusing to apologise. The Camp is functional; the violence is regulated; the trade with the Compact and the Heartlands is, in volume and reliability, considerably greater than the Heartlands' published trade records reflect. Ashfang Hold is not the wilderness it is rumoured to be. It is a working culture on bad ground, and the bad ground is the ground the Good civilisations did not want.

The chief artist's hand is asked to render the harshness with respect. The Ogres, Trolls, and Half-Ogres at Ashfang have argued — not always quietly, but with considerable consistency — that the Hall's plates of the Eastern Badlands and Ashfang Hold have historically rendered them as scenery. They are not. The Camp is a community; the Tower is a centre of governance; the Trader's Walk is a working trade road; the Pit is a regulated venue with its own customs and rules. The plate notes the regulation. The plate renders the community. The artist should as well.

## Marginalia

### Visual Character — for the Chief Artist

Ashfang's palette is a study in heat and iron. The dominant tones are bleached straw-yellow (scrubland under the unmoving sun), iron-red (rust-streaked outcrop, dried blood at the Ration Ground, bloodstone-streaked sand around the Pit), the black-grey of the Tower's basalt (the only architectural element of true darkness in the entire Hold; the Tower stands out from the bleached ground at distance, visible from the Salt Pillars three days' march away), and the smoke-grey of the Centre Fire's perpetual plume.

The signature visuals — for the artist's notebook — are:

- **The Tower** — squat, unmortared, three-storey black basalt; the only fixed structure; visible from any point inside the Camp; visible at distance as a single dark vertical against bleached ground.
- **The Centre Fire** — perpetual; smoke rising in a single column on still days, drifting on the prevailing wind on others. The smoke's colour shifts through the day from pale-grey at first kindling to pale-orange at the morning's reigniting to a clear thin trail by midday.
- **Hide tents** — pale untanned aurochs hide for the Warlord's Hide Tent, mixed hide for the Camp's tents (cattle-hide, aurochs-hide, occasional badlands-mountain-goat hide for the smaller tents). Tent-sides are weighted with packed sand-bags at the base.
- **Trophy poles** — tall pole-spans of stripped ironwood, banner-rags of clan colour at the top, skulls strung below, and braided horse-hair tails (or aurochs-tails) at the very base where the pole meets the ground. The skulls do not all face the same direction — a deliberate choice indicating that the trophies are markers, not threats.
- **The Pit** — a circular sand-floor arena, the sand a paler tone than the surrounding scrub from regular raking; the earthwork seating four feet tall and packed earth, broken only at the eastern entrance gap where contestants enter; the Bone Yard adjacent to the west gives off a different palette — paler bone-white drifting into yellow-grey — visible against the broader scrub.
- **The Tributary Pens** — eight lashed-pole structures the height of an Ogre, walls of upright stakes braced with horizontal poles, hide roofing in a pitched ridge to shed rain; the pens are uniform in build and uniform in material, which is the visual mark that the construction is policy not improvisation.
- **The Trader's Walk** — a packed-earth track the width of two Ogre-strides, marked at one-hundred-pace intervals by trophy poles; the poles' skulls along the Walk are pale and clean (the formal markers, not battle trophies — they are bleached and re-bleached).

Costume notes: Ogres and Trolls in the Camp typically wear hide-and-leather wraps with iron banded armour at chest and shoulders; lower legs and forearms wrapped in cured-hide bands. Half-Ogres tend to lighter dress, often with cloth-and-leather harnesses showing more detail of cut. Hide brow-bands worn under heavy brows by all to keep dust from the eyes. Banners are clan-pattern hide-and-iron; the Sundered Hand banner is a dark hide rectangle with two iron rivet-patterns marking a sundered five-fingered hand (the gesture is a fist rendered as an open hand with two fingers struck off — the iconography is specifically Drosk's; Mokk Eight-Tooth's pre-Drosk banner showed a different pattern).

### The Trade That Is Not Published

For the artist working on Ashfang's interfaces with the wider world: the Hold trades with the Compact, the Heartlands, and Harrowmere's Foreign Quarter on a volume the published Compact register does not reflect. Specifically:

1. **Iron** flows in. The Iron Furrows in the Eastern Badlands proper (north of the Hold) are the source of a specific working iron the Magicians' Guild and the Greyveil smiths value. Vrekka of the Iron Eye works it; the Hold ships finished iron-work west via Oggrim and the Trader's Walk to the Compact's Foreign Quarter handlers at Harrowmere. The Compact-published trade volume for Foreign Quarter iron does not include the Ashfang-sourced iron. The Compact's accountancy at the Counting House at Harrowmere does include it. The Hall has filed both registers without comment.

2. **Bondsmen and prisoners** flow out. The Tributary Pens send periodic transports west via Oggrim to the Foreign Quarter, where the prisoners (typically Half-Ogre raiders captured in Sundered Hand patrols) are sold to indenture-buyers under Foreign Quarter charter. This trade is not published. It is one of the standing arrangements that the Foreign Quarter exists to administer.

3. **Hide and rendered fat** flow in both directions. Camp-rendered fat, Camp-cured aurochs hide, and Camp-tanned working leather are exported in moderate quantity; finished goods (worked leather, cloth, finished metal) and salt-cured grain are imported.

4. **News** flows in by Oggrim and out by Halfman Brost. The two are friendly in the working sense — they have known each other thirty years; Brost was Oggrim's predecessor at the Trader's Walk for a season early in his career, before the accord-keeper post was created. Their conversations are the Hall's most reliable channel for what the Hold knows about the wider world and for what the Compact wants the Hold to know.

The chief artist may render the Hold without these trade flows visible. The Hold itself does not advertise them. The detail is provided so the artist understands that the Hold is not isolated — it is a node in a working trade network that the Compact prefers not to publish.

### Tutorial Geography — Quest Hooks

For the artist preparing visualisations of the tutorial flow at Ashfang:

**A new character at Ashfang is presumed to begin at the Camp's southern edge** (the Half-Ogre tents, the closest ring to the Trader's Walk) — this is where new arrivals, including new bondsmen and new clan-affiliates, are housed. The first conversation is typically with **Heggrim the Hookman**, who reads the new arrival's working capacity over a meal at the Ration Ground.

**Heggrim sets the first quests.** Cleanup at the Ration Ground (the rats), bone-bearing to the Bone Yard, ration distribution rounds. These are the Hold's "earn your keep" tutorial set. The character is expected to perform unglamorous work at first; the etiquette is that the Camp earns its respect.

**Vrekka of the Iron Eye sets the smithing introduction.** Tutorial-aged Smiths or Warriors may apprentice at the Open Forge for a season. The trade is iron-fetching from the Skullback Iron-Quarry and bellows-pumping; the lessons are technique-by-imitation. Vrekka does not coddle.

**Korrik Three-Brand at the Pit Apprentices' Ground** sets the Warrior and Shadow Knight introduction. The Apprentices' Ground stake-work and sandbag-striking is the trainer interface; the tutorial-aged character earns access to their first formal Pit match by clearing a Korrik-set sequence of techniques. The first match is rarely lethal — Korrik chooses the match-up and selects opponents who will give the apprentice a working test rather than a defeat.

**Threkka the Stone-Singer at the Shaman's Circle** sets the Shaman introduction. Tutorial-aged Shamans (Ogre or Troll) attend the sunrise singing for thirty days before being permitted to sing one of the seven stones; the choice of stone is Threkka's; the Sundered Hand stone is reserved for clan-members and is rarely given.

**Mireth Slow-Hand at the Healer's Tent** sets the Cleric introduction. The Camp does not have a formal Cleric tradition — Mireth's healing is Troll-tradition, not pantheon-tradition — but tutorial-aged Clerics who arrive from outside may apprentice at the Tent for a season. The arrangement is standing and is one of the reasons the Hall's three Cellis Vereth visits have been permitted. Mireth's tradition is, as far as the Hall can read, compatible with Solrath-and-Erindra liturgy at a working-craft level even if not at a doctrinal one.

**Brogga of the Long Tooth at the Tributary Pens** sets the introduction for tutorial-aged characters with administrative inclinations. Pen-keeping is the principal record-keeping role in the Hold and is a viable apprenticeship for any character whose path lies in matters of ledger, register, and authority. The role is also the introduction to the Hold's relationship with the Compact's published-and-unpublished trade — anyone working with Brogga learns that trade quickly.

**Oggrim of the Long Hand at the Trader's Walk** sets the introduction for the more diplomatic tutorial paths — Bards (yes, the Hold has a small Bard tradition through Oggrim's contact with the Foreign Quarter), Rogues, and Witch Hunters. Oggrim's mediation between outsiders and the Hold is the working class for cross-cultural courier work.

**The Tower's Audience Floor** is reachable only by formal accord — outsiders walk the Walk to the Tower's southern face under Oggrim's escort; Hold-internal characters reach it via the standing path north of the Camp under the Warlord's permission. The first audience with **Drosk of the Sundered Hand** is the Hold's tutorial culmination — the moment at which the new arrival is recognised as a Hold-affiliate and is given a formal name, role, and clan recognition (or, in the case of Half-Ogres, formal Outsider-Affiliate status, which is the highest recognition Half-Ogres receive in the Hold's working register).

### What This Plate Does Not Resolve

- **The Camp's next position.** The Camp will move within the plate's working life. The next position is selected by Threkka the Stone-Singer's reading at sunrise on the day after the move is called by Drosk; the reading consults the seven stones and the Camp's current clan composition. The plate cannot anticipate the next position.
- **The Tower's foundation extent.** The Walking Name Order's working position is that the Tower's foundation extends further than the visible ring around the Tower's foot suggests. The Order has not been permitted to investigate. The Hall renders the visible ring and notes the Order's position.
- **The full clan list.** The Standing-Stones Hall's six standing stones are clan-markers; the Hall has identified four (Sundered Hand, Bone-Set, Iron Eye, Long Tooth — the latter two known by their by-name-bearers in the Camp); two are not on the Hall's record because the Hall has not been permitted to read the stones at close range. Cellis Vereth's three visits have all been at dawn, when the Audience Floor is in shadow; the Standing-Stones Hall is on the second storey and Cellis has not visited it.
- **The accord-keeper's actual role.** Halfman Brost's quarterly reports to the Compact are not Compact-published. The Hall's working position is that Brost's reports are substantive — that the accord between Compact and Ashfang is a working-level treaty rather than a courtesy — and that Brost is closer to a diplomatic representative than to a courier. Brost has not confirmed or denied this characterisation.
- **What is in the Tributary Pens at any given filing.** The Pen census is current to Heartmonth 846 CR. The Pens' contents change quarterly. The plate's three named occupants will not be there when the artist begins work.

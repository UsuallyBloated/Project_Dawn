# Mireth — The Laughing Door
*Deity reference — Rogue, Bard, and luck-adjacent classes; Neutral-aligned*

---

## Names and Titles

**Primary name:** Mireth *(MEE-reth)*

**Titles in common use:**
- The Laughing Door
- She Who Opens
- The Tilted Scale
- The One Who Was Already There

**Titles used by specific communities:**
- **Rogues' tradition:** The Lucky Cut (specific; refers to the precise moment in a card game when the deck turns)
- **Bards:** The Open Stage (she is associated with the moment before performance — the held breath)
- **Gamblers:** The House (ironic; she is never the house, but invoking her name before a bet is tradition)
- **Merchants:** The Second Option (what you reach for when the first deal falls apart)

**Titles used in opposition:**
- The Deceiver (Solrath clergy — they consider her domain incompatible with the compact's demand for truth)
- The Pickpocket (Vethis funerary priests — slightly fond; they appreciate that she never touches their domain)
- The Necessary Problem (Erindra tradition — the cycle needs disruption sometimes; Mireth provides it)

---

## Nature and Domain

Mireth is the goddess of chance, misdirection, and the space between what is true and what is believed. Her domains are:

- **Luck** — not fate, not destiny; the specific unreasonable thing that happens despite probability
- **Trickery** — misdirection, deception, the lie that saves; the truth that lands wrong and serves better as a lie
- **Thresholds** — doors, moments of decision, the instant before everything changes; she lives at the hinge
- **Opportunity** — the gap in the armor, the unlocked window, the contact who owes someone a favor

She is not evil. This is the most important and most contested theological point about Mireth. She is Neutral — not because she lacks conviction, but because she refuses the framework. Good and Evil are, in her theology, two faces of the same seriousness, and she finds seriousness overrated as a survival strategy.

She is not frivolous. People who assume she is frivolous tend to be surprised later, when they realize she was paying attention the whole time.

## Theology

### The Laughing Door
Mireth's primary symbol is a door — specifically, one that is ajar. Not fully open (that is Vethis's domain — the door that waits). Not locked (that is Aracheth's domain — the door whose key is information). Ajar: the door someone left open by accident, or on purpose, or because they got the hinges wrong, or because they knew you were coming and didn't want to be the one who let you in.

The theological meaning: every situation has a door. It may not be obvious. It may not be the door you were looking for. Finding the door is Mireth's gift; opening it is yours.

### On Truth and Deception
Mireth's most theologically contested position: truth is a tool, not an obligation. The obligation is to the outcome — to the survival of the person, the protection of those who cannot protect themselves, the resolution of the situation. If the truth serves those ends, use the truth. If a lie serves them better, use the lie.

This is completely incompatible with Solrath's theology and Mireth knows it. She has been recorded (in Bard tradition, which is her primary oral-history keeper) as saying: "Solrath and I agree on most of the important things. We disagree on whether the method matters. He says it does. I say it depends. We have been having this argument for longer than either of us admits."

Solrath's recorded response to this: no comment.

### On Luck
Mireth does not give luck. She does not dispense it from a reservoir to favored worshippers. Luck, in her theology, is not a resource. It is a reading — a recognition that probability is not destiny, that the unlikely outcome is still an outcome, and that the person positioned to receive it has done the preparation work to be there when it arrives.

A gambler who prays to Mireth before every bet is not being helped by her. A gambler who studies the game, reads the room, positions themselves at the right table at the right time, and *then* prays to Mireth before the decisive hand — that is the correct relationship. Luck favors the prepared. The prayer is acknowledgment, not request.

### On Consequences
Mireth's theology does not let her followers off the hook for the consequences of their misdirection. A lie that saves one person and destroys another is not blessed by Mireth simply because someone invoked her. She is associated with cleverness, not with cruelty disguised as cleverness. Her tradition has a specific word for using her framework to justify selfish harm: *mirth without merit*, and it is not a compliment.

## The Tradition (No Formal Priesthood)

Mireth has no temples in the architectural sense. She has:

### Shrines
Tucked into unexpected places — behind a loose brick in an inn cellar, above a doorframe in a market stall, in the corner of a gambling hall that has been open longer than most of its customers have been alive. A Mireth shrine is a small carved door, usually no larger than a hand, always positioned at an actual threshold. No offerings required; the shrine is acknowledged by touching the lintel when you pass.

### The Laughing Company
A loose network of Bards and Rogues who maintain Mireth's oral tradition, share shrine locations, and meet irregularly in different cities. Not a guild — they have no dues, no charter, no headquarters. They have a recognition signal (a specific way of dealing cards or tuning a specific string) and a shared commitment to not writing anything important down.

The Laughing Company's oral tradition contains the closest thing to Mireth's theology: stories, not doctrine. The stories always have a door in them. The person who finds the door usually survives. The person who is certain there is no door often does not.

### The House Priests
A small subgroup who operate inside gambling halls, performing luck-blessings before major games. Not ordained — there is no ordination in Mireth's tradition. They are recognized by the community through reputation. They charge for blessings (Mireth would find it strange if they didn't) and donate a portion to causes she would approve of (their judgment).

## Iconography

### Images
Mireth is depicted in two standard forms:

**The Door Form:** A figure that is partially behind a door that is ajar. You can see most of her — enough to tell she is smiling — but not all of her. The concealed portion is always the same portion regardless of which image you look at. Nobody knows why. The artists who were asked said they did not decide it that way; it just came out that way.

**The Table Form:** Seated at a card table with no other players, the cards already played, the result already determined. She is looking at the viewer. The expression is the expression of someone who knew how the hand would go and is waiting for the other person to figure it out.

She is depicted in simple clothing — nothing that marks status or affiliation. The only consistent element across all images is that there is always a door somewhere in the frame.

### No Temples
If Mireth has a temple, nobody has found it. The Laughing Company maintains that she has a standing table at every gambling establishment that has been open for fifty years or more, and that she occasionally sits there, and that the person who sits there when she is sitting there does not realize until later. The theological implication (that Mireth might be an ordinary-looking person in a chair at a gambling hall) is the kind of thing that makes Solrath's clergy uncomfortable and Mireth's followers quietly delighted.

## Relationship to Other Deities

**Solrath:** The ongoing argument. Both would rather not fight about it. Both keep finding it necessary. Paladins and Rogues have a working relationship in practice that their respective patrons have never formally endorsed.

**Aracheth:** Deep mutual respect and deep mutual wariness. Both work in the unseen — Aracheth through the web of fate, Mireth through the tilted scale of chance. Aracheth believes luck is just a reading error in fate. Mireth believes fate is just luck that has learned to tell a better story about itself. They have not resolved this.

**Vethis:** Genuinely friendly. Mireth respects someone who does their work without drama. Vethis finds Mireth interesting and does not fully understand why, which she finds delightful.

**Erindra:** The least contentious relationship. The cycle and luck are related — disruption is necessary, and Mireth provides it. Erindra accepts her in the way the river accepts the rock: works around her, doesn't fight the shape.

## Mechanical Interactions

**Rogue class:** Mireth is the Rogue patron deity for those who choose one. Her blessings manifest as passive bonuses to detection avoidance, lock interaction, and the specific Backstab calculation — the moment when preparation meets position meets opportunity.

**Bard class:** Bards do not require a patron deity, but those who choose Mireth gain access to Laughing Company quest content and unique dialogue with certain NPCs who recognize the affiliation.

**Critical hit theology:** Critical hits are described in Mireth's oral tradition as "when she leans on the scale." This is not mechanical canon — it is flavor — but it explains why critical hits feel like luck even when they result from skill.

**Luck potions / fortune effects:** Any item in the game with a luck or fortune effect is associated with Mireth's tradition by name in its item description, even if it was manufactured by someone who has never prayed to her in their life.

## Quest Hooks

- **The Shrine Behind the Brick** — A Mireth shrine in a Greyveil inn cellar has been bricked over by the new owner during renovations. Three separate visitors have left the inn with their luck running badly enough that they have noticed. A Laughing Company Bard wants someone to un-brick the shrine. The new owner does not believe in Mireth and will need to be convinced by argument, evidence, or misdirection — the player's choice.
- **The Card Game** — The Laughing Company is convening a once-per-decade gathering. The location is unknown (each convening is in a different city). The invitation is a specific card, tucked into a location that makes no sense, found by someone who was not looking for it. The gathering's agenda is secret. Players who attend will discover that the Laughing Company has been maintaining a specific piece of knowledge for eighty years that it does not know what to do with.
- **The House Priest's Call** — A House Priest performing blessings at a gambling hall in Harrowmere has blessed someone she should not have — not because they are evil, but because they have a specific item in their pocket that she recognized, and recognizing it meant she knew what was going to happen to them when they won. She needs someone to find that person before the game ends.
- **The Tilted Scale** — Someone has been systematically removing Mireth shrines from the road between Greyveil and Millhaven. Not destroying them — removing them carefully and replacing them somewhere else. The relocated shrines are in positions that form a pattern when mapped. The Laughing Company wants to know what the pattern means before they decide what to do about whoever is making it.

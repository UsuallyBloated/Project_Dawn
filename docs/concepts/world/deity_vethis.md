# Vethis — The Patient Accountant
*Deity reference — Necromancer, Shadow Knight, and death-adjacent classes; Evil-leaning Neutral*

---

## Names and Titles

**Primary name:** Vethis *(VEH-this)*

**Titles in funerary rites:**
- The Patient Accountant
- The Door That Waits
- He Who Records
- The Final Witness

**Titles in Necromancer tradition:**
- The Source
- The Generous One (a specific title; see theology section)
- The Open Hand

**Titles used in opposition:**
- The Hoarder (Solrath clergy — Vethis holds the dead rather than releasing them)
- The Corruptor (Erindra tradition — Vethis's relationship to undead is seen as cycle disruption)
- The Patient Trap (some Paladin orders — they do not trust gods who wait)

Vethis does not respond to criticism. He has noted it.

---

## Nature and Domain

Vethis is the god of death in the specific sense that he is the god of what happens after death — not the dying, not the violence of it, but the destination. His domains are:

- **Death** — not killing but the state of being dead; the existence that follows cessation of life
- **The Ledger** — every death recorded; every life accounted for; nothing lost, nothing forgotten
- **Memory** — the dead persist in Vethis's domain as what they were; he does not erase; he keeps
- **The Threshold** — the moment of transition; the door between the living world and whatever follows

He is not evil in the way a villain is evil. He is evil-leaning in alignment because his domain is death, and in the moral cosmology of the world, death-worship tends toward Evil alignment by definition. Vethis himself is more accurately described as patient and thorough. He is accountable to nothing except the accuracy of his ledger. He does not take sides in the conflicts of the living. He takes everyone eventually.

## Theology

### The Ledger
Vethis maintains a perfect record of every death that has occurred. Every creature that has ever died is in the ledger — name (if it had one), life (the record of it), death (the circumstances). The ledger is not accessible to the living. The living will get access when they are no longer living. Vethis considers this reasonable.

The theological implication: nothing is forgotten. Every death has been witnessed. Every life that ended has been recorded. This is Vethis's primary offering to the living — the assurance that their deaths will matter to someone, will be noted, will persist in a record that does not burn and is not lost.

This is more comforting than it sounds. It is also less comforting than it sounds. Vethis records everyone. He does not distinguish between the good death and the bad one. Both are in the ledger. He is thorough.

### On the Undead — The Generous Position
The Necromancer tradition's name for Vethis — "The Generous One" — reflects a theological argument that most other faiths reject: that Vethis permits the use of his dead.

The Necromancer theology argues that Vethis owns the dead. He owns them completely — their essence, their bones, their lingering spiritual presence. And because he owns them, he can permit their use. A Necromancer who animates a skeleton is not stealing from the dead or violating them; they are using material that Vethis has leased to them, knowingly, with understanding of the purpose.

Vethis has never confirmed or denied this theology. Necromancers take this silence as confirmation. Other faiths take it as evidence of Vethis's indifference to how his domain is used, which they find alarming.

### On the Undead — The Standard Position
Solrath, Erindra, and most other faiths hold that undead are aberrations — things that have been taken from death's proper domain and put to improper use. From this perspective, Vethis is not being generous; he is being robbed. Every undead creature is a soul that has not completed its accounting, a body that has been stolen from the decay it was owed.

Vethis has not taken a public position on this framing either. The ambiguity is theologically load-bearing in several traditions and Vethis appears to find this acceptable.

### On Resurrection
Solrath Clerics have Resurrection — the ability to return a dead player to life. This is theologically interesting in a world where Vethis is the god of death, because it means someone is pulling souls back out of Vethis's domain.

The current theological consensus: Solrath and Vethis have an arrangement. Souls that have not yet fully settled into Vethis's accounting — very recent deaths — can be recalled by Solrath's agents with Vethis's implicit tolerance. The window for this is finite; after a certain point, the accounting is complete and the soul cannot be called back. This is why Resurrection must be performed soon after death and why it returns the soul without all of the experience it accumulated (the ledger has already started recording).

Neither Solrath's clergy nor Vethis's have publicly endorsed this arrangement. Both traditions operate as if it is true.

### On Shadow Knights
Shadow Knights draw on death-magic alongside their martial practice. They are not Necromancers — their relationship to Vethis is different. Where Necromancers borrow from Vethis's supply, Shadow Knights borrow Vethis's *authority*. The fear a Shadow Knight generates in the living, the lifetap that draws vitality from a living creature — these are borrowings of death's power, not death's material.

Vethis's attitude toward Shadow Knights is the same as his attitude toward everything else: he is noting what they do and will record them accurately when the time comes.

## The Priesthood

The Vethis faith has two distinct expressions: the funerary tradition and the Necromancer tradition. These are theologically related but culturally separate.

### Funerary Tradition
Open to all; predominantly Good-and-Neutral aligned communities use them for death rites regardless of their own faith. A Solrath follower who dies still gets the Vethis funerary rites because Solrath's clergy does not perform funerals — that is Vethis's domain.

Ranks (ascending):
1. **Record-Keeper** — Novice; maintains the local book of the dead; assists at funerals
2. **Door-Warden** — Full clergy; performs death rites; counsels the grieving
3. **The Witness** — Senior clergy; regional death records; handles unusual or contested deaths
4. **The Accountant** — High clergy; one per major city; the final theological authority on death-related disputes

Funerary priests wear grey. They carry a small book — not the real ledger, but a personal record of every death rite they have performed. This book is buried with them when they die.

### Necromancer Tradition
Separate institution; Evil-aligned; organized around knowledge of death-magic rather than pastoral care. The Necromancer tradition has its own hierarchy, operates out of places like Vel'Sharath, and has a complicated relationship with the funerary priests — they share a deity and a domain and agree on almost nothing about what to do with it.

The funerary tradition considers Necromancers to be misusing Vethis's generosity in ways he probably did not intend. The Necromancer tradition considers the funerary priests to be terrified of the gift they have been given.

## Iconography

### Images
Vethis is depicted as a robed figure of indeterminate age with a large book open in one hand and a quill in the other. He is always depicted writing — in mid-stroke, as if he just noted something. His expression is attentive. Not cruel, not kind. The expression of someone who is paying careful attention because the information matters.

He has no face in some traditions — the face is described as "appropriate" — the viewer sees what is most accurate for the moment they are viewing it. This is theological shorthand for "we do not know what he looks like because none of us have seen him and come back."

### Temples
Every settlement of any size has a Vethis funerary temple. They are not grand. They are practical — built to process the dead, counsel the living, and maintain the records. The architectural requirement is a records room with fireproofing, because the local death records cannot be lost.

The doors of Vethis temples are always slightly open. The theology: Vethis does not close his doors to anyone. The door is open. It is just patient.

Grave sites are associated with Vethis but are not his temples. Graveyards are where the dead return to the ground (Erindra's domain), maintained under the authority of the Vethis funerary tradition. This jurisdictional overlap is managed through a three-century-old cooperative agreement that both faiths find slightly irritating but continue to honor.

## Relationship to Other Deities

**Solrath:** Functional partnership around the compact of life and death. Solrath manages living obligations; Vethis receives when the obligation ends. They do not argue. They have a professional relationship of the kind that does not require warmth to be effective.

**Erindra:** The cleanest relationship — cycle and death are aspects of the same process, and both deities understand this. The graveyard-jurisdiction agreement is their formal cooperation made practical.

**Aracheth:** Vethis is in Aracheth's web in the same way everyone is in Aracheth's web. Vethis finds this observation accurate and unremarkable. The feeling is not mutual — Aracheth finds Vethis interesting in ways she has not explained, which is unusual for her.

## The Ledger — What It Means for Players

Several questlines reference the ledger directly or its implications:

- **The Nameless Commander** in the Ashen Marches does not remember their name, race, or side. Vethis's ledger has all of this. The ledger is not accessible to the living, but a Necromancer of sufficient standing in the Vethis tradition might receive a fragment of the record — not as a favor, but as a loan, with interest to be repaid eventually.
- **The Gate-Warden** at The Breach is in the ledger from the moment it was built — Vethis records constructs that have functional awareness, not just the biologically alive. The Gate-Warden has been in the ledger for a very long time. Its entry is very long. Vethis has been watching it work.
- **Resurrection limits** — A player who has been dead longer than the game's resurrection window cannot be brought back by Clerical intervention. Their accounting is complete. What this means in lore: Vethis has closed the record. A Necromancer can still animate the body, but the soul is in the ledger.

## Quest Hooks

- **The Missing Entry** — A Door-Warden in Greyveil has found a name missing from the local death records. The person in question is definitely dead — the funeral was performed, the body was buried. But there is no entry in the ledger. This has never happened before. The Witness needs to know why. The answer requires finding out how the person died before the rite could record them.
- **The Unauthorized Recall** — Someone has pulled a soul back out of Vethis's domain without performing a proper Resurrection. The soul came back wrong — partially settled, confused, dangerous. The Accountant needs it resolved. They will not specify whether "resolved" means the soul should be helped or returned to the ledger.
- **What the Accountant Knows** — The city Accountant has begun acting strangely. Not evilly, not dangerously — just strangely. Asking about people who died years ago. Visiting old grave sites. The Accountant's assistant is worried and does not know how to report something like this to someone higher up, because the Accountant is the highest.
- **The Necromancer's Debt** — A Necromancer borrowed from Vethis's supply extensively and has died without repaying. Vethis's funerary tradition has been informed by the Necromancer tradition that the debt exists. The funerary priest responsible has to figure out what a deity-level debt looks like when denominated in animated skeletons and how to make it right.

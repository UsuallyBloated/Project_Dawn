# NPC Profile — Seeker Drathis
*Walking Name Order. Ixareth-Kul. Levels 35–50.*

---

## Identity

**Full name:** Drathis Vehn *(DRATH-iss VEN)*
**Title:** Seeker (Walking Name Order — their term for a field researcher with full investigative authority)
**Race:** Kel`varath
**Class:** Bard (level 36)
**Age:** 58
**Alignment:** True Neutral

**Physical description:** Medium height for a Kel`varath, which is slightly below Human average. The build of someone who walks constantly and carries only what is necessary — lean, efficient, with the specific quality of movement that comes from twenty-three years of arriving at places on foot. The Walking Name Order's traveling grey, worn and repaired but clean. A small frame drum worn at the hip like most Bards wear a blade. One bag, one set of clothes, a journal that is on its second volume of the current year.

His hands are rarely still. When he is listening or thinking, he taps — not music exactly, patterns — against his thigh or the nearest surface. He is unaware that he does this. The rhythm is different each time.

Eyes: the amber-to-gold range common in Kel`varath lines with heavy Dominion ancestry. Patient eyes. Not warm in the first-impression sense, but paying attention in a way that most people's eyes are not.

---

## Who He Is

Drathis Vehn has been a Seeker of the Walking Name Order for twenty-three years. Before that he was a traveling Bard in the Dominion ruins zone — the kind of Bard who learned music to go places and stayed for the history. The Walking Name found him because he had, independently, noticed the same structural features in Kel`varath pre-Dominion ritual script and Architect notation that Valex Dethras had noticed, and had reached a different conclusion about what the connection meant.

Valex concluded: ancestral link, requires documentation. Drathis concluded: the Architects are communicating, and the communication is addressed to us.

He is not certain of this. He does not claim certainty. What he claims is that the evidence is consistent with ongoing presence — the remainders, the Warden's behavior, the Calibration State's design — in a way that documentation alone does not explain. Something designed to wait for successors and communicate with them does not stop waiting simply because the successors are slow. He has been walking for twenty-three years looking for the pieces of what it is waiting to say.

## What He Has Done

**Sites visited:**
- Sunken Ziggurat: twice. The second time he reached the Still Throne level and was in the water for approximately forty minutes before accepting that he was not going to get closer. He describes this without embarrassment. "It is not ready to be approached yet, or I am not ready to approach it. One of us needs to do more work."
- Dreaming Grove: once. Completed the Warden's interaction. The Warden asked him something. He answered. He will not say what either question or answer was. He has said it was not an Architect question — it was his question, about himself. He has said the answer was true.
- Varek's Spire: three days. He and Varek had the most civil disagreement of his career. Varek maintained that certainty erosion was a documented hazard and he should be careful. Drathis maintained that what Varek called certainty erosion, he called getting closer to accurate. They parted on good terms. He thinks Varek is the most interesting person alive and that Varek has been so close to the Residual for so long that he cannot see it clearly anymore.
- Convergence Point (Ashen Marches): four times. He has mapped it more thoroughly than any other researcher. He knows where the survey terminus marker sits and what it would take to activate it. He does not intend to activate it — that is Final Survey's approach, and he finds their approach alarming for reasons he has explained to Sathren at least three times.
- Old Thing (Millhaven): suspects there is something in the Millhaven Cellars connected to the network but has not confirmed it. He was denied access to the inn cellar two years ago because the innkeeper (Bess) was having a difficult week and he asked at the wrong moment. He has not returned. He intends to.

**What he has not done:** Attempted to trigger, activate, or accelerate anything. This is the fundamental Walking Name distinction from Final Survey.

---

## His Disagreement with Final Survey

Drathis has explained his objection to Director Sathren three times, in written form, and Sathren has each time acknowledged the objection and explained why she has assessed it at a probability she considers acceptable.

His objection: the survey completion trigger is designed to be met by someone who has completed the survey. The phrase "completing the survey" does not mean triggering the terminus mechanically — it means having done the work of actually surveying the nodes. Final Survey's approach uses the trigger without having done the full work at most of the nodes. The Architects designed this system. The Architects were careful designers. Triggering the conclusion without the requisite understanding is a category error about what the conclusion is for.

Sathren's response: this is philosophically interesting and operationally irrelevant since the system responds to conditions, not intentions.

Drathis's unspoken response: Sathren is correct about conditions and wrong about what the conditions are actually measuring, and he has no proof of this, and he knows it.

## Relationship to Players

**First meeting:** He is in Ixareth-Kul at the Walking Name's base of operations — a converted lower level of the survey complex that the Order has occupied, somewhat by squatting and somewhat by demonstrating usefulness to the Dominion Orders' general research mission. He is easy to find because he is the person asking questions of everyone who comes through. Initial interaction: interested, direct, no social preamble. He asks where you have been and what you noticed.

**Building standing:** He responds to information. Bring him documentation from any Architect site and you have his full attention. Players who have been to the Dreaming Grove and completed the Warden's interaction receive particular attention — he wants to know what the Warden asked, and if players say they won't tell him what they answered, he respects that immediately. He asks what the Warden asked because he is building a pattern of questions across known visitors.

**High standing:** He shares his full site documentation — twenty-three years of notes across four sites. He will tell players what he thinks the Calibration State's conclusion is about, which is the most complete theory any NPC will offer. He will also tell them where the Walking Name's research is incomplete and what would help. And he will ask, once, if players are willing to go back to the Millhaven inn and see if they can get into the cellar — he has reasons to believe there is an active node there that no one has surveyed.

---

## What He Believes (and Will Say)

He will explain the Walking Name position to anyone who asks:

The Architects designed a system intended to operate on a timescale of millennia. They designed it for successors they knew would not arrive for a very long time. They built in a Warden that asks questions rather than provides answers. They built a communication addressed to "whoever finds this first, after" — not a record, a communication. Systems designed to communicate require both a sender and a receiver. The sender has been sending, intermittently, for five thousand years. The receiver — players, currently — has been finding pieces of the message.

The Walking Name does not believe the Architects are physically present. They believe the network is the Architects in the only form that was designed to persist: functional, waiting, and still addressed to someone.

He is not trying to wake anything up. He is trying to read the letter.

---

## Quest Hooks

- **The Site Survey** — He asks players to document a site he has not been to. His documentation forms are precise; he wants specific things noted. What players bring back from any site advances the Walking Name research chain.
- **The Millhaven Node** — He asks players to get into the Crossed Keys Inn cellar and see if there is Architect notation in the chamber below. He can tell players what to look for. This links the Millhaven questline to the Architect network directly.
- **The Warden's Pattern** — He has documentation of the questions the Dreaming Warden has asked twelve previous visitors. He wants to know what it asked the current players. If players share this, he will show them the pattern he has found, which reveals something about what the Warden is actually assessing for — and it is not what most visitors assume.
- **The Disagreement with Sathren** — He asks players, at high standing, to reconsider working with Final Survey. Not to stop — he is not positioned to stop them and would not try — but to consider whether Sathren's probability assessment has accounted for the specific thing Drathis is worried about. He cannot prove his concern. He can present it clearly and let players decide.

## Dialogue Samples

*On the Walking Name position:*
"We do not believe the Architects are sitting somewhere waiting to speak in person. We believe the network is the conversation they left running. Five thousand years of a signal addressed to whoever arrives first after them. The Dreaming Warden asks questions. The remainders are consistently warm. The Calibration State is triggered by survey completion, not by elapsed time — they designed it to wait for an engaged recipient, not a patient one. This is not an accident of design. They were waiting for someone who was paying attention."

*On Sathren:*
"Cellen Sathren is the most organized researcher at the Orders and I find her alarming. Not malicious — methodical. The distinction matters. A malicious person makes choices; a methodical person follows a process. She has assessed the harmful scenario at twelve percent and concluded that eighty-eight percent justifies proceeding. What I cannot get her to engage with is what the harmful scenario looks like from the inside of the twelve percent. I have tried three times. I will probably try a fourth."

*On his visit to the Dreaming Grove:*
"The Warden asked me something. I will tell you this much: it was about me, not about the Architects. It wanted to know something specific about who I was and what I was doing there. I answered honestly. The honest answer was the correct answer. I left with the fragment and I have thought about the question every day since, not because I am troubled by it but because I am still working out why that was the right question to ask someone doing what I do."

*On the finger-tapping:*
"I'm not doing — " *[looks at his hand]* " — I am doing that. I don't know what it is. It started about six years ago. My best guess is the drum. Something about the frame rhythms gets into the muscle memory and it follows me. I've stopped trying to stop it."

*On Varek:*
"He gave me tea from a kettle that had been sitting for what I now believe was approximately six hours, and he told me, twice, that I should be careful about certainty erosion, and he told me this with an expression that I would describe as 'certain.' I pointed this out. He said, 'I think I may have been careful about certainty erosion insufficiently.' We agreed on that. We did not agree on anything else. I would go back tomorrow if I thought it would help. I am not sure it would help."

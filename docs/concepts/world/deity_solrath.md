# Solrath — The Undying Light
*Deity reference — Paladin and Cleric patron, Good-aligned*

---

## Names and Titles

**Primary name:** Solrath *(SOLE-rath)*

**Titles used in temple liturgy:**
- The Undying Light
- The Sword Unsheathed
- He Who Does Not Flinch
- The Judge of the Living

**Names used by enemies and critics:**
- The Burden-Giver (Shadow Knight mockery — Solrath's obligations are heavy)
- The Inflexible (theological opponents — his law is not situational)
- The Eye That Judges (Necromancer tradition — Solrath sees the dead as unfinished business)

---

## Nature and Domain

Solrath is the god of ordered light — not warmth, not comfort, but the light that shows you what is there even when you do not want to see it. His domains are:

- **Justice** — not mercy but accountability; the weight of action matched to consequence
- **Light** — truth made visible; the removal of concealment; that which the darkness cannot survive
- **Order** — structured civilization, law, the compact between the powerful and the weak
- **Duty** — obligation that holds even when no one is watching; the thing you do because it must be done, not because it is easy

He is not a gentle god. His followers are sometimes confused about this. The common folk imagine the Paladin's deity as a god of warmth and protection — a shepherd god. Solrath is not a shepherd. He is the sword that kills the wolf. The shepherd is a metaphor he has tolerated for centuries without endorsing it.

---

## Theology

### The Compact
Solrath's central teaching: the strong owe a debt to the weak. This is not charity. It is the fundamental compact of ordered civilization — those who can bear arms, make law, and hold power are obligated by that power to those who cannot. A Paladin who protects only themselves has broken the compact and is no longer serving Solrath; they are serving their own comfort.

The compact has no exceptions for personal cost. The debt is not discharged when the obligation becomes dangerous. If anything, Solrath's theology holds that the obligation becomes *more* binding when it is dangerous — a Paladin who protects people only when it is safe is not fulfilling the compact; they are performing it.

### On Evil
Solrath does not recognize evil as a theological equal. He does not accept the framing that light and dark are equivalent forces in balance. His position: evil is the breaking of the compact. It is the strong taking from the weak, the powerful using power for self-gratification, the lie told to avoid accountability. It is not metaphysically equal to good. It is a failure.

This makes Solrath's theology somewhat uncomfortable in cosmological discussions. He is not interested in balance. He is interested in the compact being honored.

### On the Undead
Solrath considers undead an abomination in the specific sense that the word requires: they have broken the natural compact of life and death. Every soul has an obligation — to live, to account for that life in death, and to pass on. The undead are souls that have refused their obligation. Solrath's position is that this refusal is not a tragedy to be mourned — it is a compact violation to be resolved.

Paladins who follow Solrath do not find undead unsettling. They find them clarifying. An undead mob is a compact violation standing in front of you. The situation is not ambiguous.

### On Death
Solrath does not rule the dead. When a soul completes its life and accounting, it passes beyond his jurisdiction. He is a god of the living — of what the living owe each other. Vethis (the death deity) handles the rest, and the two have a complicated but functional working relationship that neither discusses publicly.

## The Priesthood

### Structure
Open to all genders; historically male-dominant in martial orders, mixed in administrative temple roles. The martial and priestly arms of the Solrath faith are distinct institutions that cooperate rather than merge.

Ranks (ascending, priestly):
1. **Aspirant** — Novice; under instruction; no independent ministry
2. **Candlebearer** — Initiated; can perform basic rites; serves a temple
3. **Lightkeeper** — Full clergy; administers sacraments; runs a district ministry
4. **Voice of the Compact** — Senior clergy; interprets doctrine; handles doctrinal disputes
5. **The High Voice** — One per major temple; doctrinal authority in their region

Ranks (ascending, martial — Paladins):
1. **Initiate** — Training; under a mentor
2. **Sworn** — Full Paladin; taken the compact oath; operates independently
3. **Sentinel** — Experienced; leads small groups; first to respond to reports of corruption
4. **Warden** — Senior Paladin; commands districts; handles named threats
5. **Blademaster** — Rare; title given for exceptional service; not a rank with assigned duties, but carries enormous authority

### The Compact Oath
All Paladins of Solrath take the compact oath at their swearing ceremony. The oath is the same everywhere, in every language, unchanged for four hundred years:

*"I take from no one who cannot give freely. I stand between those who cannot stand and those who would use them. I speak the truth when the lie would serve me better. I hold the compact until I am dead, and I do not count the cost before I decide."*

The oath is administered by a Voice of the Compact or higher. The Paladin kneels. At the moment of speaking the last word, Solrath marks the Paladin — a warmth in the chest that does not fade. Every Paladin of Solrath has felt this. None of them describe it the same way twice.

### Relationship to Clerics
Solrath's Clerics are the administrative and healing arm of the faith. They are not Paladins. They do not take the compact oath. Their relationship to the martial Paladins is collegial but distinct — Clerics tend the wounded, minister to communities, and perform the sacraments; Paladins handle the situations that end in someone getting hurt.

A Solrath Cleric who enters combat is either very brave or very cornered. Either is possible.

## Iconography and Temples

### Images
Solrath is depicted as a figure in full plate armor with no face visible — not darkness inside the helmet but light, too bright to look at directly. The figure holds a sword upright, point down. In some traditions the sword is planted in the ground; in others it is being actively held. The theology of which depiction is correct has been debated for two centuries.

He is never depicted sitting. Every image of Solrath shows him standing.

### Temples
Solrath temples are built on elevated ground when possible — hills, cliff edges, high floors in cities. The temple must receive direct sunlight at noon. A temple that is permanently in shadow is considered invalid by most interpretations.

Interior design favors high vaulted ceilings, large windows oriented to catch maximum light, and floors of pale stone. There are no hidden rooms in a Solrath temple. The doctrine is that every space in the temple should be immediately visible from the main hall — concealment is not compatible with the Light.

The main altar always faces east. The sword motif appears repeatedly — in carvings, in window tracery, in the ironwork of the main doors.

**Major Temples:** Greyveil (the largest, neutral city presence — the Solrath temple in Greyveil is deliberately prominent), Millhaven (smaller; serves the region), Aelindra (cooperative with Elf faith structures; some theological tension).

### Shrines
Roadside shrines are common in Good-aligned territory — a stone post with a sunburst carved at the top, maintained by passing travelers as a community obligation. No coin boxes. No offering slots. Solrath does not want money. He wants the road maintained.

## Relationship to Other Deities

**Erindra (nature):** Respectful but distinct. Solrath represents civilization; Erindra represents what civilization exists within. They are not in conflict. Their followers occasionally are.

**Aracheth (spider-goddess):** Theological opposition. Aracheth's intelligence-as-worship is incompatible with Solrath's truth-as-obligation. Solrath's position is that knowledge hoarded for power is a compact violation — you have something that could protect the weak and you are using it to protect your position. Aracheth's position (as expressed through her priesthood) is that Solrath's followers confuse the sword with the compact and mistake activity for justice.

**Vethis (death):** Complex functional cooperation. Solrath holds the living accountable; Vethis receives them when they die. They do not compete. Their theologies occasionally produce difficult edge cases (what is owed to a dead man? whose jurisdiction is a ghost?) that neither priesthood has fully resolved.

## Quest Hooks

- **The Compact Breaker** — A Paladin of Solrath has been found taking money from a merchant to look the other way. This is a compact violation of the clearest kind. The Warden who sent the Paladin originally needs someone to find out if the report is accurate and handle what they find.
- **The Invalid Temple** — A town built a new Solrath temple in the valley because the hill site was too expensive. The regional Voice of the Compact has declared it invalid. The town has no other spiritual resources. Someone has to either fix the temple or fix the politics.
- **The Light That Moved** — A Solrath shrine on the Greyveil road has been moved three inches to the north from its original position. No one admits to moving it. The stone post weighs four hundred pounds. The Candlebearer responsible for this section of road is frightened about what this means and will not say why until he trusts the players.
- **The Paladin Who Stopped** — A Warden-rank Paladin retired seven years ago and has been living in a small village since, doing nothing. The Blademaster wants to know why. The answer is personal, true, and will require someone to decide what the compact actually demands of a person who has given everything they had.

# Varek and the Residual — Mystery Document
*Varek's Spire, levels 25–35. The central unresolved question of the tower.*

---

## What Players Encounter

Varek's Spire is a seven-floor wizard tower built on a rock shelf above the Ashen Coast. Floor 1 has a literal golem at the desk. Floor 2 has a library with practice sections removed for everyone's safety. Floor 4 is locked. Floors 5 through 7 are Varek's private space — living quarters, experiment staging, study. The basement has no official basement.

The basement has three levels: storage (B1), old work (B2), and B3, which contains the Residual.

Varek is in B3. He has been there for ninety years. He is sitting in a chair facing the Residual. The chair faces a wall of notes — ninety years of notes covering all four walls of B3. Between the notes and Varek: the Residual.

Players who get to B3 will encounter all of this and will have the following questions:

1. What is the Residual?
2. Why is Varek sitting there?
3. What has he figured out in ninety years?
4. Is he okay?

---

## What the Residual Is

The Residual does not have a defined shape. It does not move unless you look away. It is present in a way that most things in the world are not — when you are aware of it, you are extremely aware of it; when you look away, the awareness dims but does not disappear. It is not alive in the standard sense. It is not dead. It predates the standard distinction.

The best current description, from Varek's notes: *"It is a remainder. Something was here and is now not here, and the Residual is what was left when that something departed. Like heat remaining after a fire has moved on. The fire itself is not present. But the heat has not dissipated. I do not know why it has not dissipated. I have been here for ninety years watching it not dissipate."*

The Residual is connected to the Architect sites. This is not a conclusion Varek has fully reached — it is a direction his notes are moving. The specific connection: the Architect notation on the walls of B3 (Varek wrote it during a period he describes in his notes as "the months when I was not entirely myself") includes the phrase that translates to "remainder protocol." The Architects built the Residual into their construction plans. It is not an accident. It is a feature.

**What the Residual does:**
- It is warm. Not hot — warm. Specifically warm at an exact temperature that has not varied by a measurable amount in ninety years.
- Objects placed within three feet of it experience a slight temporal distortion — not enough to cause mechanical effects, but enough that Varek's pocket watch (now in his study upstairs) stopped matching real time after six months of being brought down to B3.
- Living creatures who spend extended time in B3 experience a gradual erosion of certainty about what they know. Not memory loss — specifically: things they are certain about become less certain. Facts they know remain accessible, but the confidence attached to those facts decreases. Varek has been sitting in B3 for ninety years. He is less certain about his own name than he used to be.
- Animals will not enter B3. The golem on Floor 1 has received orders to descend to B3 and has not complied; it returns to its desk every time with no explanation.

## Varek's Ninety Years

Varek was forty-three when he built the Spire. He built it because he found the Residual first — in the rock of the cliff face, during survey work for the tower's foundation — and needed a controlled environment to study it.

He is now, functionally, one hundred and thirty-three years old. Wizards do not live that long without significant effort. Varek has not made any significant effort to extend his life. He is not a lich. He is not undead. He has been measured by three separate Clerics who confirmed he is alive.

The working theory (none of it confirmed): the Residual's temporal distortion applies to biological processes as well as mechanical ones. Varek's proximity to it has slowed something in him at a fundamental level. He ages, but at a reduced rate. He is not aware of this as a deliberate choice — he sits in B3 because he is studying the Residual, and the effect of the study has been to extend his life enough to keep studying.

He does not seem unhappy about this. He does seem uncertain about whether he is happy. He is uncertain about most things, which is what ninety years facing the Residual will do to a person.

**What he has figured out:**
- The Residual is a terminus point — something the Architects built to mark a location
- It is one of several; the other Architect sites have equivalent structures, less legible because less studied
- The Residual's warmth corresponds to an energy source he cannot identify; it is not magical in any tradition he recognizes; it predates the classification systems
- The Architect notation "remainder protocol" appears to be instructions for what the Residual should do when triggered; he cannot determine the trigger condition
- He wrote a section of his notes in Architect notation during "the months" and cannot fully decode what he wrote; he is certain it is important; he is not certain whether it is his own insight translated through the notation or something the Residual communicated through him

**What he has not figured out:**
- What the trigger condition is
- What "remainder protocol" does when triggered
- Whether discovering this would be good

## Interacting with Varek

Varek is not hostile. He is distracted in a specific way — present, responsive, clearly intelligent, but operating with a reduced certainty gradient. He will answer questions. His answers will be accurate but hedged in unusual ways: "I believe this is my name" rather than "my name is." "I think I built this tower" rather than "I built this tower."

He is aware this is unusual and will mention it if asked. He will say, accurately: "I have been down here for a long time. Certainty wears off."

### What he will discuss
- The Residual (his primary subject; he has a lot to say)
- Wizard theory and practice (he was and remains an excellent Wizard; this part of him is intact)
- The Architect sites (he knows about the others; he has visited two of them in his first thirty years)
- The tower's facilities (he will not explain why Floor 4 is locked; he will say it is for everyone's benefit and believe this)

### What he will not discuss
- Floor 4
- The months when he was not entirely himself
- Why the golem won't go to B3

### What he cannot discuss (does not have access to)
- Whether the Residual is dangerous to him
- What triggered his certainty erosion (it was gradual; he did not notice it happening)
- The content of the notes he wrote in Architect notation (he can see them but cannot fully decode them)

## Floor 4 — What Is There

Floor 4 is locked with a physical lock (key: Varek's study) and a ward that requires a passphrase Varek set ninety years ago and has since partially forgotten. The partial forgetting means the ward accepts either the correct passphrase or a plausible variant of it — the ward is, appropriately, uncertain.

Inside Floor 4: an experiment that Varek was running in Year 4 of the tower's existence that he has not ended because he is not certain whether ending it would be safe. The experiment involves a mirror that does not reflect correctly — it reflects the room from a perspective that does not exist. The perspective has been slowly shifting over eighty-six years in a direction that is consistently toward the rock face where the Residual is.

The mirror is not in the Architect notation. It is not connected to the Residual as far as Varek can determine. He keeps it locked away because in Year 4 he made an error in the experiment and what was on the other side of the mirror noticed him.

He does not know if it is still noticing.

## Resolution Paths

### Path 1 — Leave it alone
Players access B3, speak with Varek, learn what they can, leave. The Residual continues. Varek continues sitting. Nothing changes. This is the most likely outcome of a first visit.

### Path 2 — Trigger the remainder protocol
If players decode enough Architect notation, cross-reference with the six-fingered builders lore, and determine the trigger condition: the Residual activates. The warmth intensifies. The temporal distortion expands briefly to the whole tower. Every clock in the tower stops. When it ends, a passage opens in the B3 wall that was not there before. Where the passage leads is a server-scale decision — it connects to a deeper Architect site, one not on the known list, and what is there is not yet determined.

### Path 3 — Help Varek leave
Players can theoretically convince Varek to leave B3. He will resist — not defensively, but with genuine uncertainty about whether he should, whether he can, whether the Residual needs to be watched. If players succeed: Varek leaves B3 for the first time in a period he cannot specify. The Residual remains. When Varek reaches the surface, he stands in direct sunlight, says nothing for approximately ninety seconds, and then asks the players what year it is. His reaction to the answer is not what players expect.

### Path 4 — Kill Varek
He is a level 35 Wizard and will fight competently. What happens to the Residual when Varek dies has not been determined. The notes on all four walls of B3 are now the only record. The golem on Floor 1 continues its work. The tower does not collapse. The Residual remains warm.

## Cross-Zone Connections

- **The Architect notation in B3** written by Varek during "the months" matches notation at the Sunken Ziggurat's Still Throne level — same hand, but Varek has never been to the Ziggurat
- **The Residual's warmth** is the same precise temperature as the clay jar in Bess's cellar (the one with the tooth)
- **The mirror on Floor 4** — what is on the other side shares a visual characteristic with the Underbreach dungeon's deepest carved reliefs; this connection is findable only by players who have cleared both areas

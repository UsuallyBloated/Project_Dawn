# Aracheth — The Weaver of Fates
*Deity reference — Dark Elf patron goddess*

---

## Names and Titles

**Primary name:** Aracheth *(Ahr-AH-keth)*

**Titles used in temple liturgy:**
- The Weaver
- She Who Holds the Threads
- The Patient Dark
- The One Who Already Knows

**Names used by non-Dark Elves:**
- The Spider Goddess (reductive; Dark Elves find this mildly insulting)
- The Web-Mother (Witch Hunter classification; hostile framing)
- The Unseen Pattern (scholarly euphemism; technically more accurate)

Aracheth does not have a male-gendered title anywhere in the surviving record. The question of whether this reflects her nature or her priesthood's editorial choices has no satisfactory answer.

---

## Nature and Domain

Aracheth is the goddess of fate conceived as architecture. Not fate as a river you are swept along — fate as a web: a structure built through countless decisions, each connection to every other, the whole visible only from above. She does not ordain outcomes. She sees them. The difference, her priesthood argues, is significant. It is the difference between a goddess who puppets and a goddess who knows.

Her domains are:
- **Fate** — not destiny but the map of consequences; the web of what leads to what
- **Power** — specifically power held through knowledge of what others do not know you know
- **Patience** — her deepest virtue; acting at the right moment rather than the available moment
- **The Unseen** — secrets, intelligence, things deliberately hidden; she sees them

She is not associated with death, although her priesthood has a practical relationship with it. She is not associated with the spider as creature but as architect — the web is the thing, not the maker. This distinction matters in her theology and is ignored in her iconography, which depicts spiders constantly.

## Theology

### The Web
The central metaphor: all events are connected by threads. Pull one thread and others move. A god who can see all threads can see the consequences of any action before it happens — or rather, can see what the most probable consequence is, given the current shape of the web.

Aracheth does not make the web. She reads it. Her power comes from reading it better than anyone else, faster than anyone else, and acting before anyone else knows the reading has occurred.

The theological implication: knowledge is power is worship. A Dark Elf who learns what others do not know, who uses that knowledge to gain and hold position, is living the goddess's will. The most faithful Dark Elf is not the most pious — it is the most informed.

### Fate and Choice
The priesthood's official position on free will: the web exists, and you have just seen your position in it. Whether you pull the thread or don't is your choice. Aracheth does not force outcomes. She does not need to — she has already seen what you are going to do, and she arranged the web accordingly long before you arrived at this junction.

Critics of the theology — including several destroyed philosophical schools — point out that this position is circular and unfalsifiable. The priesthood agrees that this is an accurate description and moves on.

### On Death and the Afterlife
Dark Elves who have served Aracheth are said to enter the web permanently on death — become part of the structure rather than ending. This is not described as comfortable. It is described as an honor. A thread that holds a particularly significant position in the web is not cut when the weaver is done with it — it becomes permanent architecture.

What happens to Dark Elves who have not served Aracheth is a subject the priesthood is carefully vague about.

## The Priesthood

### Structure
All-female. This is not policy — it is the goddess's original mandate and has not been questioned since the mandate was given, some four thousand years ago. Male Dark Elves serve as temple guards, archivists, and in specific ritual roles, but no male holds clerical rank.

Ranks (ascending):
1. **Thread-Keeper** — novices; maintain the temple, handle correspondence, learn the cipher
2. **Web-Reader** — initiated; can perform standard rites; have access to the first intelligence archive
3. **Strand-Walker** — senior clergy; conduct the transformative rites; run field intelligence networks
4. **Thread-Cutter** — high clergy; rarely seen; control the assassination index
5. **The Loom** — the High Priestess; there is one; she has held the position for sixty-two years; she is forty-eight

### Function in Dark Elf Society
The temple is, functionally, Vel'Sharath's intelligence service. The Houses provide the political structure; the temple provides the information infrastructure that all the Houses depend on. This creates a situation where even the most powerful House Matriarchs are cautious in their dealings with the temple — the priesthood knows things, and what it knows, it has written down.

The temple does not formally participate in House politics. It is above House politics, because it knows enough about each House to end any of them. This position is not stated publicly. It does not need to be.

### Intelligence Networks
The priesthood maintains informants in every major city in the known world, including surface cities where Dark Elves are actively unwelcome. These informants are not all Dark Elves — the temple recruits across race lines for surface work, paying in information rather than coin (the information is worth more).

The intelligence network is managed through a cipher that changes on a regular schedule — the schedule is itself encoded in a cipher the Thread-Keepers spend their first year learning. The cipher system was designed by Aracheth's high priestess four hundred years ago, and it has not been broken. Several Witch Hunter organizations have tried.

## Iconography and Temples

### Images
Aracheth is depicted as a Dark Elf female of indeterminate age — always seated, always at the center of a web that extends beyond the frame of the image. Her expression is not cruel, not kind, not neutral. The word the temples use is *patient*. She is waiting for you to do the thing you are going to do.

The web in her imagery is always complete — no gaps, no broken threads — and is always depicted as if she made it recently, the silk still slightly reflective. Whether the web represents fate or her specific current web of knowledge is intentionally ambiguous.

Spider imagery covers all temple surfaces. Stylized, not naturalistic — the spiders in Dark Elf temple art are architectural elements, the web-pattern used as a structural motif the way other cultures use geometric tile.

### The Main Temple (Vel'Sharath)
The Temple of the Loom occupies the highest and deepest point of Vel'Sharath simultaneously — the central spire and the lowest vault are connected by a shaft that the priesthood calls the Thread and outsiders are not permitted to see. The main ritual space is ringed by pillars carved in the shapes of spider legs.

The archives are deeper than the main vault. The architecture becomes non-standard past the third archive level. The oldest records are stored in a room that the current High Priestess has only entered three times.

### Shrines
Small Aracheth shrines exist throughout Dark Elf society — doorways, market corners, the entrance to the Houses' private quarters. The standard shrine is a web-form carved in obsidian, always with a small indentation where an offering can be placed. The offering is always information, written and burned. The smoke is the offering.

## Relationship to Other Deities

The theological landscape of Project Dawn is not fully codified, but Aracheth has the following characterized relationships:

**Neutral / acknowledged:** The major neutral trade deities; Aracheth has no objection to commerce and does not compete in that domain.

**Adversarial:** Nature deities. The web is fundamentally opposed to the wild — the web is structure, pattern, designed; nature is the disruption of pattern. Druid traditions and Aracheth's theology are philosophically incompatible.

**Complex:** Death deities. The web-permanence afterlife theology puts Aracheth in potential conflict with any deity claiming authority over the dead. The current position is that Aracheth's claim is limited to those who served her — the rest of the dead are not her concern or her problem.

---

## The Dark Spider-Blessed

Aracheth's most direct interaction with the mortal world is the marks she places on favored Dark Elves — the Dark Spider-Blessed transformation. The mark is not given by the temple; the temple recommends and the temple can facilitate the conditions, but the mark is Aracheth's decision. There have been cases where the temple's recommended candidate did not receive the mark and an unrecommended one did.

This is the primary point of theological anxiety in the Dark Elf priesthood and the one topic the High Priestess does not discuss.

## Quest Hooks

- **The Third Archive** — A Strand-Walker needs something retrieved from the third archive level. She does not explain what is in the second and a half archive level, between the third and the rest. She will not go herself.
- **The Informant Network** — A surface-city informant has gone silent. The temple needs to know whether they are dead, compromised, or simply delayed. The answer determines what the temple does next.
- **The Unmarked Candidate** — A Dark Elf who has served the temple for thirty years has not received the goddess's mark. They have received everything else — rank, authority, the respect of the priesthood. They want to know why. The answer, if they find it, will not make them feel better.
- **What the High Priestess Burned** — Someone is looking for a record that the High Priestess burned twelve years ago. They believe a copy exists. They are probably right. The record concerns the goddess's first direct communication in two hundred years and what she said.

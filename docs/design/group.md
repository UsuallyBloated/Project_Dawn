# Group Design
*Five people who understand their jobs, working as one.*

## Overview

Groups in Project Dawn are the game's central social structure. Most high-value content — named enemies, dungeon clears, high-tier loot — is designed around a full group of six players. Solo play is viable for the right classes, but group play is where the game's depth lives. The class design specifically rewards players who understand what their role asks of the group, and punishes those who don't.

The EQ DNA is deliberate here: a group is not six players who happen to be together. It is a formation. Everyone has a function. Failing your function costs the group.

---

## Group Size and Composition

**Standard group size: 6 players.**

The core triangle is tank / healer / DPS. The remaining three slots are support, additional DPS, or second healer depending on content difficulty.

**Minimum functional group (3–4 players):** A tank, a healer, and 1–2 DPS is survivable in content one tier below the group's average level. Anything harder requires full roles covered.

## Roles

### Tank
**Primary function:** Hold aggro. Keep the enemy's attacks directed at the tank rather than the healer or DPS.

**Who does it:** Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight.

- The Warrior has the highest HP and defensive skills but no mana-based threat generation — pure skill-driven aggro.
- The Paladin has holy spells that generate threat and some self-healing; plays defensively correct but not maximum damage.
- The Shadow Knight holds aggro via lifetap threat and has self-sustain, but their Evil alignment creates faction complications in Good-aligned zones.

**Tank's highest-value skill:** Positioning. A tank who keeps the mob facing away from the group prevents cleave damage and allows Rogues and melee DPS to attack from behind safely.

### Healer
**Primary function:** Keep the tank alive. Keep the group alive as secondary priority.

**Who does it:** Cleric (primary), Druid (secondary), Shaman (secondary), Paladin (self-only), Blood Mage (at cost).

- The Cleric is the game's best dedicated healer — Complete Heal is the highest single-target restoration in the game.
- The Druid heals via HoT (Regrowth, Nature's Renewal) rather than burst — better for sustained fights, weaker for sudden spike damage.
- The Shaman's Torpor is the strongest HoT in the game; their Slow debuff reduces how fast the healer needs to work.

**Healer's highest-value behavior:** Slow downtime. A healer who manages their mana over a long pull is more valuable than a healer who front-loads and runs dry. The sit-for-mana mechanic means intelligent triage matters.

### DPS
**Primary function:** Kill the enemy before the healer runs out of mana.

**Who does it:** Wizard, Sorcerer, Magician, Necromancer, Rogue, Ranger, Witch Hunter, Bard (partial), Blood Mage (partial), Monk, Beast Master.

DPS roles split into two sub-categories:

| Sub-role | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Burst DPS | Wizard, Rogue, Monk | Highest per-second damage in a narrow window |
| Sustained DPS | Sorcerer, Necromancer (DoT), Ranger | Consistent output over the full fight |
| Utility DPS | Magician (pet), Witch Hunter, Bard | Lower pure damage; brings tools that improve group performance |

The Wizard is the burst DPS ceiling — a fully memorized Wizard opening with Meteor is the highest single-hit output in the game. A Sorcerer delivers lower burst but never needs to re-memorize. These two often coexist: the Wizard handles the spike; the Sorcerer handles consistency.

### Support
**Primary function:** Amplify the group's output or reduce the threat to the group.

**Who does it:** Enchanter (primary), Bard (primary), Shaman (debuffs), Druid (Spirit of Wolf for movement), Witch Hunter (debuffs).

- The **Enchanter** is the defining support class. Clarity gives casters unlimited mana extension. Haste doubles melee DPS output. A skilled Enchanter managing Clarity on every caster and Haste on every melee while maintaining Charm on a camp add is performing at a level no other support class matches.
- The **Bard** with a full Twist running provides haste, mana regen, movement speed, and attack debuffs simultaneously. At high Twist skill, the Bard adds as much total value as a second healer in a well-coordinated group.
- The **Shaman's** Slow is the single highest-impact debuff in the game. A 40% attack speed reduction on a named mob cuts the incoming damage the healer needs to handle almost in half. Shaman Slow is why some groups specifically look for a Shaman over a second DPS.

## The Aggro/Threat System
*Design target — partially implemented.*

The tank's job is to be the highest-threat target at all times. Threat is generated by:
- Dealing damage to the enemy
- Healing targets that have threat
- Using skills and spells with inherent threat modifiers (taunt skills generate pure threat without damage)

**Threat management for non-tanks:**
- DPS who pull aggro off the tank die quickly
- Healers generate secondary threat from heals; they become a target if the tank loses control
- The Enchanter holding a charmed add generates zero threat from the charm — but the charmed mob's attacks on enemies generate threat on the Enchanter, which can break the charm

**Taunt skills (Warrior primary):** Instantly force the enemy to target the taunting character. Limited cooldown. Used reactively when aggro breaks, not as a spam tool.

## Group Dynamics — What Makes a Good Group

**Six good classes in the wrong composition lose to five classes that cover the roles.**

A group stacked with Wizards and no tank or healer will die in the first pull against appropriate content. The EQ model is intentional here: role coverage is more important than raw power.

**Ideal six-person group:**
- 1 Tank (Warrior, Paladin, or Shadow Knight)
- 1 Primary Healer (Cleric or Shaman)
- 1 Support/Utility (Enchanter or Bard)
- 3 DPS in appropriate mix (burst + sustained, or utility DPS + 2 pure)

**Undersized/underrole groups:** Viable for lower-difficulty content. Paladins and Shadow Knights can tank-heal hybrid in small groups. Rangers and Rogues can self-sustain with careful play. The Magician's earth elemental can absorb hits when no tank is present. These are concessions, not optimizations.

## Group Communication and Awareness

**Targeting conventions:**
- The tank should be clearly marked as main tank (MT) so healers prioritize
- Assist targeting (attacking whatever the designated player is targeting) prevents mob splitting
- The Enchanter's charmed adds must not be accidentally attacked — a single stray DoT or AoE breaks charm and converts the controlled mob into a group threat

**Pull discipline:**
- The tank or a designated puller brings one mob to the group's camp
- AoE spells are suppressed until single targets are established
- The Wizard waits for the tank to establish aggro before opening with Meteor
- The Shaman Slows before the healer needs to work hard

**Recovery when things go wrong:**
- Enchanter mez: any enemy that breaks out of a controlled state can be Mesmerized to reset the encounter
- Bard charm: a charmed mob fighting another mob gives the group breathing room to recover
- Feign Death (Monk): the Monk can drop out of combat and let aggro reset
- Gate (Wizard): individual escape when the pull goes catastrophically wrong
- Corpse run: if the group wipes, retrieving gear from the camp before respawn

## Solo Viability Reference

Not everyone wants to group. Some classes manage solo better than others:

| Class | Solo Viability | Primary Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Magician | Good | Earth elemental as tank + self-heal |
| Sorcerer | Good | Consistent damage, Mana Feed fallback |
| Necromancer | Good | Skeleton pet + DoT kite + self-heal |
| Ranger | Good | Range control + root/snare + self-heal |
| Bard | Good | Charm turns 1v1 into 2v1 |
| Beast Master | Good | Warder pet + self-heal |
| Monk | Good | Feign Death escape + high DPS |
| Rogue | Moderate | High DPS but no healing; requires careful pulls |
| Warrior | Moderate | Durable but slow kills without healer |
| Enchanter | Moderate | Charm is powerful but risky; no self-heal |
| Cleric | Moderate | Hard to die; hard to kill things quickly |
| Paladin | Moderate | Decent self-sustain; slower than pure DPS |
| Wizard | Conditional | Powerful when prepared; vulnerable when dry |
| Shadow Knight | Conditional | Strong Evil content; faction issues in Good zones |

## Group XP

*Design target.*

Group XP is distributed across all members, with a bonus multiplier for full groups (6 players). The bonus exists because a coordinated full group kills faster and with less downtime than six solo players — the XP reflects this.

Groups slightly below the content's level range gain more XP per kill. Groups significantly above the content's level range gain less. This creates natural progression pressure toward harder content.

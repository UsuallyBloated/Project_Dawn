The starting area at 4 km: composition sketches

A proposal, drawn by code. Nothing in the game has changed. Every named place comes from the camp file, the hills file and the NPC dialogue; the sizes, the roads and the items marked new are first proposals for you to accept or change.

4 km is the largest size our own research (zone_size_limits.md) calls "build freely": full float precision in Godot, and the server's cost follows mob count, not area. Up to 8 km is possible with a rendering pass, but a starter zone that takes half an hour to cross would strand new players.

Sheet 1. The zone map

North is up (+Z), east is right (+X). The dashed square in the middle is the whole world as it is built today. Dashed rings are running time from the town gate.

Sheet 2. Elevation profiles

Heights drawn at twice true scale. A fall of 42 m is lethal under the fall rule, so the Wraith Gate cliff (60 m) and the ridge faces (70 m) kill; the barrow's walls stay walkable slopes so its dead can climb out after you, as they do today.

Sheet 3. Scene blockouts

Flat shapes for composition and palette only. Muted earth colours with one saturated accent per scene, late afternoon light.

What this would cost to build, and what is still yours to decide

  • More mobs, or it is empty. Today's 54 spawn points spread over 16 square km would be a walk through nothing. Each camp wants several times its spawns, with roaming wildlife between camps. Mob count is the server's real cost, so this is the number to budget.
  • Terrain in chunks. The ground is one mesh and one collider today. At 4 km it must be split into chunks, and the server's ground file grows with it. The zone-size research already names this.
  • Draw distance. Fog, shadow distance and prop visibility ranges need setting on purpose, so the ridge and the hills read from the town.
  • New on these sheets: the roads and where they leave the zone (east to the capital, north into the Greywood), the farms west of the walls, the mill as a windmill, the wall around the town, and every height. The dialogue implies most of them; none has been decided.
  • Not drawn: water. A river or a lake would give the zone a second landmark, but no document mentions one, so it is your call.

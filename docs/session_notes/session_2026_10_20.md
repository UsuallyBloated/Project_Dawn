## 3. Armor mitigation: candlesan's "The Simple Math Hiding in Your Favorite Games" (notes, no code)

The user shared the short (candlesan, 3:00, https://www.youtube.com/shorts/IzHAjV8YEW4) and asked
for notes. Transcribed locally with Whisper; the formula itself appears only on screen. It was
first inferred from the narration's numbers (which fit `armor / (armor + 100)` exactly), then
confirmed by the user from the video: `% = x / (x + k)`. The video shows it without the
parentheses (user: irresponsible), which reads as `1 + k`; the parenthesised form is the real one.

### What the video argues

- **Flat armor (subtract N per hit, StarCraft's way) breaks with attack speed.** Two monsters at
  the same damage per second: one hits hard and slowly, one weakly and fast. Flat armor barely
  touches the slow one and zeroes the fast one. Diablo 3 started with flat armor and hit exactly
  this. Poison over time, ground fire and thorns would each need special rules.
- **Naive percentage armor (1 point = 1%) gets more valuable the more you have.** 80% to 90% halves
  the damage that gets through, so such games end up needing a hard cap.
- **The answer used by Diablo 2's defense, WoW armor, League, Dota 2 and Warframe:** a reduction of
  `x / (x + k)`. It is usually called diminishing returns, and the video says that is the wrong way
  to read it: measure hits survived, not percent mitigated. With 1000 life against 100-damage
  hits, every 100 armor buys exactly 10 more hits (10, 20, 30, ...). Survivability is directly
  proportional to armor, and no cap is needed.

### How we compare (read against the server, 2026-10-20)

1. **We already use this formula, with k = 100.** `combat::mitigated` (`combat.rs:99`) takes
   `armor / (armor + 100)` off a melee hit, floored at 1. That is the client's
   `Combat.receive_player_damage` formula, kept in step. Hits survived =
   `(life / hit) x (1 + armor / 100)`, a handy tuning view.
2. **Our k is fixed; some games scale it with the attacker's level.** Classic WoW is my
   recollection here, not re-checked. A fixed k means a given armor total gives the same percentage
   against a level 1 rat and a level 50 named, so the armor growth across levels decides how
   mitigation climbs. Today's numbers are small (the biggest armor on one item in `items.toml` is
   18), so this is a balance-pass question, not a bug.
3. **Only players are mitigated.** Both call sites are a hit landing on a player (the PvP swing,
   `tick.rs:3538`; a mob's swing, `:8207`). Mobs carry no armor, so a player's swing on a mob is
   never reduced.
4. **Spells, DoTs and damage shields bypass armor entirely.** That is the EQ split (armor for
   melee, resists for magic), not the video's one formula for everything, and it is consistent with
   our DoTs all being magical (Dark Decay, Entangle). The resist half does not exist yet (the proc
   follow-ups' "no enemy-resist model"). If a physical DoT (a bleed) is ever added, decide whether
   armor touches it.
5. **Truncation favours the defender on small hits.** `as i32` drops the fraction, so a 2-damage
   hit against 50 armor (1.33) lands as 1: a 50% cut where the formula says 33%. This is the
   flat-armor problem the video describes, in miniature, for fast weak hitters. Rounding (or
   carrying the fraction) would fix it if it ever matters.
6. **Exploit lens: clean.** Armor is summed server-side from the equipped items
   (`inventory.rs:157`/`:186`), never a client claim, and clamped at 0, so a negative-armor item
   cannot push the denominator to zero or turn armor into extra damage.

Nothing decided or built. EQ's own AC model works differently (not researched in this sitting);
if the balance pass wants authenticity over this formula, that is the comparison to make.

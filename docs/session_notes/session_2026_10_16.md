# Session 2026-10-16

## 1. Art pipeline: Meshy tested twice, the user's own Gnome mesh unwrapped

All art work; no game code changed. Meshy spend today: 20 credits (balance 3,463; the 10-15
Gnome model cost 30).

**Texturing options researched (free reading of Meshy's docs).** Retexture takes an uploaded
model (GLB up to 100 MB as a data URI), styled by text, one image, or up to four views (that
last needs `meshy-7`, "rolled out progressively"); 10 credits at 2K/4K, 15 at 8K; an option
keeps the model's own UVs. The docs never state the input geometry stays untouched. **The user
chose against letting Meshy texture the model: "I would much prefer we use a UV unwrapping."**

**The blockouts have no real unwrap.** All four exports carry one UV set, but the Gnome's is the
box-modelling leftover: 824 islands for 833 quads, overlapping 4.38 times over (a clean unwrap
sums to at most 1). *(Corrected 2026-10-19: the 824 came from Blender splitting the flat-shaded
GLB on import. Read from the file, the Gnome has 17 pieces, one per body part, each spread over
the whole square and stacked about 11 deep. The overlap and the conclusion stand.)*

**Meshy test 2: rebuild the user's silhouette (user's idea, 20 credits approved).** Four views
(front, side, back, three-quarter, 1024 px) rendered read-only from the user's `gnome.glb` in
background Blender into `concept_art/gnome/male_blockout/`; mesh only, 1,000 quads requested.
Task `01a145fc-a1db-7723-a68e-a48620919178`, cost exactly 20, saved under
`meshy_downloads/gnome_male_blockout/` with same-angle renders beside Meshy's previews.
Result: 2,047 triangles (GLB) / 1,013 quads and 21 triangles (FBX: glTF cannot store quads, so
the GLB is always triangulated); it kept the silhouette and connected the joints, and lost the
face (no nose or mouth), the chest detail and the hand shape. Meshy spreads its polygons evenly,
so at the budget the face gets as few as the shin, where the user's mesh spends them on purpose
(1,666 triangles with more detail). **The user's verdict: "these models are busted as fuck and
the topology is garbage... Not impressed with Meshy."** Both Gnome cards on the Art Board carry
the comparison and the verdict; `gnome/male_blockout` is a new card (pack marked approved with
a note that it was approved in chat with the spend).

**`CLAUDEgnome` unwrapped (the user's copy of their Gnome, in its own collection in
`gnome.blend`).** `gnome_backup_2026_10_16.blend` was taken first; the object has 17 loose parts
(the body is built from separate pieces with small gaps at the joints). Each part was split
along its sides and unwrapped; six variants were measured on texture-density evenness and the
two best saved as UV maps on the object: `UV_front_back` (34 islands, density 0.59 to 1.34 of
average for the middle 90% of faces, active, seams match it) and `UV_four_sides` (65 islands,
0.93 to 1.08). Both use Blender 5's minimum-stretch unwrap, no overlap and nothing outside the
square, but only half the square is filled; some islands are mirrored and the head gets no extra
density. A fingerprint of every object against the backup showed only `CLAUDEgnome` changed
(115 others identical). Checker renders of both layouts are in
`Project_Dawn_Source/reference/uv_checker/gnome/`. The user liked that Meshy's model connects
the joints; the user's own mesh would need its pieces joined (edge loops bridged at the elbows,
knees, neck and so on) before a final unwrap.

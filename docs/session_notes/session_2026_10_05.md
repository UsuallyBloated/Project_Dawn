## 1. The client-only spell backlog, recounted

The To-Do has carried "~32 spells missing from `spells.toml`" since 2026-07-22. A script
comparing the two files gave the exact picture: the client has 156 spells, the server had
124, and the 32 missing split three ways.

| Group | Count | Why it was missing |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary class nukes | 13 | Never ported |
| Ports | 10 | Gate, Succor, Evacuate plus seven zone ports |
| Blocked on other systems | 9 | Seven prestige-class spells, Camouflage (stealth), Warder's Mend (pet heal) |

Of the 13, nine are plain direct damage with nothing the server's ENEMY arm does not already
do. The other four are not:

- **Dark Decay and Entangle are damage over time**, and the server has no DoT model at all
  (no field in the spell struct, no ticking effect on an enemy). They are also the only two
  DoT spells in the game, and most of their damage is the DoT (168 of 188, and 90 of 100), so
  porting the direct hit alone would ship a spell that does a tenth of what its tooltip says.
- **Exsanguinate** is a mana drain; no server field.
- **Torpor** slows the target's attacks and heals the caster over time. *(This line first
  said the data looked wrong, "an ENEMY spell that heals". That was my misreading, corrected
  the same day; see section 5.)* The server's ENEMY arm applies the slow but has no step that
  gives the caster the HoT. Left alone pending the user.

Checked on the way: the 124 spells both sides already shared show **zero drift** on damage,
mana cost, cast time, cooldown and level. The lockstep has held.

## 3. A lockstep check that stays

The recount and the port each took a throwaway script. `tools/check_spell_lockstep.gd` makes
it one command:

```
godot --headless --path . -s tools/check_spell_lockstep.gd
```

It loads the client's real `SpellDefinitions.ALL` (no regex over the source), parses the
server's `spells.toml`, and compares every field the server carries. It prints DRIFT,
SERVER-ONLY and CLIENT-ONLY and exits 1 on the first two. Read-only, unlike the stale
`export_spells.gd`.

Its first run corrected something written an hour earlier in this note: the hand check had
compared five fields and found nothing, and the full comparison found one difference,
**Poet's Mending** (heal 8 on the client, 0 on the server). That one is deliberate and
documented in `spells.toml`: Bard songs keep 0 damage and 0 heal on the server because their
pulses are still applied client-side (the Bard song rework To-Do). The check skips those two
fields for anything the client marks as a song, so the result today is DRIFT 0 across all 133 shared spells, on every
field the server has, not just five.

Proved on a doctored copy: Fireball's damage changed by one and an extra spell appended, and
the check reported both and exited 1.

## 5. A wrong claim about Torpor, corrected

I told the user Torpor's data "looks wrong": an ENEMY-target spell carrying a heal-over-time,
which I read as healing the enemy and guessed was the Shaman's ally heal with the wrong
target type. The user answered on that premise: "Remove the heal, and keep the slowing
effect."

Before changing anything I read the spell's own description and the class design, and the
premise was false. `docs/concepts/classes/shaman.md` lists a five-rank Torpor line, every rank
"Target: slow N% attack speed; self HoT", marked as the class signature. The spell's
description says the same ("The spiritual resonance mends your wounds over time"), and
`spells.gd` applies `hot_hps` to the local player whatever the target type. The data is as
designed.

Nothing was changed. Removing the heal would leave Torpor (level 20, 80 mana, 2.5 s cast,
60 s cooldown, 70% for 24 s) strictly worse than Slow, which the Shaman already has at level
8 (35 mana, 1 s cast, 30 s cooldown, 70% for 30 s). The question went back to the user with
the facts straight. The To-Do entry and the earlier line in this note are corrected in place.

The lesson is the standing one: read the description and the design doc before calling data
wrong. A field name is not the design.

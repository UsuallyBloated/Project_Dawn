# Session 2026-10-15

## 1. Resurrection sickness rebuilt to the P99 design

The morning's question was whether EverQuest had res sickness at all; it did, as Resurrection
Effects, and the user chose it whole: "Lets keep the design you found on the P99 wiki page.
Difficult is what we are looking for." Server `528ae23`, client `9c5e333`, `res_sickness_checklist.md`
rewritten (13 rows). Yesterday's build took one point per four levels off each primary and
halved regen, sized on a wrong claim about our stat scale that the user caught the same
evening (the real scale: base 10, race and class, then a class's main stats gain 1 to 4 a level
for life while the rest never move).

What exists now: on accept the player lands at a quarter of max HP with no mana (one-shot,
announced at once with its own HealthUpdate and ManaUpdate; not damage, so no stand or camp
cancel rides on it), and STR, AGI, INT, WIS and CON lose a level-scaled fraction of their
INTRINSIC value (race, class and level via `char_data::compute`, never gear or buffs), a tenth at
level 1 to a half at 60, which is P99's shape: about half a level 60's STR. Five minutes and
EQ's two lines as before. The regen halving was never P99's and is gone with its test.

Intrinsic is what makes the client honest: the snapshot carries names and durations only, so
the sheet can show the cut only if the client computes the same number. `PlayerStats` now has
`intrinsic_character(race, class, level)`, the race-class-level math `apply_character` always
ran, callable on its own (`apply_character` starts from it, unchanged in effect), and
`BuffManager`'s reconcile applies the server's formula to it. The fraction is f64 in the same
order of operations on both sides (GDScript's float is f64), so the two round the same product;
a one-point disagreement on the sheet would otherwise have been possible at half-point
products. Kept in lockstep by hand, like the level tables.

Tests: unit (the fraction at 1, 20, 60 and past 60; the cut on sample stats and on a level 60
Human Warrior, half off STR 142 and nothing below zero); the resurrection integration test now
also checks the landing (a HealthUpdate at a quarter of max, a ManaUpdate at zero; its first
draft read the last update in the window and regen had ticked one HP, so it looks for the
landing values instead); a headless probe for the client half (a Human Warrior at 1, 20 and 60:
2, 14 and 70 off STR, WIS never below zero). Full suite green (276 unit, 98 integration).

Unchanged by the 2026-06-22 lock: the XP refund tiers (25/50/75%). Flagged: buffs are not
persisted, so a relog clears the sickness, as camping did in classic; a knob if it should
persist.

## 3. Art pipeline: the first two base-body packs, the low-poly budget, one facing rule

All free work: no Meshy credits spent. Client only; nothing crosses the wire.

**Facing, one rule for every model.** The user re-exported Human, Halfling, Ogre and Gnome as one
whole mesh each (mirror applied), facing Blender's Front view. glTF puts that face toward +Z and
characters here walk toward -Z, so `race_model.gd` gained `MODEL_FACING_YAW = PI`, applied to
every model; no model is rotated in Blender. The new `gnome.glb` is in `assets/`; a headless
probe mounted it as the game does and found the nose and toes pointing the walking direction.
All four exports measure inside their race's height range and are 1,442 to 1,868 triangles.

**Low poly, detail in the textures** (user: "lets be sure not to go nuts with polygons"). The
budget is **2,000 triangles for a bare body**, equipment extra; models are four-sided polygons,
so Meshy is asked for 1,000. `meshy_client.py` enforces it (`TRIANGLE_BUDGET`, `POLY_BUDGET`,
remesh always on, quad topology, anything above refused). The change was shown to the user; the
spend hook rightly refused a shell compile check of the client, so it is verified by reading and
by the tools loading after the next restart. `measure_glb.py` now counts triangles against the
budget (PASS / OVER).

**The base body is a blank mannequin** (user, in three steps): plain grey form-fitting
undergarments, barefoot, bald, beardless, no accent colour, a neutral face, "pretty void of
character", because players will change their appearance in game at any time. Recorded in the
style bible, the pipeline doc and a new To-Do entry, which also holds the user's two slider
ideas (build, wiry to stout; height within the race's range) and asks for a research session.

**Style A chosen** by the side-by-side test (painted concept sheet; B's ink outlines would likely
bake into Meshy's texture; C broke the bare-body rule). The Gnome's head is about 1/4 of his
height in the user's model, so `creature_heights.md` follows the model, and Gnomes read wiry.

**Two packs reviewed and passing** (`concept_art/gnome/male/`, `concept_art/human/male/`, four
views each, reviews on the Art Board cards). What made them work, now in the style bible:
"{view} view only, single figure only" at the start of the prompt (the original test's wording);
a text-only generation in a fresh session once edits started drifting; the Gnome's front
attached as the style reference for the next race, which removed the Human's ink outlines;
"clean silhouette defined by shading, not lines" in place of "crisp silhouette"; "no floor line";
"head and body turned together" for the three-quarter view.

Next: the user approves the Gnome pack on the board, VS Code restarts so the client change
loads, then the first paid step (the Gnome's multi-image-to-3D) waits on the user's approval
with the live balance and price shown.

**Later the same day: the first Meshy model.** The user approved the pack and the 30 credits;

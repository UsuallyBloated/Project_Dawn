you being the others). Built on `feat/jump-relay` in both repos, branched from the trade tips
(server `a7f5fef`, client `ac567bf`) so the one new message rides the same `PD_W0028` as the

What exists: `player.gd` latches a jump and sends it on the next Move's `jumping` flag; the
Move handler reads the flag through `PerConnection::note_move_jump` (rising edge only, at most
once per `JUMP_RELAY_MIN_INTERVAL` of 500 ms, a relayed jump stands the player, nothing reads it
into position); `ServerWorldMsg::PlayerJumped {{ id }}` appended after `HungerUpdate` and fanned to
the in-world players who can see the jumper, never the jumper; the DLL's `player_jumped` signal,
connected by name in `Net`; `RemotePlayer.play_jump` draws the local jump arc (the same 7 m/s and
20 m/s² as `player.gd`, 1.2 m, 0.7 s) as an offset on the visual and nameplate, never on the
interpolated position, and a corpse does not hop. Exploit lens: cosmetic by construction (the
server's position integration never sees the flag), and the rate limit bounds what a forged
stream can make peers' screens draw.

Tests: a unit test on the edge-and-rate rule; an integration test with a watcher beside a jumper
(one jump plus a forged burst of ten flags inside the interval reaches the watcher as one cue and
the jumper as none, an edge past the interval relays once more, the jumper's own Position keeps
y = 0); a headless probe of the hop. Two of the test's first drafts were wrong in the obvious ways
(a flag never cleared between edges; a burst sent after the interval had passed) and were fixed
as tests. `remote_jump_checklist.md`, 9 rows, on the branch.
